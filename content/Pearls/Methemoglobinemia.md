Methemoglobinemia causes tissue hypoxia due to accumulation of methemoglobin in the blood (haemoglobin that contains ferric form of iron $Fe^{3+}$ rather than the normal $Fe^{2+}$)

Methemoglobin is the oxidized form of Hb, which has an affinity for cyanide. Oxygen does not bind as easily to the heme subunit with the ferric form of iron. 

The remaining heme sites has increased affinity for oxygen resulting in decreased ability to release oxygen to tissues.

**Left-shift** of the oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve
- tissue hypoxia
- functional anemia


Risk factors:
- drugs
	- nitrates        
	- anesthetics
	- dapsone        
	- lidocaine, benzocaine, and bupivacaine
	- nitroglycerin
- infants are more susceptible to hemoglobin oxidation

### Management

- 100% oxygen + methylene blue - reduces haemoglobin to non-oxydised form


Contraindicated in [[Full/Pregnancy|Pregnancy]] (teratogen), [[Pearls/G6PD deficiency|G6PD deficiency]] (haemolytic anaemia), patients taking an SSRI (precipitate serotonin syndrome)
