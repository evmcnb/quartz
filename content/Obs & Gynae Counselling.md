## [[Pearls/Gestational Diabetes|Gestational Diabetes]]

- Macrosomia $\rightarrow$ risk of [[Pearls/Shoulder dystocia|Shoulder dystocia]]
- [[Pearls/Neonatal hypoglycaemia|Neonatal hypoglycaemia]]
- Low threshold for oral glucose tolerance test at 24-28 weeks gestation

Thresholds for diagnosis: 
- Fasting glucose $\geq$ 5.6 mmol/L
- 2-hour glucose $\geq$ 7.8 mmol/L

#### New onset

- If the fasting glucose is $\lt$ 7 mmol/L a trial of diet and exercise should be offered.
	- If the glucose targets are not met within 1-2 weeks, metformin should be started
	- In targets are still not met, **insulin should be added** - short acting
- If the fasting glucose is > 7 mmol/L insulin should be started. 

#### Pre-existing

- **Stop oral hypoglycaemic agents apart from metformin and commence insulin** 
- Folic acid 5mg/day from pre-conception to 12 weeks gestation
- Detailed anomaly scan at 20 weeks including 4 chamber view of heart and outflow tracts.

## Reduced fetal movements

- Explaining why the patient did the right thing by coming in
- The plan for assessment (CTG, ultrasound), 
- Strict safety netting for when to return.

## [[Pearls/Breech presentation|Breech presentation]] & ECV

- If your baby is breech at 36 weeks of pregnancy, it is unlikely that they will turn into a head-down position on their own.
- 2 out of 3 ECVs are successful at Leeds. 1 in 20 risk that the baby may turn back into breech
- There are very few reasons an ECV should not be offered. Safe in past LSCS, micro/macrosomic babies, oligo or polyhydramnios
- While you lie on the bed, the specially trained doctor or midwife puts their hands on your tummy, under the baby’s bottom. Gently but firmly, they move your baby in a forwards or sometimes a backwards roll. The procedure takes about 10 minutes. We monitor your baby’s heartbeat closely. **An ECV can be uncomfortable. Please tell the doctor or midwife to stop if you are in pain. If this happens, they may be able to move their hands to a more comfortable position for you. An ECV can be stopped at any time, if needed.**
- If you have a [[Pearls/Rhesus negative pregnancy|Rhesus negative pregnancy]] (RhD negative), we offer you a blood test and an ant-D injection after the procedure.
- All babies at breech at & after 36 weeks need a scan for [[Pearls/Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip|Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip]]

**Options if don't want ECV or ECV fails**
- Planned [[Pearls/Caesarean Section|Caesarean Section]] from 39 weeks
- Plan for a breech vaginal birth - can be more complicated and higher risk 

## [[Pearls/Induction of Labour|Induction of Labour]] 


**Bishop score:** used to assess whether induction of [[Full/Labour]] will be required:

|                          | **0**     | **1**        | **2**    | **3** |
| ------------------------ | --------- | ------------ | -------- | ----- |
| **Cervical position**    | Posterior | Intermediate | Anterior | -     |
| **Cervical consistency** | Firm      | Intermediate | Soft     | -     |
| **Cervical effacement**  | 0-30%     | 40-50%       | 60-70%   | 80%   |
| **Cervical dilation**    | <1 cm     | 1-2 cm       | 3-4 cm   | >5 cm |
| **Fetal station**        | -3        | -2           | -1, 0    | +1,+2 |

- A score of < 5 indicates that labour is unlikely to start without induction
- A score of $\geq$ 8 indicates a high chance of spontaneous labour, or response to interventions made to induce it 

**Membrane Sweep**
- Cervix must be already dilated
- Involves the examining finger passing through the cervix to rotate against the wall of the uterus to separate the chorionic membrane from the decidua
- Can be done by a midwife at the antenatal clinic. Woman are typically offered this at the 40- and 41- week antenatal visit
- **Regarded as an adjunct** prior to formal labour induction.
- Can be uncomfortable and cause mild cramping. Call MAU if heavy bleeding or if waters break

**Prostaglandins** - misoprostol (oral), dinoprostone (vaginal)
- Stimulate contraction of the uterine muscles
- Also have a role in ripening the cervix

**Oxytocin infusion**
- Stimulate contraction of the uterine muscles
- Also have a role in ripening the cervix

**Artificial Rupture of Membrane**
- Long plastic tool with a hook to break the amniotic sac during a vaginal exam 
- Cervix must be partially dilated and baby's head well engaged
- If not, risk of [[Pearls/Umbilical cord prolapse|Umbilical cord prolapse]]
- Once sac is broken risk of [[Full/Chorioamnionitis|Chorioamnionitis]]. Deliver within 24 hours

## Vaginal Birth After Caesarean

Giving birth vaginally carries small risks for you and your baby but, if you have a successful vaginal birth, future labours are less complicated with fewer risks for you and your baby.

- Planned VBAC is an appropriate method of delivery for pregnant women >37 weeks gestation with a single previous caesarean delivery 
- Case by case decision for pre-mature babies
- Around 70-75% have a successful vaginal delivery
- Contraindications include previous uterine rupture or classical caesarean scar or multiple previous caesarean sections

For those having a VBAC need the following:
- good IV access
- continuous CTG 
- adequate progress 


## [[Pearls/Miscarriage|Miscarriage]] Management

- Women with a pregnancy <6 weeks gestation with bleeding can be managed expectantly in the community
- $>$ 6 weeks **need a referral to an early pregnancy assessment unit**

First line involves waiting 7-14 days for the miscarriage to complete spontaneously. If unsuccessful then **vaginal misoprostol** given no indication for surgical management. 

#### Medical Management

**Missed miscarriage**
- Oral mifepristone 200mg - progesterone receptor antagonist → weakening attachment to the endometrial wall, cervical softening and dilation + induction of uterine contractions
- 48 hours later oral/vaginal/sublingual misoprostol 800mcg - prostaglandin analogue binds to myometrial cells causing strong contractions
- If bleed has not started within 48 hours of misoprostol should represent

**Incomplete miscarriage**
- Single dose of misoprostol 600 mcg

#### Surgical Management

Indicated when patient unwell otherwise patient choice. Two pain options are
- Vacuum aspiration - done under local as an outpatient
- Surgical management - in theatre

**_Anti-rhesus D prophylaxis_** is given to **_rhesus negative_** women having surgical management of miscarriage.

## [[Full/Menorrhagia|Menorrhagia]]

**Does not want contraception**
- Tranexamic acid when there is no pain (Antifibrinolytic)
- Mefenamic acid when there is pain (NSAID)

**Wants contraception**
1. [[Pearls/Intrauterine contraceptive device|Mirena coil]] - 5 years 
2. [[Pearls/Combined oral contraceptive pill|Combined oral contraceptive pill]]
3. Long acting progestogens


## [[Pearls/Polycystic ovary syndrome|Polycystic ovary syndrome]]

- Endocrine disorder not just "cysts" on the ovaries
- Triad of symptoms (irregular periods, hyperandrogenism, ultrasound findings)
- Treatment with weight loss and oral contraceptive (cycle regulation)

## [[Pearls/Endometriosis|Endometriosis]]

Validating her pain, explaining the pathophysiology in lay terms (womb lining growing outside the womb), and discussing management (analgesia, hormonal suppression, surgery).

Management depends on clinical features - poor correlation between laparoscopic findings and severity of symptoms. 
- NSAIDs $\pm$ paracetamol 
- If analgesia doesn't help, hormonal therapies such as [[Pearls/Combined oral contraceptive pill|Combined oral contraceptive pill]]
	- Dienogest - progestogen licensed for endometriosis
	- Mirena IUS

If analgesia & hormone treatment doesn't help or fertility is wanted, the patient should be referred to secondary care. Treatments include:
- GnRH analogues 
- Surgery - laparoscopic excision/ablation for those trying to conceive 

## [[Pearls/Contraceptive Methods|Contraceptive Methods]]

|**Condition**|**UKMEC Category**|**The Risk to Explain**|
|---|---|---|
|**Migraine with Aura**|**UKMEC 4** (Absolutely contraindicated)|Greatly increases the risk of an ischemic stroke.|
|**Age > 35 AND Smoker**|**UKMEC 3/4** (Depends on amount smoked)|High risk of cardiovascular events (Myocardial Infarction/Stroke) and VTE.|
|**BMI $\ge$ 35**|**UKMEC 3** (Risks outweigh benefits)|Significant risk of Venous Thromboembolism (DVT/PE).|
|**Hypertension**|**UKMEC 3/4** (Depends on severity)|Stroke and cardiovascular disease.|
|**Previous VTE**|**UKMEC 4**|Recurrent blood clots.|

#### Mirena 

- T-shaped device put into the womb that releases local dose of progesterone 
- Highly effective (8 years for contraception)
- Requires insertion, risk of irregular spotting for first 3-6 months

#### Copper Coil 

- Hormone free T shaped device - kills sperm 
- Highly effective last 5-10 years, with normal cycle
- Can make periods heavier, longer and more painful 

#### Implant

- Small rod placed under the skin of the upper arm, releasing progesterone 
- Most effective reversible method available - lasts 3 years
- Unpredictable bleeding profile: some none, some regular some irregular

#### Progesterone Only Pill

- If really wants a pill rather than a device
- Active breast cancer only contraindication. Can be started after birth
- Must be taken every day, new pills have 12 hour window. 
- Can cause irregular bleeding 

## Abnormal Cervical Screening Results

- Abnormal smear results **do not mean cancer**
- What CIN and HPV mean
- HPV positive with normal cytology - repeat the HPV test after 12 months
- HPV positive with abnormal cytology - refer for colposcopy
- Inadequate sample - repeat the smear after at least 3 months 

>*The test showed some changes in the cells on your cervix. I want to be very clear: **this is not cancer**. These are early warning signs. We call them 'pre-cancerous' changes, which just means that if we ignored them for many years, they could potentially turn into cancer. By finding them now, we can monitor them or easily treat them to stop that from happening."*

- Next step is to take a closer look at colposcopy. Similar to having a smear but takes longer

### Treatment Options

**Large loop excision of the Transformational Zone**
- If during the colposcopy the doctor sees any abnormal cells, they can offer a quick proceedure to remove them.
- Most common way is with a thin loop of wire that cuts through and removes the cells
- Before they do anything they'll numb your cervix. Might still feel pressure
- For 4 weeks after avoid tampons, penetrative sex and swimming/baths

- **Normal:** A watery, bloody, or brownish discharge for 2 to 4 weeks. It is also normal to pass a small scab after a week or so.
- **Red Flags:** Bleeding heavier than a normal period (e.g., soaking through a pad in an hour), a foul-smelling discharge, or developing a high fever.

- After 6 months will be invited back for another smear test to check its all gone.  

#### FAQs

**Does this mean my partner is cheating on me**
- No can be dormant for months, years or decades. Impossible to know when or from whom it was given

**Will it affect fertility**
- If we need to treat there are different options. Some of these will have no effect on fertility if that is what you'd prefer. 
- LLETZ may weaken the cervix so there is a slight increased risk of having the baby a little earlier. But let your midwife know at the time.

**Able to continue contraception?**
- Yes, having CIN does not restrict the use of any contraception. In active cervical cancer inserting IUD/IUS is contraindicated tho

## Hormone Replacement Therapy

- Ask about hysterectomy - oestrogen only if yes
- Last menstrual period? >12 months or not
- Mirena can be beneficial - often stops periods, and acts as HRT. Would just need oestrogen gel then

**Contraindications**
- Current or past breast-cancer
- Any oestrogen sensitive cancer
- Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding
- Untreated [[Pearls/Endometrial hyperplasia|Endometrial hyperplasia]]

- Small increased risk of breast cancer, 4 per 1,000. Does not increase your risk of dying from breast cancer
- No increased risk if HRT taken transdermally 
- Generally protective to the heart if started within 10 years of menopause. Only if started much later is it a risk

##### Other symptom management
- Vaginal lubricant or moisturiser
- fluoxetine, citalopram or venlafaxine for vasomotor symptoms

### Contraception

It is recommended to use effective contraception until the following time:  
- 12 months after the last period in women > 50 years
- 24 months after the last period in women < 50 years
