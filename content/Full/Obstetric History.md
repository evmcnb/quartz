### Key Pregnancy Details

Useful to confirm the details of the pregnancy before going forward:
- Gestational age - from LMP
- Gravidity - is the number of times a woman has been pregnant, regardless of the outcome
- Parity - the total number of times a woman has given birth to a child with a gestational age of 24 weeks or more, regardless of whether the child was born alive or not


## Presenting Complaint 

Usual couple open questions before progressing to closed 

## History of Presenting Complaint

#### Site

Ask about the **location** of the symptom:

- _“Where is the pain?”_
- _“Can you point to where you experience the pain?”_

#### Onset

Clarify **how** and **when** the symptom developed:

- _“Did the pain come on suddenly or gradually?”_
- _“When did the pain first start?”_
- _“How long have you been experiencing the pain?”_

#### Character

Ask about the **specific** **characteristics** of the symptom:

- _“How would you describe the pain?”_ (e.g. dull ache, throbbing, sharp)
- _“Is the pain constant or does it come and go?”_

#### Radiation

Ask if the symptom **moves** **anywhere** else:

- _“Does the pain spread elsewhere?”_

#### Associated symptoms

Ask if there are other symptoms which are **associated** with the primary symptom:

- _“Are there any other symptoms that seem associated with the pain?”_ (e.g. shortness of breath in pulmonary embolism)

#### Time course

Clarify how the symptom has **changed** over **time**:

- _“How has the pain changed over time?”_

#### Exacerbating or relieving factors

Ask if anything makes the symptom **worse** or **better**:

- _“Does anything make the pain worse?”_ (e.g. patients with symphysis pubis dysfunction may find going up or down the stairs makes things worse)
- _“Does anything make the pain better?”_ (e.g. patients with gastro-oesophageal reflux may find that antacid medication helps with their symptoms)

#### Severity

Assess the **severity** of the symptom by asking the patient to grade it on a scale of 0-10:

- _“On a scale of 0-10, how severe is the pain, if 0 is no pain and 10 is the worst pain you’ve ever experienced?”_

#### Reduced fetal movements
 
 Can be associated with fetal distress, and absent fetal movements may indicate early fetal demise. Fetal movements start between 16 and 24 weeks gestation. A mother will know the usual amount of movements.

*Have you noticed any change in the amount of your baby's movement?*

#### Vaginal bleeding

Bleeding in early pregnancy is common and can be normal or due to [[Pearls/Miscarriage|Miscarriage]] or [[Full/Ectopic Pregnancy|Ectopic Pregnancy]]. If the woman is later in the pregnancy, ask about any bleeding earlier in the pregnancy and if any additional investigations were carried out then:
- fever
- ultrasound scan results (position of placenta)
- cervical screening history
- sexual history
- past medical history

Important to ask about any pain associated with the bleeding and any trauma. 

*Have you noticed any bleeding coming from the vagina?*

Quantify as more or less than a period. 

#### Vaginal discharge

Important to distinguish between normal and abnormal. May be caused by [[Pearls/Sexually Transmitted Infection Treatments|Sexually Transmitted Infection Treatments]] and/or the fluid loss from the vagina could indicate rupture of amniotic membrane

*Have you noticed and changes to your vaginal discharge?*

Should note:
- Volume 
- Colour
- Consistency
- Smell 

#### Headache/visual changes/epigastric pain/oedema

[[Full/Pre-eclampsia|Pre-eclampsia]] is relatively common. The condition can be life threatening for the mother and the fetus. Need to ask about:
- Headache
- Swelling of the hands/feet/face
- Pain in the epigastric region
- Visual disturbances
- Reduced fetal movements 


#### Nausea and vomiting

Very common and usually mild requiring only reassurance and basic hydration advice. Typically begins between week 4 & 7, peaking at week 9-16 and resolving around 20th week. 

Persistent vomiting and severe nausea can progress to [[Pearls/Hyperemesis gravidarum|Hyperemesis gravidarum]]

Plus a summary

## Systemic enquiry

- **Systemic**: fatigue (e.g. anaemia), fever (e.g. chorioamnionitis, urinary tract infection), weight loss (e.g. hyperemesis gravidarum)
- **Respiratory**: dyspnoea (e.g. pulmonary embolism, anaemia), chest pain (e.g. pulmonary embolism)
- **Gastrointestinal**: abdominal pain (e.g. placental abruption), vomiting (e.g. hyperemesis gravidarum), reflux (e.g. gastro-oesophageal reflux disease), bowel movements (e.g. constipation)
- **Genitourinary**: urinary frequency, dysuria and urgency (e.g. urinary tract infection), abnormal vaginal discharge (e.g. vaginal candidiasis, gonorrhoea)
- **Neurological**: visual changes, motor or sensory disturbances, headache (e.g. pre-eclampsia)
- **Musculoskeletal**: pelvic pain (e.g. symphysis pubis dysfunction)
- **Dermatological**: rashes, skin lesions, linea nigra

## Current pregnancy

Asking a patient about **pregnancy planning** and if **artificial reproduction** techniques such as in vitro fertilisation (IVF) were used is important.

_“Can you tell me more about this pregnancy?”_

_“When did you find out you were pregnant?”_

### Gestation 

Clarify the current gestational age and if this is from ultrasound or not

### Scan results

Fetal anomaly scan between 18 and 20+6 weeks. Check
- Growth of the fetus
- Placental position - if embedded in the lower third of the uterine cavity there is an increased risk of [[Pearls/Placenta praevia|Placenta praevia]]
- Any other abnormalities

### Screening

- [[Pearls/Down's syndrome|Down's syndrome]]
- [[Pearls/Rhesus negative pregnancy|Rhesus status]] 
- Hepatitis B, HIV and syphilis


### Other details

- Clarify the patient took folic acid prior to conception and during the first trimester
- Explore the planned mode of delivery 
- Any medical illnesses during the pregnancy
- Any hospitalisation or emergency reviews

### Immunisation history

Check the patient is up-to-date with their vaccinations including: 
- Flu 
- Whooping cough
- Hep B if at risk 

### Mental health history

**Patients should be screened.**
- Ask about previous mental health diagnosis 
- Any current thoughts of self-harm/suicide if relevant - can be referred to the perinatal mental health team

## Previous obstetric history

- Gestation at delivery
- Birth weight
- Mode of delivery
- Complications 
- Assisted reproduction (IVF)

#### Stillbirth

Needs to be asked about sensitively. A **stillbirth** is when a baby is born deceased **after 24 completed weeks** of pregnancy.

Clarify the gestation of the stillbirth if not already documented

### Other pregnancies

#### Miscarriage

Loss of pregnancy before 24 weeks gestation. Clarify
- The trimester at which the miscarriage occurred (most common in the first trimester)
- Clarify if medical surgical management was required

#### Termination of pregnancy

- Clarify gestation
- Method of management - surgical or medical

#### Ectopic pregnancy

Clarify the site and how it was managed


## Gynaecological history 

**Cervical screening**
- Confirm the date and result of the last smear
- Ask if the patient received any treatment if abnormal and what follow ups are in place

**Previous conditions**
- [[Pearls/Sexually Transmitted Infection Treatments|Sexually Transmitted Infection Treatments]]
- [[Pearls/Endometriosis|Endometriosis]]
- Bartholin's cyst
- Cervical ectropion
- Malignancy


## Past Medical History

- _“Do you have any medical conditions?”_
- _“Are you currently seeing a doctor or specialist regularly?”_


Ask the patient if they've previously undergone any **surgery** or **procedures** in the past such as:
- Abdo or pelvic surgery - may influence delivery decisions
- Previous [[Pearls/Caesarean Section|Caesarean Section]] - increased risk of [[Pearls/Uterine rupture|Uterine rupture]]
- Loop excision of the transitional zone (LETZ) - increased risk of cervical incompetence 


## Drug history

- _“Are you currently taking any prescribed medications or over-the-counter treatments?”_
- _“Have you stopped taking any prescribed medication since you became pregnant?”_

Check if the patient was using contraception prior to becoming pregnant and if so what method. 

Check what medications they are on and if they are safe to continue for the pregnancy


## Family History

- Inherited genetic conditions: [[Full/Cystic Fibrosis|Cystic Fibrosis]], [[Pearls/Sickle Cell Disease|Sickle Cell Disease]]
- [[Full/Diabetes Mellitus|Diabetes Mellitus]]
- [[Full/Pre-eclampsia|Pre-eclampsia]]

## Social History

**General social context**
- Where they are living and with whom. 
- What tasks they can carry out
- If there has been any social work involvement 

**Smoking** - offer cessation

**Alcohol** - record frequency, type and volume.  Offer support services to reduce/stop their intake

**Recreational drug use** - drug cessation services

**Diet and weight** - what does it look like on an average day

**Occupation** - are there plans in place for maternity lave

**Domestic abuse** - _“Do you feel safe at home?”_
