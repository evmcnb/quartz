Caused by parvovirus B19. It is spread by the respiratory route and a person is infectious 3 to 5 days before the appearance of the rash

![[z_attachments/Pasted image 20260309203229.png|500]]

The rose-red rash makes the cheeks appear bright red, hence the name 'slapped cheek syndrome'. The rash may spread to the rest of the body but unlike many other rashes, it **only rarely involves the palms and soles.**

- Child feels better as soon as the rash appears
- For a couple months afterwards heat (warm bath, sunlight etc) will trigger a recurrence of the rash itself
- No specific treatment
- Can cause acute arthritis in adults
- Children can continue to go to school as by the time the rash appears they are no longer infectious
- Can affect 20 week fetus 

Parvovirus can cause aplastic anaemia.
