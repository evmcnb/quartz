A spontaneous termination of a pregnancy
- Early miscarriage - before 12 weeks gestation
- Late miscarriage - 12-24 weeks gestation

Different types: 
- **Missed miscarriage** - the fetus is no longer alive, but no symptoms have occured
- **Threatened miscarriage** - vaginal bleeding with a closed cervix and a fetus that is still alive
- **Inevitable miscarriage** - vaginal bleeding with an open cervix
- **Anembryonic pregnancy** - a gestational sac is present but contains no embryo
  
- **Incomplete miscarriage** - retrained products of conception remain in the uterus 
- **Complete miscarriage** - no products of conception left in the uterus 


> [!info] Epidemiology
> This risk of miscarriage is significantly increased with women over 35 or men over 40

### Management

#### Expectant management

- Women with a pregnancy <6 weeks gestation with bleeding can be managed expectantly in the community
- >6 weeks **need a referral to an early pregnancy assessment unit**

First line involves waiting 7-14 days for the miscarriage to complete spontaneously. If unsuccessful then medical or surgical management may be offered

> [!warning] NICE Criteria
> Some situations cannot be managed expectantly:
> - Increased risk of haemorrhage 
> - Evidence of infection
>   Previous adverse/traumatic pregnancy

#### Medical Management

Women should be offered anti-emetics and pain relief

**Missed miscarriage**
- Oral mifepristone 200mg - progesterone receptor antagonist → weakening attachment to the endometrial wall, cervical softening and dilation + induction of uterine contractions
- 48 hours later oral/vaginal/sublingual misoprostol 800mcg - prostaglandin analogue binds to myometrial cells causing strong contractions
- If bleed has not started within 48 hours of misoprostol should represent

**Incomplete miscarriage**
- Single dose of misoprostol 600 mcg

**A pregnancy test should be performed at 3 weeks**


#### Surgical Management

Indicated when patient unwell otherwise patient choice. Two pain options are
- Vacuum aspiration - done under local as an outpatient
- Surgical management - in theatre

**_Anti-rhesus D prophylaxis_** is given to **_rhesus negative_** women having surgical management of miscarriage.


### Recurrent miscarriage

Defined as 3 or more consecutive spontaneous abortions

Causes:
- [[Antiphospholipid syndrome]]
- Endocrine disorders: poorly controlled [[Full/Diabetes Mellitus|Diabetes Mellitus]]/thyroid disorders. [[Pearls/Polycystic ovary syndrome|Polycystic ovary syndrome]]
- Uterine abnormality
- Parental chromosomal abnormalities
- Smoking

**Women with 3 or more consecutive miscarriages should be referred to a specialist recurrent miscarriage clinic**
