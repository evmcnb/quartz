- Abnormalities of kidney function or structure present on at least 2 occasions **>3 months apart**.
- Unlikely to be reversible
- CKD replaces the term chronic renal failure.

5 Stages dependent upon eGFR and albumin/creatine ratio (ACR)

## Causes/Factors

- [[Full/Diabetes Mellitus]]
- [[Full/Essential hypertension|Hypertension]]
- Age 75+
- [[Full/Glomerulonephritis]]
- Obstruction of kidney (without acute effect)
- [[Full/Polycystic Kidney Disease]]
- [[Pearls/Alport syndrome]]
- APOL 1 Gene
- [[Full/Acute Kidney Injury]]
- [[Reflux Nephropathy]]
- Chronic nephrotoxin use

## Symptoms

- Silent disease
- May not manifest until significant decrease in kidney function

- Reduced urine output
- Aches and pains
- Salt and water retention
- Loss of lean body mass-muscle weakness
- Vomiting etc

## Diagnostic Tests


A diagnosis can be made when there are consistent results **_over three months_** of either:
- **_Estimated glomerular filtration rate_** (**_eGFR_**) is sustained **_below 60 mL/min/1.73 m_****_2_**
- **_Urine albumin:creatinine ratio_** (**_ACR_**) is sustained **_above 3 mg/mmol_**

Things to check:
- Proteinuria - urine albumin: creatinine ratio 
- Haematuria - urine dip or microscopy 
- Renal US
- BP for hypertension
- HbA1c for diabetes
- Lipid profile for hypercholesterolaemia 

Distinguish between AKI and CKD: baseline creatinine value. Favouring CKD:
- [[Pearls/Anaemia|Anaemia]]
- low calcium
- high phosphate
- elevated parathroid hormone
- Ultrasound appearances

## Management


The **_Kidney Failure Risk Equation_** can be used to estimate the 5-year risk of kidney failure requiring dialysis.


> [!info] Referral Criteria 
> NICE CKS suggest referral to a renal specialist when:
> - eGFR < 30
> - Urine ACR >70
> - Accelerated progression - decrease in eGFR of 25% of 15 within 12 months
> - 5-year risk of requiring dialysis over 5%
> - Uncontrolled hypertension despite 4 or more anti-hypertensives

Treat the underlying causes: 
- Change life style to reduce risk
- Optimise diabetic and HTN control (target <130/80)
- Reduce/remove nephrotoxic drugs
- Tolvaptan (for [[Full/Polycystic Kidney Disease]])


**Medications** to help slow disease progression/complications
- ACEi - offered to all with suspicious ACR 
- SGLT-2i - specifically dapagliflozin esp in those with diabetes 
- [[Pearls/Statins|Statin]] - primary prevention


## Complications/red Flags

- Increased risk of other outcomes
  - [[Full/Coronary Artery Disease]]
  - [[Full/Acute Kidney Injury]]
  - [[Pearls/Anaemia|Anaemia]] - iron and EPO 
  - [[Full/Essential hypertension]]

### Renal bone disease

- High phosphate - reduced excretion from the kidneys 
- Low vitamin D - lack of metabolism 
- Low serum calcium - active vitamin D is essential for calcium reabsorption in the intestines

The low calcium leads to secondary [[Pearls/Hyperparathyroid|Hyperparathyroid]]. The parathyroid hormone stimulates osteoclast activity to increase bone absorption.

**Results in:**
- [[Pearls/Osteomalacia|Osteomalacia]] - due to increased bone turnover without calcium supply
- [[Osteosclerosis]] - osteoblasts respond by increasing their activity to match the osteoclasts, creating new improperly mineralised bone due to the lack of calcium 
- Rugger jersey spine - characteristic finding on spinal XR. Sclerosis of bone ends of each vertebral body (whiter) and osteomalacia in the center of the body (darker)

**Management**
- Low phosphate diet
- Phosphate binders
- Active vitamin D supplementation
- Adequate calcium intake
