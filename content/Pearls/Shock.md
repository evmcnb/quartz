4 main types:
- Hypovolaemic - (bleed, diarrhoea, vomiting)
- Cardiogenic - ()
- Obstructive
- Distributive  - (sepsis, anaphylaxis)


### Presentation in all:

- Altered mental status
- Tachycardia
- Hypotension
- Tachypnea 
- Cold, clammy extremities 


Pre-shock (compensatory mechanisms active) $\rightarrow$ Shock (cannot compensate, symptoms appear) $\rightarrow$ End organ dysfunction (irreversible organ damage)

## Investigations

- FBC 
- Urinalysis
- ABG/VBG
- ECG
- Echo
- CXR
- Blood cultures CRP

##### Hypovolaemic 

Sudden drop in blood volume either haemorrhagic (trauma, internal bleeding) or not (burns, water loss from infection). Decrease in MAP

##### Cardiogenic

Failure to pump blood around the body. 
1. Cardiomyopathic - [[Full/myocardial infarction|myocardial infarction]], [[Pearls/Myocarditis|Myocarditis]]
2. Arrhythmic - arrhythmia leading to loss of MAP
3. Mechanical - structural issues in the heart, valvular insufficiencies/defects, ventricular rupture. 
4. Other - toxins, infection, severe hypertension

##### Obstructive 

Obstruction to cardiac outflow or filling
- [[Full/pulmonary embolism|pulmonary embolism]]
- Severe pulmonary hypertension
- [[Cardiac Tamponade]]
- Constrictive [[Pearls/Pericarditis|Pericarditis]]
- [[Full/pneumothorax|pneumothorax]]

##### Distributive 

Systemic vasodilation directing intravascular volume to the interstitial space 
- [[Full/Sepsis|Sepsis]]
- [[Pearls/Anaphylactic shock|Anaphylactic shock]]
- Neurogenic - severe traumatic brain or spinal cord injury that compromises the sympathetic nervous system. Results in unopposed parasympathetic action. Spinal cord injuries above T6


## Management 

Principles of management; specifics will vary for each

- O2 for all patients
- Aggressive fluids (blood products if haemorrhaging)
- Low GCS consider airway input
- Monitor glucose
- Maintain temperature at 36$\degree$C
- Assess for underlying cause
