## Abdominal Pain

Key diagnostic criteria is between surgical and non-surgical causes:
- [[Full/Appendicitis|Appendicitis]] (3+ years)
- [[Pearls/Intussusception|Intussusception]] (6-18 months)
- [[Pearls/Intestinal Malrotation|Intestinal Malrotation]] (1-3 weeks)
- [[Pearls/Mesenteric adenitis|Mesenteric adenitis]] (following viral infection)
- [[Full/Urinary Tract Infection|Urinary Tract Infection]]
- [[Pearls/Constipation in Children|Constipation in Children]]

**Always need to rule out [[Pearls/Testicular Torsion|Testicular Torsion]]** 

> [!danger] Bilious vomiting - always abnormal
> Surgical emergency until proven otherwise (possible [[Pearls/Volvulus]]) 

Symptoms in neonates and younger infants are often erroneously attributed to abdominal pain by parents. A thorough examination and consideration of a broad range of differential diagnoses is required

**Repeat exam is useful to assess persistence/evolution of symptoms**

Analgesia should be given and does not mask potentially serious causes of pain.

## [[Full/asthma|Asthma]] & [[Pearls/Pre-school wheeze in children|wheeze]]

Acute wheeze can have a number of different causes including asthma, viral infections, pneumonia, inhaled foreign bodies, gastro-oesophageal reflux and cardiac failure.

Acute wheeze with viral infection is very common. Unlike asthma, they **do not respond to $\beta$ agonists**. Steroids are unlikely to be beneficial in children <5 with sats >92% and only mild respiratory distres.

Wheeze in infants (<12 months) is likely bronchiolitis

## [[Full/Bronchiolitis|Bronchiolitis]]

- Clinical diagnosis usually made in infants <12 months old
- Typical presentation of cough and coryzal symptoms with widespread inspiratory crackles and expiratory wheeze.
- Mild bronchiolitis can be managed in the community (usually criteria plus feeding >50% of normal)
- No place for $\beta$ agonists or steroids 

## [[Full/Croup|Croup]]

- Clinical syndrome of barking cough, inspiratory stridor and a hoarse voice
- 80% caused by parainfluenza virus 
- 6 months - 6 years, peak at 2 years
- Need to consider more sinister causes of airway issues 
- Mild croup can be managed in the community
- Dexamethasone (0.15mg/kg). Not all pharmacies keep in stock. If likely to be a delay in issuing then give oral prednisolone 1-2mg/kg.

## Fever & mild infections

- Better to usually measure temperature in the axilla rather than distressing further with ear probe
- Any fever >38 needs urgent assessment (unless within 12 hours of a vaccine)
- Beware that classic signs of meningitis are often absent in infants with bacterial meningitis.

## Gastroenteritis

- Gastroenteritis is defined as diarrhoea +/- vomiting +/- abdominal pain. 
- Children with just vomiting or pain without diarrhoea should be assessed for other causes before a diagnosis of gastroenteritis is made.

The following features should prompt careful consideration of alternative differential diagnoses:
- Severe abdominal pain or abdominal signs
- Persistent diarrhoea (>10 days)
- Blood in stool
- Bilious (green) vomit - should all be referred for assessment
- Persistent vomiting without diarrhoea

Oral rehydration is mainstay of treatment - dioralyte or apple juice with water 1:1
