Most children achieve continence by 3 or 4 years old. 

Enuresis is the involuntary discharge or urine by day or night or both in a child aged **5 years** or older (in the absence of congenital or acquired defects of the nervous system of urinary tract)

Nocturnal enuresis can be defined as either primary (the child has never achieved continence) or secondary (the child has been dry for at least 6 months before)

Management escalation:
1. Look for possible causes/triggers: consipation, [[Full/Diabetes Mellitus|Diabetes Mellitus]], UTI if secondary
2. General advice: fluid intake, toileting patterns (during day and before bed)
3. Reward system (star charts) - for using the toilet before bed rather than "dry nights"
4. Enuresis alarm - high success rate
5. Desmopressin - for short-term control or an alarm is ineffective/not acceptable to the family 

Assess family dynamic as may be a sign of abuse.
