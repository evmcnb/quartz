Three main types:
- General
- Regional
- Local

**Balanced anaesthesia**
- Amnesia - lack of response and recall to noxious stimuli
- Analgesia
- Akinesis

## General 
### Induction Agents

IV agents - lasts 4-10 minutes:
- Propofol - most commonly used induction and maintenance agent  (1.5 - 2.5mg/kg)
	- Good suppression of airway reflexes - easy igel
	- Causes pain on injection & invol movements
	- Marked drop in BP 
- Thiopentone - mainly used for RSI (4 -  5mg/kg)
	- Barbiturate
	- Contraindicated in porphyria
- Etomidate - cardio-stable agent (0.2 - 0.3mg/kg)
	- Causes adreno-cortical suppression
- Ketamine - not commonly used as induction agent (1 - 1.5 mg/kg)
	- Increases HR & BP
	- Causes dissociative anaesthesia & emergence delirium 

Inhalational agents - used in paediatric or needle-phobic patients
- Sevoflurane (MAC 2%) - no irritation to airway & sweet smalling
- Desflurane (MAC 6%) - rapid onset and offset. Phased out due to greenhouse effect
- Isoflurane (MAC 1.15%) - least effect on organ blood flow (used in organ donation)
### Maintenance

Can be continuous infusion of induction agent or inhalational agents. Most commonly propofol

Any **inhalational agent** can be used for maintenance.  

### Muscle relaxants

Depolarising:
- Suxamethonium (1 -1.5mg/kg) - 30-45 seconds to effect, RSI 
	- Can lead to muscle pains, fasciculations, hyperkalaemia, rise in ICP, IOP and gastric pressure 
	- Can cause malignant hyperthermia

Non-depolarising - slow onset and variable duration but with less side effects
- Short-acting: mivacurium 
- Intermediate acting: **rocuronium**, vecuronium, atracurium
- Long acting: Pancuronium 
- Ability to reverse with neostigmine & glycopyrrolate 

### Airway management

Long duration surgeries, those with aspiration risk  or inaccessible airway during the surgery $\rightarrow$ **ET tube** a definitive airway 

Supraglottic airways can be used when muscle paralysis is not required 


### Analgesia

#### Opioids

**Short acting**
- Suppress response to laryngoscopy and airway insertion & surgical pain
- Fentanyl is the most common (then alfentanil and remifentanil)

**Long acting**
- For intra-op and post-op analgesia
- **Morphine** & oxycodone

#### Non-opioid

Paracetamol most common


### Fluids

- Most common in surgery is Hartmann's solution 
- Blood products may be used as required


## Regional


### Central Neuraxial Blocks

Commonly used for operations on the lower half of the body - obstetrics, orthopaedics, some gen surg. 

> [!info] Spinal Cord Anatomy
> **What are the three layers of the spinal cord?**
> - Pia mater, arachnoid mater, dura mater
>   
> **Where is CSF present?**
> - Between pia and arachnoid i.e. subarachnoid space
> - An injection into this space is a spinal block
>   
>**What lies between the dura mater and the vertebral canal?**
>- Epidural space.
>  
>**Where does the spinal cord end in adults?**
>  - Lower border of L1
>    
> **Where does the subarachnoid space end?**
>  - S1
>    
> ![[z_attachments/Epidrual-Injection-In-Lumbar-Spine.png|450]]


During an epi-dural block the needle passes through: skin, sub cut fat, supraspinous ligament, interspinous ligament, ligamentum flavum and parietal layer of dura mater.

For a spinal injection the needle goes through the dura and arachnoid 

**Advantages**
- Patients with respiratory disease
- Patients whom IV analgesics may be less desirable (OSA, PONV)
