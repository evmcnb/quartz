Patients having cancer treatment whose neutrophil count is $< 1 \times 10^9/L$ **and** have:
- Temperature $>38\degree C$ **OR**
- Other signs and symptoms consistent with clinically significant sepsis
should be treated as a case of neutropenic sepsis until proven otherwise


> [!danger] 
> Overall mortality is around 5% and patients can quickly decompensate
> - Typically occurs 7-14 days post chemotherapy (timing is key to ask on a history)
> - Ask for local trust policy for neutropenic sepsis 



## Causes/Factors

Gram-positive bacteria such as *Staphylococcus epidermidis* are the most common cause of neutropenic sepsis


Common with intense chemo regimes:
- Haematological malignancies (e.g. [[Pearls/Acute myeloid leukaemia|Acute myeloid leukaemia]])
- Germ cell tumours
- Breast cancers

Medications that may cause neutropenia include
- **_Chemotherapy_** (for cancer)
- **_Clozapine_** (for schizophrenia)
- **_Hydroxychloroquine_** (for rheumatoid arthritis)
- **_Methotrexate_** (for rheumatoid arthritis)
- **_Sulfasalazine_** (for rheumatoid arthritis)
- **_Carbimazole_** (for hyperthyroidism)
- **_Quinine_** (for malaria)
- **_Infliximab_** (a monoclonal antibody used for various autoimmune conditions)
- **_Rituximab_** (a monoclonal antibody used for various autoimmune conditions and cancer

## Symptoms

**From the history**
- Timing of chemo, new lines, stents etc
- Previous episodes

## Signs

**Localise the source of infection**
- CNS (neuro signs)
- Mucositis
- Central line
- Cellulitis
- Resp
- Ureteric stent
- Osteomyelitis 
- GI/urinary tract
