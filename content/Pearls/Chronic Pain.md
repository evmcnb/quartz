Pain that is present/reoccurs in one or more areas for **more than 3 months**. 
- Chronic primary pain - where no underlying condition can adequately explain the pain
- Chronic secondary pain - where an underlying condition can explain the pain - e.g. arthritis

Pain is a psychological manifestation of biological stimuli. Chronic pain is a complex condition that can be challenging to manage. It often fluctuates, with flare-ups, and may get worse over time. Good communication and building a relationship is important. Part of management aims to maintain the quality of life despite the pain.

> [!warning] Pain management
> NICE Guidelines (2021) clearly state to ***avoid*** essentially all forms of analgesia in chronic primary pain except for anti-depressants. They should **not** be started on
> - Paracetamol 
> - NSAIDs
> - Opiates
> - Anti-eplieptics (pregabalin/gabapentin)

Management of chronic primary pain often involves  modulating the brains interpretation of pain signals. This includes: 
- Group exercise
- Acceptance and commitment therapy
- Cognitive behavioural therapy
- Acupuncture
- Anti-depressants (e.g., amitriptyline, duloxetine or an SSRI)
