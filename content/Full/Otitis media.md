#### Glue ear

Glue ear describes otitis media with an effusion. It is common. Hearing loss is usually the presenting feature. Usually treated conservatively and resolves without treatment within 3 months. 
- AOM = infection of middle ear  
- OME = negative pressure leading to fluid build up (glue) usually caused by eustachian tube dysfunction. You tend to get a retracted tympanic membrane with OME.

## Causes/Factors

Usually preceded by a viral upper respiratory tract infection, the infection in the ear is usually ***Strep pneumoniae* or *Haem. influenzae***

Viral URTIs are thought to disturb the normal microbiome allowing bacteria to infect the middle ear via the Eustachian tube. 

## Symptoms

Uncomplicated

- <72 hours of mild pain
- Absence of severe systemic symptoms
- Temp <39
- No ear discharge

Complicated

- Severe pain
- Perforated eardrum
- And/or purulent discharge
- Bilateral infection

## Signs

- Bulging tympanic membrane - loss of light refex
- Opacification or erythema of the tympanic membrane
- Perforation with purulent otorrhoea

## Diagnostic Tests

Majority of criteria use the following to diagnose: 
- acute onset of symptoms
- presence of middle ear effusion
- inflammation of the tympanic membrane 

Swab any pus

## Management

Generally a self-limiting condition that does not require antibiotics. Parents should be advised to seek help with the symptoms worse or do not improve after 3 days.

If an antibiotic is given:
- amoxicillin 500mg TDS 5-7 days is 1st line. 
- Eerythromycin or clarithromycin 2nd line.


> [!warning] Criteria for immediate antibiotic prescription
> - The child is <3 months old
> - Immunocompromise or high risk of complications 
> - The child is < 2 years old **with** bilateral otitis media
> - If symptoms are present for 4 or more days
> - **Otitis media with perforation** and/or discharge in the canal 


Recurrent acute otitis media defined as: 3 or more episodes in 6 months, 4+ episodes in 12 months 

For children a surgical option may be grommets and myringotomy 

## Complications/red Flags

###### Mastoiditis

- Most common complication - inflammation of the mastoid [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] and air cells
- CT scan always required
- Analgesia, IV antibiotics +/- mastoidectomy
- Risks [[Full/Meningitis|Meningitis]] if left untreated
