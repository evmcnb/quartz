Enhance the effect of the inhibitory neurotransmitter gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) by increasing the **frequency** of chloride channels.

Used for:
- sedation
- hypnotic
- anxiolytic
- anticonvulsant
- muscle relaxant


### Common Formulations

#### Midazolam 

Fast onset and short half-life. Can be found as: 
- Buccal
- IV
- SC ([[Full/Palliative Care|Palliative Care]])
- Intranasal/oral solution

#### Diazepam

Diazepam has a fast onset but a very long half-life. Can be found as:
- Rectal 
- Oral (tablets & liquid)
- IV

#### Lorazepam

Lorazepam is less lipid-soluble than diazepam, meaning it takes slightly longer to work but stays in the central nervous system longer, preventing seizure recurrence. Used to terminate [[Pearls/Epilepsy#Status epilepticus|status epilepticus]]
- IV

### Withdrawal 

The dose should be withdrawn in steps of about 1/8 (range 1/10 to 1/4) of the daily dose every fortnight.

If patients withdraw too quickly from benzodiazepines they may experience withdrawal with symptoms very similar to [[Pearls/Alcohol withdrawal|Alcohol withdrawal]]:

- insomnia
- irritability
- anxiety
- tremor
- loss of appetite
- tinnitus
- perspiration
- perceptual disturbances
- seizures
