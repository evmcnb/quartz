> [!info] Normal Calcium Homeostasis
> **Hyper-**
> - Thyroid recognise high blood calcium levels
 > - Release calcitonin which promotes osteoblasts to deposits calcium in the bones
> - Reduces absorption of calcium from kidneys
> - Therefore, calcium blood levels fall
> 
> **Hypo**
> - Low calcium level is detected by the parathyroid gland
> - Releases PTH which promotes osteoclasts to release calcium from the bones
> - Also stimulates kidneys to absorb calcium from the urine
> - Convert vitamin D to stimulate the bowel to absorb the calcium


**Non-PTH mediated** - PTH low/suppressed, vitamin D intoxication, chronic granulomatous disorders, thiazide diuretics

**PTH-mediated** - high PTH, primary hyperparathyroidism

## Clinical Presentation

**Stones** - renal stones due to hypercalciuria

**Bones** - [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] pain and [[Full/osteoporosis]] due to inappropriately high PTH

**Moans** - lethargy, fatigue, [[Full/Depression]]

**Groans** - abdominal pain, constipation, nausea, vomiting

#### Expanded: 

Mild
* Polyuria, polydipsia
* Mood disturbance
* Nausea, fatigue constipation
* Anorexia

Severe
* Abdo pain
* Coma
* Pancreatitis
* Cardiac arrhythmias - isolated QTc shortening


All 
- **Neuro**: confusion, seizures, proximal neuropathy, hyporeflexia, coma
- **General**: dehydration, weakness, fatigue, bone pain
- **GI**: weight loss, N&V, abdo pain, constipation, [[Full/Ileus|Ileus]], dyspepsia, [[Full/pancreatitis|pancreatitis]], polyuria
- **Cardiac:** bradycardia, short QTc, wide T wave, prolonged PR 

### Paraneoplastic Hypercalcaemia

When the hypercalcaemia is due to a malignancy. Range of factors produced by tumours which increase bone reportion and potentially increase renal tubular calcium reabsorption:
- **Transforming growth factor alpha** – polypeptide stimulator of growth and replication that is produced by many tumour cells. It is powerful stimulator of bone resorption
- **Parathyroid hormone related peptides** – tumour associated protein that mimics PTH, stimulating bone reabsorption and increasing plasma calcium. Most commonly with [[Pearls/Lung Tumours#Non-small cell management|NSCLC]]

Common culprits:
- Lung ([[Pearls/Lung Tumours#Non-small cell management|NSCLC]])
- [[Osteosarcoma]]
- [[Full/Breast carcinoma|Breast carcinoma]]
- [[Full/Prostatic Adenocarcinoma|Prostatic Adenocarcinoma]]
- [[Full/Renal Cell Carcinoma|Renal Cell Carcinoma]]
- [[Pearls/Multiple myeloma|Multiple myeloma]] & [[Pearls/Lymphoma|Lymphoma]]
