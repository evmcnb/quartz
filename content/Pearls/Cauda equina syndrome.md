Where the lumbosacral nerve roots that extend below the spinal cord are compressed

Need to consider in any patient who presents with new/worsening lower back pain. Late diagnosis may lead to permanent nerve damage resulting in long term leg weakness and incontinence.

**Causes:**  
- the most common cause is a central disc prolapse
    - this typically occurs at L4/5 or L5/S1
- other causes include:
    - [[Pearls/Spinal metastases|Spinal metastases]]
    - infection: abscess, discitis
    - trauma
    - haematoma

No one symptom/sign to include/exclude CES. Features include:
- low back pain
- bilateral sciatica (present in 50% of cases)
- altered sensation in perianal area
- decreased anal tone
- urinary dysfunction 

Investigations - urgent MRI

Management - surgical decompression
