* **Type I:** **A**llergic / Anaphylactic
* **Type II:** **C**ytotoxic
* **Type III:** **I**mmune Complex
* **Type IV:** **D**elayed

---

## Detailed Breakdown

| Type | Name | Key Mediator | Onset Time | Mechanism | Common Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | **Immediate** (Allergic) | **IgE** | Seconds to Minutes | Antigen binds to IgE on **mast cells** & basophils, triggering degranulation (histamine release). | • Anaphylaxis<br>• Asthma<br>• Bee stings<br>• Hay fever |
| **II** | **Cytotoxic** | **IgG** or **IgM** | Minutes to Hours | Antibodies bind directly to antigens on **cell surfaces**, causing cell destruction via complement or phagocytosis. | • Blood transfusion reactions<br>• Hemolytic disease of the newborn<br>• Graves' disease |
| **III** | **Immune Complex** | **IgG** or **IgM** | 3–10 Hours | Antigen-Antibody **complexes** form and deposit in tissues (joints, vessels, kidneys), triggering inflammation. | • Serum sickness<br>• Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)<br>• Rheumatoid Arthritis |
| **IV** | **Delayed** | **T-Cells** | 48–72 Hours | Sensitized **T-cells** (CD4+ or CD8+) release cytokines and activate macrophages. **No antibodies involved.** | • Contact dermatitis (Poison Ivy/Nickel)<br>• TB Skin Test (Mantoux)<br>• Transplant rejection |
