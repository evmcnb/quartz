Diagnosis of exclusion. Persistent fatigue for a minimum of 6 weeks which significantly impairs the ability to engage in usual activities which cannot be explained by any other condition.

- Debilitating fatigue not caused by excessive physical or cognitive exertion and not significantly relieved by rest
- Unrefreshing sleep/sleep disturbance
- Cognitive problems - word finding difficulties, poor concentration & short term memory impairment
- *Post-exertional malaise* 

Diagnosis can be made after **3 months** of persistent long-term symptoms and if they cannot be explained by any other condition

> [!warning] Controversial NICE Guidelines (2021): 
> The current NICE guidelines for ME/CFS have significant critiques from the wider research community; see [here](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/researchers-produce-systematic-critique-of-2021-nice-guideline-on-cfs-and-me), [here](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/representing-you/policy-areas/guidance-on-me-cfs) and [here](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9141828/)
> 1. NICE retrospectively downgraded large RCTs & studies which didn't include post-exertional symptom exacerbation - most of which supported CBT and exercise therapy.
> 2. Selectively considered outcomes and included flawed methodological studies
> 3. Prioritised evidence from qualitative studies and patient organised surveys over the gold standard RCT which contradicted each other. 
> 4. At the same time, NICE downgraded all outcomes related to fatigue considering it too subjective to measure
> 5. Little use of data aggregation and meta-analysis
> 6. NICE mischaracterised exercise therapy and removed it as a treatment 
> 7. In replacement they recommend "energy management" in which patients are encouraged to stay within limits imposed by their illness. There is no evidence to support this approach 
> 8. In fact, the only trial of  "pacing" showed no benefit over specialist medical care, and a reduced effectiveness over CBT or exercise therapy. 
>    
>  The general consensus of the wider community seems to be that these changes were informed by ideology rather than evidence

## Causes/Factors

- Unknown
- Viral infection trigger?
- Mental stress
- [[Full/Depression|Depression]]
- Traumatic event

## Symptoms

- Extreme fatigue
- Unrefreshing sleep problems
- Difficulty with physical and mental activities


#### Severity

**Mild**
- Able to care for oneself and do some domestic tasks but have difficulty with mobility
- Able to work or attend education but to do this probably stopped all leisure and social pursuits
- Often have reduced hours, take days off and use the weekend to cope with the rest of the week

**Moderate**
- Reduced mobility and restrictions in all activities of daily living
- May have peaks and troughs in level of symptoms and ability to do things
- Have usually stopped work or education and need rest periods - often in the afternoon
- Sleep at night is generally poor and disturbed 

**Severe**
- Unable to do any activity for oneself
- Severe cognitive difficulty and may depend on a wheelchair for mobility
- Often unable to leave the house or have a severe and prolonged effect after doing so
- May also spend most of the time in bed with constant tiredness and often extremely sensitive to light and sound. 

## Management

Rule out all other causes of fatigue: 
- FBC serum ferritin 
- ESR and CRP
- LFTs
- Renal function
- TFTs
- HbA1c
- IgA tissues transglutaminases
- Urinalysis
- CK
- Bone chemistry and myeloma screen


Case by case investigations
- Vitamin d levels
- Vitamin B12 and folate levels
- Mon spot test for infectious mononucleosis
- HIV test
- Hepatitis serology
- Early morning cortisol level
- Chest Xray and sputum samples to screen for tuberculosis
