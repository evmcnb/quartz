"Wear and tear" - nothing to do from immune system

1. Cartilage flakes off
2. Fragments of cartilage triggers slight inflammatory reaction
3. [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] form cysts as tries to remodel

![|575](z_attachments/575-1.png)

Affects weight bearing joints
## Causes/Factors

- Age - 80% over 75y
- F > M 
- Occupation - farmers 
- Athlete (doing something to accelerate wear)
- FH
- Other inflammatory arthropathies
- Osteoporosis
- Joint malalignment 

## Signs 

- Stiffness worse after long periods of inactivity (e.g. sleep), which lasts only a few minutes
- Pain after exercise relieved by rest
- Crepitus over joint 
- Inflammation around the joint


Hand specific
- Usually bilateral: CMC and DIP joints are effected more than PIPs
- Heberen's nodes (DIP joint)
- Bouchard's nodes (PIP joint)
- Carpometacarpal involvement - squaring of the thumbs (fixed adduction)

## Symptoms

- Increasing pain over months/years
- Siffness and reduced range of movement (especially base of thumb)
- Worse on use
- Referred pain to the joint below
- Reduced power due to muscle wastage from reduced use
- Can get referred pain from joints above and below

No systemic features or early morning stiffness. This is seen in [[Full/Inflammatory Arthritis#Rheumatoid Arthritis|RA]]
## Diagnostic Tests

- Clinical examination - if typical features diagnosis can be made. 
- XR AP + lateral of affected joint

***LOSS Pneumonic***
1. **L**oss of joint space
2. **O**steophyte formation (bony spurs protrude out)
3. **S**clerosis (underlying [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] becomes hard)
4. **S**ubchondrial cyst


- Normal CRP and antibodies
- Does not affect other organs

## Management

Life-style:
- Exercise
- Weight loss if indicated
- Occupational therapy

**Medications**
1. Topical NSAIDs
2. Oral NSAIDs + PPI cover (contraindicated in patients taking aspirin)
3. Only paracetamol or weak opioids in cases of: infrequent use/short term relief or above medications are contraindicated (e.g. with increased bleeding risk)

Intra-articular steroid injections may be tried if standard pharmacological treatment is ineffective. Patients should be aware that they only provide short-term relief (2-10 weeks)
(don't recommend glucosamine supplements - no benefit) 

If conservative methods fail then refer for consideration of joint replacement
### Joint replacement


Joints to replace:
1. Hip
2. Knee
3. Shoulder 

Not performed until conservative methods fail.
- around 25% of patients are now younger than 60-years-old
- whilst obesity is often thought to be a barrier to joint replacement there is only a slight increase in short-term complications. There is no difference in long-term joint replacement survival

**Surgical techniques**  
- **Total joint replacement**: for hips the most common type of operation is a cemented hip replacement. A metal femoral component is cemented into the femoral shaft. This is accompanied by a cemented acetabular polyethylene cup
- uncemented hip replacements are becoming increasingly popular, particularly in younger more active patients. They are more expensive than conventional cemented hip replacements
- hip resurfacing is also sometimes used where a metal cap is attached over the femoral head. This is often used in younger patients and has the advantage that the femoral neck is preserved which may be useful if conventional arthroplasty is needed later in life

Recovery: 
- Given course of physiotherapy and home exercises
- Walking sticks or crutches used for up to 6 weeks after replacement 

Avoid to minimise the risk of dislocation:
- Avoid flexing the hip
- Avoid low chairs
- Do not cross your legs 
- Sleep on your back for the first 6 weeks


## Complications/red Flags

- Total hip replacement dislocation 
- Wound and joint infections 
- VTE - receive LMWH for 4 weeks following replacement
