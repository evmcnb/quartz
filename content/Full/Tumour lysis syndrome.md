Oncological emergency due to turnover of high cell mass malignancies resulting in severe metabolic derangement. Usually in aggressive malignancies 

Chemotherapy for rapidly proliferating tumours (leukaemia, [[Pearls/Lymphoma|Lymphoma]], myeloma) leads to cell death add ↑urate, K+, phosphate and ↓calcium.

Prevent with hydration and uricolytics eg rasburicase, allpurinol
## Causes/Factors

- Lymphoproliferative malignancy + chemotherapy, radiotherapy or corticosteroids
- Can occur spontaneously - but normally **12-72h post chemotherapy** 
- Large tumour burden
- LDH > 1500 IU
- Extensive bone marrow involvement
- High tumour sensitivity to [[Pearls/Cytotoxic agents|Cytotoxic agents]]

- Poorly differentiated lymphomas - e.g. [[Burkitt lymphoma]] and [[Non-Hodgkin lymphoma]]s
- Leukaemia - [[Pearls/Acute myeloid leukaemia|Acute myeloid leukaemia]], [[Pearls/Chronic myeloid leukaemia|Chronic myeloid leukaemia]] and [[Pearls/Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia|Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia]] 
-Some fast-growing solid tumours - [[Pearls/Liver Tumours|hepatocellular carcinoma]], [[Full/Testicular Cancer]], [[Full/Lung Tumours|small cell lung cancer]], [[Full/Breast carcinoma|Breast carcinoma]], and neuroblastoma


## Presentation

- Nausea and vomiting
- Confusion
- Muscle cramps and tetany
- Diarrhoea
- Lethargy
- Reduced urine output
- Syncope
- Chest pain
- Palpitations

**Electrolytes**
- Hyperkalaemia
- Hyperphosphataemia
- **Hypo**calcaemia
- Hyperuricaemia
- High creatinine and urea consistent with renal impairment
- Low HCO3 consistent with metabolic acidosis +/- hyperlactemia
- High lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)
## Investigations

- [[Full/RRAPID|RRAPID]]
- Bloods - FBC, U&E, bone profile, uric acid, LDH
- ECG
- Urine pH

## Management

### Prevention

| **Low risk**                                      | **Intermediate risk**                                                                                 | **High risk**                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Solid tumours                                     | Early-stage Burkitt’s lymphoma, with LDH < 2 times the upper limit of normal                          | Certain high-grade non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma (e.g. diffuse large B cell lymphoma)              |
| Chronic leukaemias                                | Early-stage lymphoblastic lymphoma with LDH < 2 times the upper limit of normal                       | ALL with WCC > 100 x 109/L OR WCC < 100 x 109/L and LDH > 2 times the upper limit of normal |
| [[Pearls/Multiple myeloma\|Multiple myeloma]]     | ALL with WCC < 100 x 109/L and LDH < 2 times the upper limit of normal                                | AML with WCC > 100 x 109/L                                                                  |
| [[Pearls/Hodgkin's Lymphoma\|Hodgkin's Lymphoma]] | AML with WCC 25 x 109/L – 100 x 109/L OR WCC < 25 x 109/L and LDH > 2 times the upper limit of normal |                                                                                             |
| Low proliferating non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma          | CLL with WCC >50 x 109/L and/or treated with fludarabine or targeted agents                           |                                                                                             |

#### Low risk

- Regular monitoring of **blood tests** (U&Es, bone profile, uric acid, LDH)
- Monitor **fluid balance**
- Consider **allopurinol** if hyperuricaemia is present before starting chemotherapy treatment (300mg for 7 days, to start 2 days before chemotherapy treatment)

#### Intermediate risk

- Regular monitoring of **blood tests** (U&Es, bone profile, uric acid, LDH), including 1-2 times daily for the first three days of treatment and daily after that
- **Intravenous hydration** with normal saline for two days before treatment (aim to maintain urine output 100 mL/m²/hour)
- **Allopurinol** should be given to patients with hyperuricemia; if allopurinol does not reduce serum uric acid, consider rasburicase

#### High risk

- Regular monitoring of **blood tests** (U&Es, bone profile, uric acid, LDH), including 3-4 times daily after starting treatment
- **Intravenous hydration** with normal saline for two days before treatment (aim to maintain urine output 100 mL/m²/hour)
- **Rasburicase** should be given to patients with hyperuricaemia

### General Management 

- Intravenous fluids: aiming to maintain urine output >100 mL/m²/hour (monitor urine output hourly)
- Observations should be monitored at least 4-6 hourly, including fluid balance assessment
- Daily weights
- Blood tests every 6 hours after diagnosis of TLS
- ECG at baseline, consider cardiac monitor if hyperkalaemia/hypocalcaemia


#### Electrolyte Management

- **Hyperuricaemia**: intravenous rasburicase for 3-7 days (use maximum dose allopurinol if rasburicase contraindicated)
- **Hyperkalaemia**: calcium gluconate for cardiac protection, followed by a glucose/insulin infusion
- **Hyperphosphataemia**: phosphate-binding agents can be considered but are rarely used
- **Hypocalcaemia**: symptomatic hypocalcaemia should be treated with **IV calcium gluconate**; seizures can be managed with anticonvulsant medication according to local guidelines
## Complications/red Flags

- [[Full/Acute Kidney Injury|Acute Kidney Injury]]: due to calcium phosphate deposition and uric acid
- Cardiac arrhythmias: due to hyperkalaemia and/or hypocalcaemia
- [[Pearls/Seizure symptoms per region|Seizures]]: due to hypocalcaemia and/or hyperphosphataemia
- Lactic acidosis: due to chemotherapy-induced cell death and AKI
