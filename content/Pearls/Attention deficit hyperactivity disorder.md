> [!success] Triad of Symptoms
> - Hyperactivity
> - Inattention
> - Impulsivity


Features that are persistent. Has to be an element of developmental delay. Cannot diagnose with severe learning disability. 
- For children $\leq$ 16, six of the features have to be present
- For those 17+ the five of the features must be present

|Inattention|Hyperactivity/Impulsivity|
|---|---|
|Does not follow through on instructions|Unable to play quietly|
|Reluctant to engage in mentally-intense tasks|Talks excessively|
|Easily distracted|Does not wait their turn easily|
|Finds it difficult to sustain tasks|Will spontaneously leave their seat when expected to sit|
|Finds it difficult to organise tasks or activities|Is often 'on the go'|
|Often forgetful in daily activities|Often interruptive or intrusive to others|
|Often loses things necessary for tasks or activities|Will answer prematurely, before a question has been finished|
|Often does not seem to listen when spoken to directly|WIll run and climb in situations where it is not appropriate|


> [!warning] Pseudo-ADHD
> Some drugs (especially asthma) can mimic the symptoms of ADHD in children with no prior history:
> - Montelukast
> - Salbutamol
> - Steroids


### Management

- Omega-3-fatty acid diet
- Information/family support
- School intervention
- Cognitive approaches (CBT)
- Behaviour management

**Drug therapy should be a last resort** and is only available to those aged 5 and up. Patients with mild/moderate symptoms can usually benefit from parents attending education and training programs 

All of the following drugs are potentially cardiotoxic. Need to perform a baseline ECG before starting treatment and refer to cardiology if any significant PMH or FH. 
- Methylphenidate (ritalin) first line 6 week trial. 
	- Weight and height should be monitored every 6 months - reduced appetite in 60%
	- MR for school aged children. Want it to wear off before sleep.
- If inadequate response switch to atomoxetine/lisdexamfetamine then dexamfetamine. Better side effect profile
