Non-specific clinical presentation in infants - off feeding + temperature. Have a low threshold for suspecting it. 

Viral - more common
* Enteroviruses 50%
* HSV, [[Pearls/Influenza|Influenza]], EBV, adenovirus, CMV

Bacterial:
* Neonates - *Group B Strep, E. coli, Listeria monocytogenes*
* >1month-5 years - *Strep pneumoniae, Neisseria meningitidis*

*Medical emergency - urgent dose of IM benzylpenicilin in GP*


## Investigations

Rely less on clinical signs in young infants. Often full septic screen: 
- Blood cultures
- Urine sample
- CXR
- LP
- Viral nose swabs 

**Contraindication to lumbar puncture (any signs of raised ICP)**  
- focal neurological signs
- papilloedema
- disseminated intravascular coagulation
- signs of cerebral herniation
- clinically shocked
- significant bulging of the fontanelle

For patients with [[Pearls/Meningococcal septicaemia]] a lumbar puncture is ***contraindicated*** - blood cultures and PCR for meningococcus should be obtained.

## Management

#### 1. Antibiotics
- < 3 months: IV amoxicillin (or ampicillin) + IV cefotaxime
- > 3 months: IV cefotaxime (or ceftriaxone) (or IV ben pen + gent)

#### 2. Steroids
- **Do not give** in children <3 months 
- dexamethasone should be considered if the lumbar puncture reveals any of the following (anything that confirms a bacterial cause):
	- frankly purulent CSF 
	- CSF white blood cell count greater than 1000/microlitre
	- raised CSF white blood cell count with protein concentration greater than 1 g/litre
	- bacteria on Gram stain

#### 3. Fluids
- Treat any shock 

#### 4. Cerebral Monitoring
- EEG? 
- mechanical ventilation if respiratory impairment

#### 5. Contact tracing
- If within 7 days oral ciprofloxacin is now preferred over rifampicin
