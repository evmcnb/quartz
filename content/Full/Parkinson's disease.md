- Lack of dopamine
- Symptoms begin when 80% of dopamine producing cells (substantia nigra) have died

**Symptoms are characteristically symmetrical**


**Parkinsonism** is anything that brings about these symptoms.
**Parkinson's** is the progressive disease - most common cause of Parkinsonism


## Causes/Factors

- FH
- Male
- Head injury
- Pesticide exposure (maybe)

- Drug induced (anti-psychotic drugs) - reduce dopamine e.g. risperidone, haloperidol, [[Pearls/Metoclopramide|Metoclopramide]] promethazine, prochlorperazine

**Decrease risk**
- Smoking
- Coffee 
- NSAIDs
- Estrogen replacement

## Symptoms


- [[Full/Depression]]
- Parkinson's Dementia (Lewy Body)
- Micrographia
- Sleep disorders - REM sleep disturbance, presents up to 30 years before Parkinson's 
- Postural hypotension
- Urinary problems

- Loss of smell
- Hypersalivation

## Signs

**TRAP features**

- Tremor - pill rolling tremor - resting 3-5 Hz. **Improves with voluntary movement**
- Rigidity - cogwheel or lead pipe
- Akinesia/bradykinesia - reduced blink, face expression, soft voice, shuffling steps, reduced arm swing. Trouble getting out of chair
- Postural changes (imbalance, falls)

## Diagnostic Tests

Clinical diagnosis so just need to rule out other causes
- MRI
- Blood tests
- No specific medical test
- Referral to movement clinical before medicating 

If unsure specialist can do single photon emission computed tomography (SPECT).

## Management

Should only be diagnosed and managed by a specialist. **Urgent referral to neurology**

If too much dopamine is replaced, can cause frontal changes in patients. Need to be given at a specific time of day every day. Sudden withdrawal can cause life threatening symptoms.

For first-line treatment:  
- if the motor symptoms are affecting the patient's quality of life: levodopa - precursor of dopamine that passes through the BBB
- if the motor symptoms are not affecting the patient's quality of life: dopamine agonist (non-ergot derived), levodopa or monoamine oxidase B (MAO-B) inhibitor


|                                | Levodopa                                       | Dopamine agonists                                                                            | MAO-B inhibitors                               |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Motor symptoms**             | More improvement in motor symptoms             | Less improvement in motor symptoms                                                           | Less improvement in motor symptoms             |
| **Activities of daily living** | More improvement in activities of daily living | Less improvement in activities of daily living. Risk of inhibition disorders (impulsiveness) | Less improvement in activities of daily living |
| **Motor complications**        | More motor complications                       | Fewer motor complications                                                                    | Fewer motor complications                      |
| **Adverse events**             | Fewer specified adverse events*                | More specified adverse events*                                                               | Fewer specified adverse events*                |

If the first line treatment doesn't work, an additional medication can be added: 
- Dopamine agonists
- MAO-B inhibitors
- COMT inhibitors
- Amantadine

### Levadopa 

Nearly always combined with a decarboxylase inhibitor (e.g. carbidopa or benserazide). This prevents the peripheral metabolism of levodopa to dopamine outside of the brain and hence can reduce side effects:
- nausea and vomiting (very common)
- dyskinesias
- palpitations
- postural hypotension
- psychosis
- dry mouth
- anorexia

#### Difficulties in steady dose

Due to the difficulty in achieving a steady dose of levadopa there are several adverse side effects that can present: 
- end-of-dose wearing off - symptoms often worsen towards the end of dosage interval. This results in a decline of motor activity
- on-off phenomenon - large variations in motor performance between the on and off period
- dyskinesias at peak dose - dystonia, chorea and athetosis (involuntary writhing movements)
	- these effects may worsen over time with - clinicians therefore may limit doses until necessary


Deep brain stimulation - electrodes placed in important areas of the brain and pulses are generated from a device under the skin in chest 
## Complications/red Flags
The resting tremor may take longer than the other symptoms to improve after medicating
