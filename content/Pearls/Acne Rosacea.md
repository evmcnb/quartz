Unknown cause. Triggers include: 
- alcohol 
- sunlight 

![[z_attachments/Pasted image 20261009171003.png|512]]

Features & progression: 
- Flushing is first symptom
- Typically in the nose, cheeks and forehead
- telangiectasia are common
- later develops into persistent erythema with papules and pustules

#### Management

1. Daily application of high factor suncream
2. For flushing: PRN brimonidine gel (topical alpha-adrenergic agonist). it typically reduces redness within 30 minutes, reaching peak action at 3-6 hours, after which the redness returns to the baseline

If papules/pustules
- Mild-to-moderate: topical ivermectin
- Moderate-to-severe: combination of topical ivermectin + oral doxycycline
