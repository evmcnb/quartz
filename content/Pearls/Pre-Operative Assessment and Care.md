All types of anaesthetisa alter normal body physiology to some degree. This may cause several common effects:
- A - airway obstruction
- B - hypoventilation & hypoxia
- C - Hypotension
- D - PONV
- E - heat loss and hypothermia (esp in low body weight individuals)
- G - loss of airway reflexes and reflux risk
   

> [!success] TL;DR OSCE Mark Scheme for Pre-Op Assessment
> - Anaesthesia History - previous GAs, presence of any complications
> - PMH - RA, exclude heart & lung disease, hypertension, diabetes, anaemia, epilepsy
> - Specific implications - connective tissue/rheumatology disorders (SLE, RA, AS)
> - Cardiorespiratory - dyspnoea, exercise tolerance, cough, wheeze, chest pain, orthopnoea, PND, ankle swelling
> - Gastro - reflux, heartburn
> - Airway assessment - just history, cervical spine, jaw, dentition
> - DH - current medications & previous (recent steroids), reactions and sensitivities
> - SH & FH - smoking, alcohol, problems with anaesthesia
> - Preoperative investigations - Blood tests (FBC, U&Es), ECG.

### 1. Identify

- Anaesthetic history
	- Previous? What was it? GA/spinal/epidural, regional. For what surgery?
	- Any issues? PONV, difficult airway, ICU/HDU stay
	- Any family history of problems with anaesthetics? 
		- *Suxamethonium apnoea*, takes ages to break down. 
		- *Malignant hyperthermia*, autosomal dominant, anaesthetic emergency. Caused by suxamethonium and inhaled agents (sevoflurane, isoflurane, desflurane)
- Presenting complaint
	- If trauma - mechanism. Head/neck injury (manipulated a lot during airway management)? Other injury
	- Cardiovascular component (LOC without warning)
	- Acute risks - vomiting
- PMH
	- Cardio - angina, previous MI, HF, hypertension
	- Resp - asthma, COPD, current/recent resp infections
	- Exercise tolerance - flights of stairs, walking distance
	- Others - diabetes, epilepsy, TIA/stroke, renal disease, liver disease
- DH inc. allergies
	- Regular medication - when did they last take them
	- Anti-coagulations
	- Anti-hypertensives, beta blockers, time critical medications (epilepsy, PD)
	- **ALLERGIES** - can't ask a patient when they're asleep
- SH - smoking, alcohol, illicit
	- Smoking ususal, do they want nicotine replacement
	- Alcohol affects metabolism - quantify in units
	- Illicit substances can affect heart
	- Occupation - side effects
- Fasting & reflux
	- 2 hours - clear fluids (not fizzy or with sediment)
	- 4 hours - breastmilk
	- 6 hours - solid food (simple)
	- Tablets - take with a small sip of water at any time
	- Paediatrics - fluids up to 1 hour before to prevent dehydration
	- Trauma/pain will delay gastric emptying
	- Reflux - judge vomiting risk 
- Examination - dental/airway
	- Generaly appearance
	- Brief cardio/resp exam
	- Airway/dental 
	- Obs - HR, BP , RR, SpO2, Temp, Cap glucose if diabetc
	- **Weight & height** 

#### Airway assessment

Can I BVM easily? Can I intubate? 
- General inspection - jaw, beards, missing teeth
- Mallampati grade
- Mouth opening - can they physically open their jaw wide
- Dental - loose teeth / caps
- Neck movement - need neck extension for airway device placement

#### ASA Grade

The American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) grading system classifies the physical status of the patient for anaesthesia. Patients are given a grade to describe their current fitness prior to undergoing anaesthesia/surgery:

- **_ASA I_** – normal healthy patient
- **_ASA II_** – mild systemic disease without substantive functional limitations 
- **_ASA III_** – severe systemic disease with substantive functional limitations
- **_ASA IV_** – severe systemic disease that constantly threatens life
- **_ASA V_** – “moribund” and expected to die without the operation
- **_ASA VI_** – declared brain-dead and undergoing an organ donation operation

#### Investigations

Targeted investigations are important - only tests that we can do something about
- Pregnancy test - for all women of childbearing age 
- Sickle cell testing - only if FH
- CXR very rarely required
- Cardio sypmtoms require vurther tests - ECG at least, Echo, angio
- Acute patients require a more thorough work up

### 2. Optimise

**Smoking** - risk of hypoxia and reduced oxygen carriage, laryngospasm, poor wound healing

**Weight loss** - difficult airway management (desat quicker), poor wound healing, high VTE risk

**Exercise** - esp around major cancer surgery, reduce cardiovascular risk

**BP control** - linked to adverse cardiovascular events 

Alcohol intake reduction, dental fiation/extraction


#### Medicines Management

**Essential medications to continue:**
- Anti-epileptics
- Parkinson medications (time critical)
- Steroids if >5mg/day - **high risk of adrenal crisis** if stopped 
- If patient cannot tolerate oral route seek senior/pharmacy support

**Less essential but important**
- Beta blockers
- Aspirin for IHD
- PPI for GORD

**Anti-coagulations**
Each trust have their own guidelines. Important for surgery and anaesthesia.
**Generally:**
- Aspirin - continue
- Clopidogrel - stop 7 days prior. If stroke/MI in past year seek expert advice
- DOACs - stop 24-72 hours prior to surgery depending on surgery risk
- Warfarin - stop 5 days prior. If high risk of thrombosis - bridge with LMWH
- LMWH - last dose 12 hours prior (24 hours if on treatment dose for VTE)

If these timings cannot be achieved due to urgent surgery seek haem advice. 

**Diabetes management**

Type 1 **always** get their long acting insulin. Give 80% of dose day before & during reduced oral intake.
If missing >1 meal $\rightarrow$ variable rate insulin infusion

Type 2 omit agents with potential for hypos (sulphonyurea & SGLT2i) on the morning before surgery

Generally:
- First on the list, restart normal medication when diet resumed
- If acutely unwell or poorly managed diabetes - VRII with dexsaline + potassium alongside
- Frequent cap glucoses 
- Always prescribe PRN hypoglycaemia treatment

**Steroids**

Patients at risk of adrenal insufficiency may require supplemental steroids during times of physiological stress.

For patients on long-term corticosteroids:
- Convert to IV hydrocortisone at induction for moderate to major surgical procedures 
- Fludrocortisone supplementation if there is associated hypotension or the patient is on a long-term corticosteroid that does not  have a significant mineralocorticoid activity. 
- Post-operative steroid replacement:
    - For minor procedures: Usual oral corticosteroids may be resumed immediately post-operatively if the patient is clinically stable and tolerating oral intake.
    - For major surgery: Continue IV hydrocortisone (e.g. 50 mg every 6 hours or equivalent continuous infusion) for up to 72 hours post-operatively, with transition back to oral steroids as the patient recovers.
- Ensure appropriate tapering of steroids after the period of stress has resolved, returning to the patient’s maintenance dose.

**ACEi & ARBs**

ACE inhibitors and angiotensin-II receptor antagonists can be associated with severe hypotension after induction of anaesthesia; these drugs should be discontinued 24 hours before surgery.

**Others**

*SEMLA 54321*  
- Smoking: 5 weeks (at least, earlier the better)  
- Estrogen pills: 4 weeks  
- MAO inhibitors: 3 weeks  
- Lithium: 2 weeks  
- Aspirin: 1 week


**Anticipated of haemorrhage**

Need a group and save for all procedures except minor. 
- Group & save/antibody screen - identification of blood type/presence of atypical antibodies
- Crossmatch - allocation of compatible units of blood to a single patient. Need 2 samples per patient to reduced transfusion error 

**VTE prophylaxis**

Surgical patients at increased risk (immobility, inflammation, dehydration, cancer, other patient factors)
- Risk assessment on admission 
- Most prescribed a LMWH **at least 4 hours after surgery** - important to check with spinal/epidural performed
- Patients may need longer duration of LMWH if cancer surgery or lower limb orthopaedics 

### 3. Consent

As long as anaesthesia is for a surgical procedure with signed consent, verbal consent is acceptable. May include HDU/ICU post op stay.
