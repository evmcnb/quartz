Non-cancerous growth of squamous epithelium that is trapped within the base of the skull causing local destruction. Most common between 10-20 years old. 

**Being born with a cleft palate increases the risk of cholesteatoma around 100 fold.**

- foul-smelling, non-resolving discharge
- hearing loss

Other features are determined by local invasion:  
- vertigo
- facial nerve palsy
- cerebellopontine angle syndrome

  
Otoscopy  
- 'attic crust' - seen in the uppermost part of the ear drum

Management  
- patients are referred to ENT for consideration of surgical removal

Can lead to conductive hearing loss due to erosion of middle ear structures or Eustachian tube obstruction if untreated.
