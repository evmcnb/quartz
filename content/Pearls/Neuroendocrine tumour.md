# Neuroendocrine Tumours (NETs)

## Overview

- **Definition:** Rare neoplasms arising from cells of the neuroendocrine system, which share traits of both nerve cells and hormone-producing endocrine cells.
- **Common locations:** Gastrointestinal (GI) tract (most common), pancreas, and lungs.
- **Classification:**
    - **Functioning:** Actively secrete hormones, leading to specific clinical syndromes.
    - **Non-functioning:** Do not secrete active hormones. Often diagnosed late due to _mass effect_ (pressing on surrounding organs) or found incidentally on imaging.

---

## Key Types & Clinical Syndromes

### Carcinoid Tumours (primarily GI or lung)

- Can lead to **Carcinoid Syndrome**.
- **Important:** For GI carcinoids, this syndrome typically only occurs once the tumour has metastasised to the liver — this allows serotonin to bypass hepatic first-pass metabolism and reach the systemic circulation.
- **Key hormone:** Excess serotonin.
- **Classic symptoms:**
    - Cutaneous flushing
    - Secretory diarrhoea
    - Bronchospasm (wheezing)
    - Right-sided valvular heart disease (endocardial fibrosis → tricuspid regurgitation, pulmonary stenosis)

### Pancreatic NETs (pNETs)

|Tumour|Hormone|Clinical features|
|---|---|---|
|**Insulinoma**|Insulin|Fasting hypoglycaemia (sweating, confusion, palpitations); Whipple's triad|
|**Gastrinoma** (Zollinger-Ellison syndrome)|Gastrin|Severe, multiple, or refractory peptic ulcers; diarrhoea|
|**VIPoma**|Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide|**WDHA** syndrome — Watery Diarrhoea, Hypokalaemia, Achlorhydria|
|**Glucagonoma**|Glucagon|Mild diabetes, _necrolytic migratory erythema_ (classic blistering rash), weight loss, ↑ DVT risk|

> [!tldr] Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia (MEN) Syndromes
> 
> A subset of NETs occur as part of inherited **autosomal dominant** tumour syndromes. Always consider MEN in young patients, those with multifocal disease, or a positive family history.
> 
> ### MEN1 (Wermer syndrome)
> 
> - **Gene:** _MEN1_ tumour suppressor (menin protein) on chromosome **11q13**.
> - **Classic "3 Ps":**
>     - **Parathyroid** hyperplasia → primary hyperparathyroidism (most common, ~95%, usually first manifestation)
>     - **Pancreatic** NETs → **gastrinoma** is most common; also insulinoma
>     - **Pituitary** adenomas → prolactinoma most common
> - Also associated with foregut carcinoids and adrenocortical tumours.
> 
> ### MEN2 (_RET_ proto-oncogene mutation, chromosome **10q11**)
> 
> ||**MEN2A (Sipple syndrome)**|**MEN2B**|
> |---|---|---|
> |Medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)|✅ ~100%|✅ ~100% (earlier, more aggressive)|
> |Phaeochromocytoma|✅ ~50%|✅ ~50%|
> |Primary hyperparathyroidism|✅ ~20–30%|❌|
> |Mucosal neuromas / marfanoid habitus|❌|✅|
> 
> ### Clinical pearls
> 
> - **MTC is virtually 100% penetrant in MEN2** → prophylactic thyroidectomy is recommended in childhood for _RET_ mutation carriers.
> - Always **screen for and treat phaeochromocytoma first** before any other surgery (including thyroidectomy or parathyroidectomy) to avoid hypertensive crisis.
> - First-degree relatives of affected patients should be offered genetic testing.

## Diagnosis

### Tumour markers (blood/urine)

- **Chromogranin A (CgA):** General serum marker elevated in most NETs (sensitive but not specific).
- **24-hour urinary 5-HIAA:** Measures a breakdown product of serotonin — highly specific for diagnosing carcinoid syndrome.
- **Hormone-specific assays** depending on suspected syndrome (e.g. fasting insulin/C-peptide, gastrin, VIP, glucagon).

### Histology/pathology (biopsy)

- Tissue typically stains positive for **synaptophysin** and **chromogranin**.
- Tumours are graded **G1–G3** based on the **Ki-67 proliferation index** — a higher percentage indicates faster cell division and a more aggressive tumour.

### Imaging

- **CT/MRI** of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis for localisation and staging.
- **Functional imaging:**
    - **Gallium-68 DOTATATE PET/CT** — highly sensitive, current gold standard.
    - **Somatostatin receptor scintigraphy (Octreoscan)** — older alternative.
    - Both rely on the fact that most NETs express somatostatin receptors.

## Management Principles

- **Surgery:** The primary and only _potentially curative_ treatment for localised disease.
- **Symptom control & tumour suppression:** **Somatostatin analogues** (e.g. octreotide, lanreotide) — bind to somatostatin receptors to halt excess hormone release and can also slow tumour growth.
- **Advanced/metastatic disease:**
    - **PRRT (Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy):** Delivers targeted radiation directly to cells expressing somatostatin receptors (e.g. Lutetium-177 DOTATATE).
    - **Liver-directed therapies:** Hepatic artery embolisation or ablation, as NETs frequently metastasise to the liver.
    - **Targeted systemic therapies:** e.g. everolimus (mTOR inhibitor), sunitinib (TKI).
    - **Chemotherapy:** Typically reserved for high-grade, poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinomas.
