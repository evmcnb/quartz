* **Definition:** Rare neoplasms arising from cells of the neuroendocrine system, which share traits of both nerve cells and hormone-producing endocrine cells.
* **Common Locations:** Gastrointestinal (GI) tract (most common), pancreas, and lungs.
* **Classification:** **Functioning:** Actively secrete hormones, leading to specific clinical syndromes.
    * **Non-functioning:** Do not secrete active hormones. Often diagnosed late due to "mass effect" (pressing on surrounding organs) or found incidentally.

## Key Types & Clinical Syndromes

### Carcinoid Tumours (Primarily GI or Lung)
* Can lead to **Carcinoid Syndrome**. 
* *Note:* For GI carcinoids, this syndrome typically only occurs if the tumour has metastasized to the liver (allowing serotonin to bypass hepatic metabolism and enter systemic circulation).
* **Key Hormone:** Excess Serotonin.
* **Classic Symptoms:** 
	* Cutaneous flushing
    * Secretory diarrhea
    * Bronchospasm (wheezing)
    * Right-sided heart valvular lesions (fibrosis, tricuspid regurg)

### Pancreatic NETs (pNETs)
* **Insulinoma:** Secretes insulin. *Symptoms:* Fasting hypoglycemia (sweating, confusion, palpitations).
* **Gastrinoma (Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome):** Secretes gastrin. *Symptoms:* Severe, multiple, or refractory peptic ulcers and diarrhea.
* **VIPoma:** Secretes Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide (VIP). *Symptoms:* WDHA syndrome (Watery Diarrhea, Hypokalemia, Achlorhydria).
* **Glucagonoma:** Secretes glucagon. *Symptoms:* Mild diabetes, necrolytic migratory erythema (a classic blistering rash), weight loss, DVT risk.

## Diagnosis
* **Tumour Markers (Blood/Urine):**
    * **Chromogranin A (CgA):** A general serum marker elevated in most NETs.
    * **24-hour urine 5-HIAA:** Measures a breakdown product of serotonin (highly specific for diagnosing carcinoid syndrome).
* **Histology/Pathology (Biopsy):**
    * Tissue typically stains positive for **Synaptophysin** and **Chromogranin**.
    * Tumours are graded (G1 to G3) based on the **Ki-67 proliferation index** (a higher percentage means faster cell division and a more aggressive tumour).
* **Imaging:**
    * CT/MRI of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis for localization and staging.
    * **Functional Imaging:** Gallium-68 DOTATATE PET/CT (highly sensitive) or Somatostatin receptor scintigraphy (Octreoscan). These scans light up tumours that express somatostatin receptors, which most NETs do.

## Management Principles
* **Surgery:** The primary and only potentially curative treatment for localized disease.
* **Symptom Control & Tumour Suppression:** **Somatostatin analogues** (e.g., Octreotide, Lanreotide). These bind to somatostatin receptors to halt excess hormone release and can also slow tumour growth.
* **Advanced/Metastatic Disease Interventions:**
    * **PRRT (Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy):** Delivers targeted radiation directly to cells expressing somatostatin receptors.
    * **Liver-directed therapies:** Hepatic artery embolization or ablation, as NETs frequently metastasize to the liver.
    * **Targeted systemic therapies:** e.g., Everolimus, Sunitinib.
    * **Chemotherapy:** Typically reserved for high-grade, poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinomas.
