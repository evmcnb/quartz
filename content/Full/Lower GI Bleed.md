Lower GI Bleed bleeding from jejunum to the anus. Fresh blood per rectum (haematochezia)

## Signs & Symptoms

As per the other diseases

## Investigations

**Coloscopy** is the diagnostic and therapeutic modality of choice for suspected lower GI bleeding. - OGD if no lower GI bleeding source is identified, looking for an upper GI bleed causing rectal bleeding.

**Bedside tests**
- PR examination to confirm bleeding and may reveal an obvious cause e.g. haemorrhoids, anal malignancy
- ECG – arrhythmias, AF risk factor for ischaemic colitis
- Blood gas – rapidly check Haemoglobin, lactate may be significantly raised in ischaemic colitis
- Stool cultures, viral PCR, C. Diff testing
- Faecal calprotectin – suspected IBD

Blood tests

- FBC for anaemia
- CRP for inflammation
- Coagulation screen – bleeding diathesis
- Group and save, cross match
- U&Es, LFTs, bone profile
## Management

A-E approach for significant bleeds, major haemorrhage call may be required

**Resuscitation** 
- Fluid bolus
- Transfuse RBC is Hb < 70, targeting 70-100
- Transfuse platelets if below 50

Medical management – indicated for some causes for lower GI bleeding, abx for infective colitis, immunomodulators for IBD

Patients with small volume bleeding who are well and haemodynamically stable, may be observed and scared with safety netting advice and follow up investigations if bleeding self terminates

Patients with significant or continuous bleeding should be admitted for further investigation and treatment
