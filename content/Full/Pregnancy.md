Pre-existing conditions and non-obstetric disease cause more maternal deaths in the UK than obstetric complications

Pregnant women should receive the same investigations and treatments as non-pregnant patients with avoidance of harm to the foetus wherever possible

### Risk

A high risk pregnancy involves factors increasing health risks for the parent or baby. All of the following increase risk: 
**Maternal health**
- Age: <17 or 40+
- BMI >30 - increased risk of [[Pearls/Gestational Diabetes|Gestational Diabetes]], [[Full/Pre-eclampsia|Pre-eclampsia]] and [[Pearls/VTE in Pregnancy|VTE in Pregnancy]]
- Chronic conditions: [[Full/Essential hypertension|hypertension]], [[Full/Diabetes Mellitus|Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Full/Chronic Kidney Disease|Chronic Kidney Disease]], [[Full/Coronary Artery Disease|Ischemic Heart Disease]], thyroid issues, autoimmune diseases, [[Pearls/Epilepsy|Epilepsy]], [[Full/HIV|HIV]], cancer. 
- Smoking, vaping, alcohol, recreational drugs, caffeine
- Previous abdominal surgery - adhesions making access to uterus more difficult 
- Previous [[Full/Deep Vein Thrombosis|Deep Vein Thrombosis]] or a strong family history - especially if oestrogen induced
- Absence of prenatal care
- Multiple gestation
- 3 consecutive miscarriages 
- High parity and low interpregnancy interval - [[Pearls/Uterine rupture|Uterine rupture]] and [[Pearls/Postpartum haemorrhage|Postpartum haemorrhage]]
- Post-date gestation/pre-term delivery
- [[Pearls/Placenta praevia|Placenta praevia]]
- IUGR
- Augmentation of labour
- [[Full/Pre-eclampsia|Pre-eclampsia]]

May need extra counselling, investigations, scans and reviews. 
## Radiology

If the uterus is positioned outside the imagining field of view, the radiation dose to the foetus is minimal.
Exposure from the following is well below the threshold of risk to the foetus:

- Plain radiograph: chest, extremities, spine
- CT: head, chest (but consider radiation to maternal breast in pregnancy

Ultrasound and MRI are generally preferred when imaging abdomen

## Chronic Conditions

### [[Pearls/Hypothyroid in pregnancy|Hypothyroid in pregnancy]]

Left untreated, it can lead to several adverse pregnancy outcomes - miscarriage, anaemia, small for GA and [[Full/Pre-eclampsia]].

The levothyroxine dose needs to be increased during pregnancy by at least 25-50mcg (30-50%). Titrated on TSH level aiming for low-normal TSH level. 

### Hypertension

Medications will likely need to be changed. As seen above the following should be stopped as they may cause congenital abnormalities:
- ACEi
- ARBs
- Thiazide and thiazide-like diuretics (e.g. indapamide)

Medications that are considered safe: 
- Labetalol (although other beta-blockers may have adverse effects)
- CCBs
- Alpha-blockers (e.g. doxazosin)

### Epilepsy

Women with epilepsy should take folic acid 5mg daily before conception to reduce the risk of neural tube defects.

Pregnancy may worsen seizure control due to the stress. Seizures are not known to be harmful to the pregnancy other than the risk of physical injury. 

Ideally should be controlled with a single agent before becoming pregnant:
- Levetiracetam, lamotrigine and carbamazepine are the safer anti-epiletic medication in pregnancy. 
- Sodium valproate is avoided - neural tube defects
- Phenytoin is avoided - cleft lip/palate

### Rheumatoid Arthritis

Ideally should be well controlled for at least 3 months before becoming pregnant. Often the symptoms will improve during pregnancy but may flare up after delivery. The medications may need to be altered by rheumatology:
- Methotrexate is contraindicated
- Hydroxychloroquine is safe and first-line choice
- Sulfasalazine is safe 
- Corticosteroids may be used during flare-ups
- NSAIDs are generally avoided unless really necessary
