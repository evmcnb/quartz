Defined as when cardiac output is inadequate for the body's normal requirements. End stage of all cardiac pathologies

## Classifications

***Need to differentiate between reduced and preserved ejection fraction types***

**Systolic failure** aka *HFrEF* - inability of the ventricle to contract normally resulting in $\downarrow$ CO. 
- Ejection fraction <40%
- Eccentric hypertrophy ("dilated cardiomyopathy")

**Diastolic failure** aka *HFpEF*- Inability of the ventricle to relax and fill normally $\uparrow$ filling pressure. 
- EF >50% - preserved ejection fraction
- Concentric hypertrophy


![[Excalidraw/Drawing 2025-08-11 19.44.33.excalidraw.png|500]]

**Acute heart failure** - new-onset acute or decompensation of chronic heart failure with pulmonary and peripheral oedema without peripheral hypoperfusion

**Chronic heart failure** - Develops slowly, venous congestion is common but arterial pressure is well maintained

**Low-output heart failure** - $\downarrow$ CO which fails to increase on exercise. Causes
- Excessive pre-load: mitral regurgitation or fluid overload
- Pump failure: systolic/diastolic heart failure, $\downarrow$ HR (eg $\beta$ blockers)
- Chronic excess afterload: [[Full/Aortic Stenosis]], [[Full/Essential hypertension|hypertension]]

**High-output heart failure** - rare, normal or increase CO with demand


> [!tldr] NYHA Classification
> 1. No limitation
> 2. Slight limitation
> 3. Marked limitation
> 4. Unable to carry out physical activity. Symptoms at rest


## Causes/Factors

Systolic and diastolic failure pathology often coexist

Systolic:

- Need to identified if its an ischemic aetiology ([[Full/Coronary Artery Disease]], [[Full/myocardial infarction]]) or non-ischemic (drugs, viruses, drinking, pregnancy, genetic disorders, chemotherapy, L to R shunts, [[Takotsubo cardiomyopathy]])
	- Patients with ischemic HF have a much higher risk for arrhythmias $\rightarrow$ lower threshold for ICD implantation

Diastolic:

- Long standing uncontrolled [[Full/Essential hypertension|hypertension]]
- Infiltrative diseases - [[Pearls/Amyloidosis]], [[Full/Sarcoidosis|Sarcoidosis]], [[Pearls/Scleroderma]]
- Restrictive cardiomyopathies
- Contributing diseases ([[Full/Obesity]], [[Full/Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Full/COPD]], [[Full/Chronic Kidney Disease]], [[Full/Obstructive Sleep Apnoea]])
## Symptoms

- Fatigue
- Dyspnoea
- Orthopnoea
- Paroxysmal nocturnal (breathless at night)

## Signs

- High JV pressure
- Tachycardia
- Hypotension
- Cachexia (loss of skeletal muscle)
- 3rd heart sound (dilated cardio)

## Diagnostic Tests

- Echo to assess ejection fraction, ventricle dilation and valves
- ECG
- Bloods: ***NT-proBNP*** (N-Terminal pro-Brain Natriuretic Peptide) *apparently just 'BNP' has different reference ranges*
- CXR - [[Full/Chest X-Ray Interpretation#Heart Failure|ABCDE of heart failure on CXR]]

## Management

Initial:
- Refer to cardiology and assessment with echo, [[Pearls/BNP|BNP]] 400-2000 $\rightarrow$ 6 week wait, **2000+** 2WW
- Drug treatment
- Lifestyle advice - annual flu vaccine, stop smoking, 


> [!success] 4 Key Pillars of Systolic Management
> ACEi and Beta blockers first line for HFrEF.
>- **A**CE/ARB inhibitor (e.g., **_ramipril_**, losartan) titrated as high as tolerated - removal of excess fluid (angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor (ARNI) if ACEi not tolerated, e.g. dry cough then ARB if ARNI not tolerated (angioedema)). Need appropriate eGFR and K+ levels 
>- **B**eta blocker (e.g., **_bisoprolol_** (good for reactive airways), metoprolol succinate, carvedilol ($\alpha$ & $\beta$ effects)) titrated as high as tolerated - decreases heart rate and increases force of 
>-  **M**ineralcorticoid receptor antagonist (aldosterone antagonist) when symptoms are not controlled with ACE and $\beta$-blockers (e.g., **_spironolactone_** or **_eplerenone_**). Can also cause hyperkalaemia contraction 
>-  **S**GLT2i (causes peeing out glucose) eg forxiga, dapagliflozin. eGFR >20 to give. Critical for both systolic and diastolic failure
>
>If patients on the above combination of 4 medications continue to have symptoms **switch ACE-inhibitor for an angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor**


Loop diuretics no longer mainstay - (e.g., **_furosemide_** or **_bumetanide_**) - no **prognostic** benefit only symptomatic 

U&Es should be monitored as ACEi, diuretics and aldosterone antagonists can all cause electrolyte disturbances. ACEi and aldosterone antagonists can cause [[Pearls/Hyperkalaemia|Hyperkalaemia]]

**Other mortality benefits**:
- **IV Iron** replacement -  FAIR-HF trial if ferritin <100 or ferritin <300 & transferrin sat <20%
- ICD if <35% after 3 months therapy for ischemic, 6 for non-ischaemic
- Cardiac resynchronisation therapy if in a BBB - sync both chambers back to increase EF 


> [!warning] Diastolic Management
> No concrete management for the management - need to **treat the underlying disorder**
> - MRA
> - SGLT2i $\downarrow$ cardiovascular death 


### Acute management

- IV loop diuretics. Higher dose than which the person was admitted.
- Maintain oxygen targets
- Vasodilators - IV GTN isosorbide dinitrate (reduce preload and afterload). Should not be given in systolic < 100. Have a role if concomitant myocardial ischaemia, severe hypertension or regurgitant aortic or mitral valve disease

In acute heart failure with hypotension, inotropes (e.g. dobutamine) be considered for patients with severe left ventricular dysfunction who have potentially reversible cardiogenic shock

Surgical - heart transplant can be considered

CPAP with [[Pearls/Pulmonary oedema due to HF exacerbation|Pulmonary oedema due to HF exacerbation]]

Only stop beta blockers if HR <50, 2nd or 3rd degree AV block or shock. 
## Complications/red Flags

- Arrythmias
- Thromboembolism
