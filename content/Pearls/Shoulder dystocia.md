Complication of vaginal cephalic delivery. The **inability** to deliver the body of the fetus using gentle traction after the head is already delivered. 

![[z_attachments/Pasted image 20251230154946.png]]

Usually occurs due to the impaction of the anterior fetal shoulder on the maternal pubic symphysis. 

**Obstetric emergency** - cause of maternal and fetal morbidity

Key risk factors
- Fetal macrosomia (associated with maternal [[Full/Diabetes Mellitus|Diabetes Mellitus]])
- Prolonged labour 

### Management

Senior help including anaesthetics and paediatrics required

Definative management with **McRoberts' manoeuvre**:
1. Flexion and abduction of the maternal hips, bringing the mother's thighs towards her abdomen
2. This rotation increases the relative anterior-posterior angle of the pelvis and facilitates a successful delivery 

Next after this is suprapubic pressure - putting force on the posterior aspect of the baby's anterior shoulder to encourage it down and under the pubic symphysis

An episiotomy will not relieve the bony obstruction but is sometimes used to allow for better access for internal manoeuvres (not included here see zero to finals)

#### Complications 

- Postpartum haemorrhage
- Perineal tears
- Brachial plexus injury
- Neonatal death
