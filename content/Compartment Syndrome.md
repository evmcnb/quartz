Pressure within a fascial compartment is elevated cutting off blood flow to the contents of the compartment resulting in necrosis.

The two main fractures carrying this complication include supracondylar fractures and tibial shaft injuries.

- Pain out of proportion even on passive movement. Excessive use of PRN raises suspicion 
- Parasthesia
- Pallor


> [!danger] Pulse
> The presence of a pulse does not rule out compartment syndrome. Necrosis still may occur as a result of microvascular compromise

Diagnosis via intracompartmental pressure measurements. >20mmHg are abnormal >40 diagnostic 

No changes on XR so useless

**Treatment** with fasciotomies, if left progresses to amputation


> [!info] Chronic compartment syndrome
> Usually associated with exertion. During exertion, the pressure within the compartment rises, blood flow to the compartment is restricted, and symptoms start. During rest, the pressure falls, and symptoms begin to resolve. It is not an emergency.
