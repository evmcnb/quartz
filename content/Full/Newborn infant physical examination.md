## Introduction

- **Wash hands** (and don PPE if needed)
- **Introduce yourself** (name and role)
- Confirm infants's **name** and **DOB** - use it! 
- Explain what the examination involves to the parents - "Need to carry out a routine head to toe exam of your child"
- Gain consent from the parents to continue
- Adequately expose the child for assessment - ask the parent to undress down to  their nappy 

The optimal way to perform is from head to toe. In reality its an opportunisitic examination. If they're settled, listen to the heart. If they're crying look at the palate etc. 

#### Questions to ask the parents

**Unlikely to ask these in an OSCE but covered in a full assessment**

##### Maternal History
- Pregnancy details - date, type of delivery, complications, high-risk screening results
- Breech presentation - if breech at 36 weeks or delivery the baby will need an ultrasound for increased risk of development dysplasia of the hip 
- Risk factors for infection
- Abnormalities on scan
- Family history of hearing problems, heart problems, cataracts, kidney problems

##### Newborn history
- Feeding pattern
- Urination
- Passing of meconium
- Parental concerns 

## Weight

Ensure that the infants weight is recorded & check on weight chart its centile 

If a baby is small, you should also plot head circumference and length to determine whether this is symmetrical or asymmetrical (weight disproportionately low, head circumference preserved )

**Asymmetrical growth restriction** is commonly due to **placental** **insufficiency**, whilst **symmetrical** **growth restriction** is more commonly due to **fetal** **factors** such as genetic abnormalities or intrauterine infection.

## General Inspection

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Pallor</span> - underlying <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">anaemia</span>, <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">congestive cardiac failure</span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Cyanosis</span> -  poor circulation, <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">right-to-left shunt</span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Jaundice</span> - newborn <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">jaundice</span>. Can be physiological (appearing at 2-3 days and resolving by day 10), or by a wide range of pathologies (haemolytic disease, infection and [[[[Pearls/Gilbert Syndrome|Gilbert Syndrome]]]])

### Tone

**Observe** the newborn when they're picked up & the assessment should continue throughout the exam

**Assess tone** by gently moving the newborn's limbs passively 

## Head

##### Circumference

**Measure head circumference and record it in the notes.**

**Microcephaly** describes a head that is smaller than expected for age and sex. Microcephaly may be associated with reduced brain size or atrophy.

**Macrocephaly** describes a head that is larger than expected for age and sex. Macrocephaly may be normal but may also be associated with hydrocephalus, cranial vault abnormalities or genetic abnormalities.

##### Shape

**Inspect** the shape & the cranial sutures (note if they are closely applied, widely separated or normal)
- Cranial moulding - common after birth and usually resolves within a few days

#### Fontanelle

**Palpate** the anterior fontanelle 
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Flat</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">normal</span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Sunken</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">dehydration</span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Tense/bulging</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">raised intracranial pressure</span> (hydrocephalus)

## Skin

Very important to document any birthmarks or bruising/lacerations from birth trauma found in initial examination in-case of safe-guarding concerns in the future. There are body maps in the red book
- **Bruising/lacerations**
- **Vernix** - a waxy/cheese-like white substance coating the skin of the newborns (normal finding)
- **Birthmarks**
	- Salmon patch - red or pink patches often on an infant's eyelids, head or neck. Common and usually fade by the age of 2
	- Haemangiomas - blood vessels which form a raised lump on the skin that appears soon after birth. Typically get bigger over the first 6-12 months, then shrink and disappear by 7.
	- Port-wine stain (naevus flammeus) - red/purple marks on the face and neck which are typically present from birth and do not regress
	

## Face

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Appearance</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">dysmorphic features</span> (e.g. epicanthic folds in [[Pearls/Down's syndrome]])
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Asymmetry</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">facial nerve palsy</span> secondary to instrumental delivery
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Trauma</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">from labour</span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Assess patency of nasal passages</span>. Obligate nasal breathers so will present with <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">respiratory distress if they have bilateral choanal atresia </span>

## Eyes

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Inspect eyes and sclera</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">erythema, discharge, jaundice, haemorrhages</span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Inspect position and shape of the eyes</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">ptosis, epicanthic folds</span>

##### Fundal reflex (red reflex)

**Assess** for the **fundal** **reflex** in **each** **eye**:
1. Look through the ophthalmoscope, shining the light towards the patient’s eye at a distance of approximately one arm’s length.
2. Observe for a reddish/orange reflection in each pupil, caused by light reflecting back from the vascularised retina.

An absent fundal reflex or the presence of a white reflex requires **immediate ophthalmology referral** (congenital [[Full/Cataracts|Cataracts]], [[Full/Retinal detachment|Retinal detachment]], vitreous haemorrhage and [[Retinoblastoma]]

## Ears

<span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Inspect the pinna</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">asymmetry, skin tags or accessory auricles</span>. All infants should undergo a hearing screening test prior to discharge from hospital 


## Mouth and palate

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Inspect for clefts of hard or soft palate</span> - the full palate should be examined by visual inspection. Use a tongue depressor and a torch, and ask a parent to help keep the baby’s head still. You must visualise the whole palate, and see the central uvula to ensure it is intact. You cannot rely on palpation to exclude a cleft.
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Inspect tongue and gums</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">tongue-tie (ankyloglossia)</span>

## Neck and clavicles

<span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Inspect for length, webbing, lumps or clavicular fracture</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">shortened webbed neck in Turners syndrome.</span>

## Upper limbs

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Assess symmetry</span> - equal in size and length
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Inspect fingers & palms</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">polydactyly, number of palmar creases (2 normal)</span>

##### Palpate brachial pulse on each upper limb 

Any asymmetry may be suggestive of an underlying vascular abnormality 


## Chest

#### Inspection

##### Respiratory Rate
Normal rate of 40-60 per minute

##### Work of breathing

Assess for signs of increased work of breathing:
- Difficulty feeding
- Expiratory grunting
- Tracheal tug
- Supraclavicular recession
- Intercostal recession
- Nasal flaring
- Abdominal breathing
- Head bobbing

##### Other signs

- Pectus excavatum - sunken appearance of the chest
- Pectus carinatum - protrusion of the sternum & ribs
- Asymmetrical chest wall expansion - unilateral lung pathology

#### Auscultation

##### Lungs
Normal pattern
- Pay attention to inspiratory and expiratory sounds
- Note quality and volume of breath sounds
- Note additional sounds
- Repeat auscultation on posterior aspect

##### Heart
Auscultate upwards through the valve areas using the diaphragm & bell
- **Mitral valve:** 5th intercostal space – midclavicular line (apex beat)
- **Tricuspid valve:** 4th or 5th intercostal space – lower left sternal edge
- **Pulmonary valve:** 2nd intercostal space – left sternal edge
- **Aortic valve:** 2nd intercostal space – right sternal edge
- Other sounds: gallops/split S2, pericardial rubs

Normal heart rate of 120-150

See [[Pearls/Paediatric Murmurs|Paediatric Murmurs]]


###### Pulse Oximetry

If there's one in front of you. Don't if not

## Abdomen

#### Inspection

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Distension</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">bowel obstruction, organomegaly, ascites </span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Umbilicus</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">swelling, discharge, erythema </span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Inguinal hernia</span> - arrange paediatric surgical review

#### Palpation

- **Liver** - should be palpable no more than 2cm below the costal margin
- **Spleen** - may be palpable at left costal margin 
- **Kidneys** - only palpable using deep bimanual palpation
- **Bladder** - should not be palpable in healthy infants

## Genitalia

Note any ambiguity of the genitalia - typically associated with [[Pearls/Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia|Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia]] in girls

### Males

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Note the position of the urethral meatus </span>- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">abnormal in</span> [[Pearls/Hypospadias|Hypospadias]] <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">or epispadias</span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Note size of the penis </span>- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">at least 2cm</span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Assess for evidence of testicular swelling</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">hydrocele</span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Palpate the scrotum to ensure both testes are present  </span>- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">unilateral</span> [[Full/Cryptorchidism|Undescended testis]] <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">is common, bilateral consider disorder of sexual development<br></span>
### Females

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Inspect the labia</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">not fused</span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Inspect the clitoris</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">normal size</span>
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Any vaginal discharge</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">white is normal due to maternal oestrogens</span>


## Hips

#### Special tests

Each hip should be examined individually. All clothing should be removed. Basically just dislocating the hip and then relocating it and calling it a special test 

##### Barlow test

Used to screen for [[Pearls/Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip]] by attempting to dislocate it:
1. Stabilise the contralateral side of the pelvis. Place your thumb anteriorly over the symphysis pubis and other fingers over the sacrum
2. Adduct the hip whilst applying light pressure on the knee (directing force posteriorly)

If the hip is unstable the femoral head will slip over the posterior rim of the acetabulum producing a palpable sensation of dislocation


##### Ortolani test

The **Ortolani test** is then used to confirm a positive finding:
1. Stabilise the contralateral side of the pelviss
2. Flex the hip and knee of a supine infant to 90$\degree$ 
3. Apply anterior pressure to the femur whilst abducting the hip joint

A positive sign is the clunk ass the femoral head relocates

## Lower limbs

**Inspect:**
- Asymmetry
- Oedema - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">hypoalbuminaemia or congestive cardiac failure</span>
- Ankle deformities - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">talipes (club foot, common)</span>
- Missing digits

##### Femoral pulses
Palpate and compare femoral pulses - if possible. 

##### Tone & movement

- Decreased in infants with [[Pearls/Down's syndrome]]
- May be asymmetrically increased in upper motor neuron lesions (ischaemic stroke, intracranial haemorrhage)

## Back and spine

Inspect for:
- Scoliosis
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Hair tufts & sacral pits</span> - <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">neural tube defects (spina bifida)</span>
- Birthmarks
- Naevi

## Anus

**Inspect the anus for patency:** abnormal embryological development of the rectum can result in an imperforate anus.

**Meconium should be passed within 24 hours:** a delay is suggestive of obstruction or [[Full/Hirschsprung’s Disease|Hirschsprung’s Disease]]

## Reflexes 

Assess a selection of the following reflexes which should all be present in a healthy newborn. The absence of several reflexes may indicate an underlying neurological abnormality

##### Palmar grasp reflex

When an object is placed in the infant's hand and strokes their palm, the fingers will close

##### Sucking reflex

Causes the child to suck anything that touches the roof of their mouth. Most apparent during feeding

##### Rooting reflex

A newborn will turn its head towards anything that strokes its cheek or mouth. Present at birth to 4 months as it gradually comes under voluntary control.

##### Stepping reflex

When the soless of the infants feet touch a flat surface they will appear to walk by placing one foot in front of the other

##### Startle reflex

Support the infants upper bank with one hand and then drop back. Extension of legs and head whilst the arms jerk upwards and fingers extend. 

Asymmetry may be due to hemiparesis, brachial plexus injury or a fractured clavicle.

## To Complete the Exam

- **Explain** to the parents that the examination is now **finished**.
- Share results and explain any referrals required
- Check any further questions
- **Dispose of PPE** appropriately and **wash your hands**.
- **Document** your findings. NIPE is documented using a national online system which then needs to be printed and place in the baby's notes.
