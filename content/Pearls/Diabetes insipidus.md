#### Cranial (central) DI - ADH deficiency
Failure of ADH _production/secretion_ from the hypothalamus/posterior pituitary
- Causes: idiopathic (often autoimmune), tumours (craniopharyngioma, pituitary adenoma, mets), trauma/neurosurgery, infiltration (sarcoidosis, Langerhans cell histiocytosis), infection, and inherited forms. 
- **Responds to desmopressin.**

#### Nephrogenic DI - ADH resistance
Kidney is _resistant_ to ADH despite normal/high levels. 
- Causes: **lithium** (classic), hypercalcaemia, hypokalaemia, chronic kidney disease, and inherited forms (AVPR2 or aquaporin-2 mutations).
- **Does not respond to desmopressin.**


## Investigations

Confirm true polyuria (>3 L/day), exclude diabetes mellitus/hypercalcaemia/hypokalaemia. The **water deprivation test** followed by desmopressin distinguishes the types


## Management

- **Cranial:** desmopressin (oral, intranasal, or SC); treat the underlying cause
- **Nephrogenic:** remove the offending drug/correct electrolytes; low-salt/low-protein diet, **thiazide diuretics** (paradoxical antidiuresis), amiloride (especially lithium-induced), NSAIDs
- Ensure free access to water and monitor sodium carefully in all types
