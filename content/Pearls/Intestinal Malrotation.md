During the first trimester of pregnancy, the intestines normally grow so rapidly that they temporarily move into the umbilical cord. As they move back into the abdomen, they undergo a **270-degree counterclockwise rotation** around the superior mesenteric artery (SMA).

![[z_attachments/Pasted image 20260302162728.png]]

In malrotation this process is partially or entirely incomplete 

- 2x more common in boys
- 1/3 present within the first week
- 50% within the first month

Diagnosis with XR with contrast.

- Bilious vomiting
- Abdominal pain
- Distended abdo 
- Bloody stools

Management with surgery

### Complications

- **Midgut Volvulus:** This is a surgical emergency. Because the bowel isn't anchored, it can twist around its own blood supply (the SMA). This cuts off blood flow, leading to rapid tissue death (gangrene) if not corrected immediately.
- **Duodenal Obstruction:** The aforementioned Ladd Bands can compress the duodenum, preventing food and waste from passing through.
