### Breast vs Bottle

- Recommended to exclusively breastfeed for the first 6 months of life
- Followed by continued breastfeeding alongside appropriate complementary foods up to the age of 2

Both breast and bottle-fed babies can be overfed - but more common with bottle feeding

Breast feeding benefits: 
- Contains maternal antibodies that protect against infections
- Associated with fewer GI and respiratory infections
- Reduced risk of [[Pearls/Sudden Infant Death Syndrome]]
- Maternal reduced risk of breast and ovarian cancer 

### Feeding Volumes

Feeds are typically given **_every 2–3 hours_** initially, gradually spacing to **_every 4 hours_** or longer as the infant grows. Most babies eventually feed **_on demand_**.

Volumes are gradually increased in the first week of life as tolerated. For example:

- Day 1: 60 mL/kg/day
- Day 2: 90 mL/kg/day
- Day 3: 120 mL/kg/day
- Day 4 and onwards: 150 mL/kg/day

Preterm/underweight babies may require higher volumes

#### Initial weight loss

Its normal for babies to lose weight after birth: 
- Breastfed: up to 10% of birth weight
- Formula-fed: up to 5% of birth weight

Weight loss occurs by the 5th day of life and should regain their birth weight by around day 10-14. 

If they do not regain their weight within 2 weeks or weight loss exceeds these limits, hospital assessment in required. 

***The most reliable sign of dehydration in newborns in weight loss, even if they do not appear clinically dehydrated.***


### Weaning

The gradual transition from milk to food usually starting at 6 months (when the baby can sit upright and shows interest in food). 
- Starts with smooth pureed foods
- Weaning will progress towards a normal healthy diet
- Milk feeds are continued alongside food until at least 12 months

## Growth Charts

On a growth chart:
- **_Age_** is plotted on the **_x-axis_**
- **_Weight_**, **_height_** or **_head circumference_** on the **_y-axis_**
- Each measurement is represented by a **_dot_** on the chart
- The curves represent **_centile lines_**, showing the **_normal distribution_** of growth for healthy children

| **Phase**     | **Approximate Age**                | **Key Drivers**                                        |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Infancy**   | Birth → 2 years                    | Rapid growth driven mainly by nutrition                |
| **Childhood** | 2 years → puberty                  | Steady linear growth influenced by growth hormone      |
| **Pubertal**  | Puberty onset → end of adolescence | Rapid growth driven by sex hormones and growth hormone |

**_Faltering growth_** involves a slower than expected gain in weight in a child, defined in the NICE guidelines (2017) as: 
- Weight **_<2nd centile_** (regardless of birthweight)
- A fall across **_≥1 centile spaces_** if the birthweight was **_<9th centile_**
- A fall across **_≥2 centile spaces_** if the birthweight was **_9th-91st centile_**
- A fall across **_≥3 centile spaces_** if the birthweight was **_>91st centile_**

### Causes of faltering growth

Inadequate nutritional intake
- During breastfeeding
- Reduced availability of food
- Family/parental problems
- Neglect
- [[Pearls/Pyloric Stenosis|Pyloric Stenosis]]

Difficulty feeding
- Tongue tie
- Poor suck (e.g. [[cerebral palsy]])
- Cleft lip/palate
- Genetic conditions causing an abnormal facial structure


Malabsorption
- [[Full/Cystic Fibrosis|Cystic Fibrosis]]
- [[Full/Coeliac Disease|Coeliac Disease]]
- [[Cow's milk intolerance]]
- Chronic diarrhoea
- [[Full/inflammatory bowel disease|inflammatory bowel disease]]

Increased energy requirements
- [[Pearls/Hyperthyroidism|Hyperthyroidism]]
- Chronic disease/infection (CHD, CF, HIV)
- Malignancy

Inability to process nutrition 
- [[Pearls/Inborn errors of metabolism|Inborn errors of metabolism]]
- [[Full/Diabetes Mellitus|T1DM]]

#### Assessment

**History of**:
- Pregnancy, birth, developmental milestones
- Soocial history
- Feeding/eating history - breast or bottle fed? Feeding times? Volume and frequency? Any difficulties with feeding?

**Exam**:
- Observe feeding
- Assessing the physical and mental health of the mother
- Observing parent-child interactions
- Plotting height and weight on a growth chart
- Calculating the BMI centile (if older than 2)
- Calculating the mid-parental centile on a growth chart (the expected height centile for the child of these parents)

**Investigate for**:
- [[Full/Urinary Tract Infection|Urinary Tract Infection]] with [[Pearls/Urine Dipstick|Urine Dipstick]]
- [[Full/Coeliac Disease|Coeliac Disease]] (anti-TTG antibodies)
- Other focused investigations for an underlying diagnosis 

**Management**:

Inadequate nutrition is managed based on individual circumstances, and may involve:
- **_Regular structured mealtimes_** and **_snacks_**
- **_Reduced milk consumption_** to improve appetite for other foods
- **_Dietician input_**
- **_Energy-dense foods_** to boost calories
- **_Nutritional supplement drinks_**
