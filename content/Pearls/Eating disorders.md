Psychiatric conditions involving an unhealthy and distorted obsession with body image and food. Main types:
- Anorexia nervosa
- Bulimia nervosa
- Binge eating disorder

More common in young females

### Anorexia nervosa

The person feels overweight despite evidence of normal or low body weight. It involves **obsessively restricting calorie intake** to lose weight. Features include:
- Weight loss - using diet pills/laxatives to limit absorption
- Amenorrhoea - disruption of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal ais (no LH or FSH production)
- Lanugo hair - fine soft hair across most of the body
- Hypotension
- Hypothermia
- Mood changes

Has the **highest mortality** of any psychiatric condition. Up to 10% of patients will eventually die because of the disorder despite treatment. 

### Bulimia nervosa

Normal body weight that tends to fluctuate. Binge eating followed by purging by inducing vomiting or taking laxatives to prevent the calories from being absorbed. Features:
- Erosion of teeth
- Swollen salivary glands
- Mouth ulcers
- [[Full/Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease|GORD]]
- Calluses on the knuckles where they have been scraped across the teeth (Russell's sign)

### Binge eating disorder

Episdoes where the person excessively overeats, often as an expression of underlying psychological distress. The person typically feels a loss of control. Not a restrictive condition and the patients are likely to be overweight. 
- A planned binge involving "binge" foods
- Eating very quickly
- Unrelated to feelings of hunger
- Becoming uncomfortably full 
- Eating in a dazed state

Basically a mukbang 


## Management

Need to involve specialist services and MDT particularly with anorexia nervosa. Management in centred around changing behaviour and addressing environmental factors
- CBT-ED (eating disorder focused CBT)
- Family therapy in children 
- Possible trial of fluoxetine
