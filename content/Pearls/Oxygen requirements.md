If the patient is receiving **[[Pearls/Oxygen|Oxygen]] therapy** their **PaO2** should be approximately **10kPa less than** the **% inspired concentration FiO2** (so a patient on 40% [[Pearls/Oxygen|Oxygen]] would be expected to have a PaO2 of approximately 30kPa /225mmHg).

* Remember room air has an [[Pearls/Oxygen|Oxygen]] concentration of 21% already.
* FiO2 stands for fraction of inspired [[Pearls/Oxygen|Oxygen]] (O2).
* Every litre approximately increases the FiO2 by about 4 percent.

## Nasal canula rates:

1-6L/min of oxygen. 

## Simple face mask:

Simple face masks can deliver 5-10L at a maximum FiO2. Used for patients with no concerns for type 2 respiratory failure. 


## Reservoir (non-rebreath) mask

Reservoir masks deliver [[Pearls/Oxygen|Oxygen]] at concentrations between 60% and 90% when used at a flow rate of 10–15 l/min.³ Maximum non-invasive oxygen delivery method. 



## Venturi Masks

**Deliver a fixed concentration of oxygen**
Used in patients with chronic lungs diseases at risk of developing type 2 respiratory failure. In these patients its important to know exactly the concentration of oxygen being delivered.

## Bag valve masks

Asks in a similar was as a non-rebreath but acts with **positive pressure**. Flow rate of 15L 60-90% 

## CPAP hood

Positive pressure as well. Flow rate 1-15L. The difference between ventilation and oxygenation needs


## Ventilator

Invasive positive pressure.. Oxygen flow rate of 1-15L.
