#### Hep A
- Hep A vaccine is preferred
- Human Normal Immunoglobulin (HNIG) for those who cannot receive the vaccine or where rapid protection is needed

#### Hep B

HBsAg positive source: 
- Known responder (anti-HBs above threshold), a booster dose of vaccine may be needed
- If they are a non responder, the should also receive HBIG

#### Hep C

There are no effective post-exposure prophylaxis
- Repeat HCV RNA at 6 + 12 weeks exposure
- HCV antibody at 3 and 6 months
- If seroconversion occurs, refer to hepatology for direct-acting antiviral therapy 

#### HIV

The risk of HIV transmission depends  of the nature of the exposure and the viral load of the source. Low risk events e.g. human bites do not require PEP unless blood is present
- First line regime: oral tenofovir disproxil/emtricitabine + raltegravir
- Should be started STAT (ideally within 1-2 hours and no later than 72 hours)
- Treatment lasts 4 weeks
- HIV testing required at 12 weeks
- Reduces transmission by 80%

#### Varicella zoster

 Varicella zoster immunoglobulin (VZIG) should be given to IgG-negative pregnant women or immunosuppressed individuals after significant exposure.
 - If >10 days post-exposure, oral aciclovir may be used


##### Estimates of transmission risk for single needlestick injury

| Virus       | Chance of transmission               |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Hepatitis B | 20–30% (if source is HBeAg positive) |
| Hepatitis C | 0.5–2%                               |
| HIV         | 0.3%                                 |
