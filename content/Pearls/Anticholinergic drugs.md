Almost always means blocking muscarinic ACh receptors aka blocking the parasympathetic pathway

> [!success] Anticholinergic side effects
> - **Can't see:** Blurred vision and dry eyes (caused by dilated pupils and reduced tears).
> - **Can't pee:** Urinary retention (difficulty starting a stream or emptying the bladder).
> - **Can't spit:** Dry mouth (decreased saliva production).
> - **Can't shit:** Constipation (slowed digestion and bowel motility).


With a high burden, they can cause dizziness, vision changes, sedation and a drop in BP, increase in heart rate. This can all lead to a **high risk of falls**

| **Muscarinic effect blocked**          | **When it's the target effect**                                                                               | **When it's a side effect**                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **↓ secretions (salivary, bronchial)** | Glycopyrrolate/atropine as pre-op antisialagogue; hyoscine for palliative "death rattle"                      | Dry mouth from a TCA (amitriptyline) or oxybutynin (a common reason elderly patients stop their meds)                                    |
| **Bronchodilation**                    | Ipratropium / tiotropium in COPD & asthma                                                                     |                                                                                                                                          |
| **Mydriasis + cycloplegia**            | Tropicamide for fundoscopy; cyclopentolate for refraction; atropine in uveitis to prevent synechiae           | Blurred near vision from any systemic anticholinergic; precipitating [[Full/Acute Angle Closure Glaucoma\|Acute Angle Closure Glaucoma]] |
| **↑ heart rate (blocks vagal tone)**   | Atropine for symptomatic bradycardia or organophosphate poisoning                                             | Palpitations/tachycardia in TCA overdose or in a cardiac patient given an antimuscarinic                                                 |
| **↓ GI motility / antispasmodic**      | Dicyclomine, hyoscyamine for IBS; hyoscine butylbromide (Buscopan) for gut colic                              | Constipation or ileus -worsened when combined with opioids                                                                               |
| **Bladder detrusor relaxation**        | Oxybutynin, tolterodine, solifenacin for overactive bladder                                                   | Acute urinary retention in an older man with BPH given diphenhydramine or a TCA                                                          |
| **Central effects**                    | Scopolamine for motion sickness; benztropine/trihexyphenidyl for Parkinson tremor & drug-induced EPS/dystonia | Confusion, delirium, memory impairment (especially elderly) — the "mad as a hatter" piece                                                |
| **Sedation**                           | Diphenhydramine as an OTC sleep aid / antiemetic                                                              | Daytime drowsiness and impaired cognition                                                                                                |
