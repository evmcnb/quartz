### Ligaments

- **Anterior cruciate ligament (ACL)**: Resists anterior translation of the tibia relative to the femur (and helps limit rotation/hyperextension). Typically damaged by a sudden deceleration, pivoting, or twisting on a planted foot.
- **Posterior cruciate ligament (PCL)**: Resists posterior translation of the tibia relative to the femur. Typically damaged by a force driving the tibia backward, classically the "dashboard injury" (knee striking the dashboard in a car crash) or falling onto a flexed knee with the foot pointed down.
- **Lateral collateral ligament (LCL):** Resists varus stress (forces pushing the knee outward / adducting the lower leg). Typically damaged by a blow to the inner (medial) side of the knee that forces it into varus, or by severe twisting.
- **Medial collateral ligament (MCL)**: Resists valgus stress (forces pushing the knee inward / abducting the lower leg). Typically damaged by a direct blow to the outer (lateral) side of the knee that forces it into valgus.

### Menisci

- **Medial meniscus**: Bears and distributes load on the medial compartment and helps resist excessive rotation. It is firmly attached to the joint capsule and to the MCL, so it can't move out of the way easily. Typically damaged by a twisting force on a flexed, weight-bearing knee (e.g. pivoting with the foot planted). Because of its fixed attachment, it's injured far more often than the lateral meniscus, and it's the classic third component of the "unhappy triad" (ACL + MCL + medial meniscus), torn by a valgus-plus-rotation blow to the outside of the knee.
- **Lateral meniscus**: Bears and distributes load on the lateral compartment and contributes to stability and shock absorption. It is more loosely attached and more mobile (it's not bound to the LCL), so it moves out of harm's way more readily and is injured less often. Still damaged by twisting/rotational forces on a loaded knee, and acute tears often accompany ACL injuries.

Outer 1/3 have vascular supply so heal well. Inner 2/3 are avascular and heal poorly
