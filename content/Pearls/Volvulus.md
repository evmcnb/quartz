Volvulus may be defined as torsion of the colon around it's mesenteric axis resulting in compromised blood flow and closed loop obstruction.

Malrotation of the intestines may **cause** a volvulus which is the ***surgical emergency***

- Sigmoid volvulus (80%) - large bowel obstruction caused by sigmoid colon twisting around sigmoid mesocolon 
- Caecum volvulus (20%) - most people its a retroperitoneal structure so no risk. In the remaining  there is developmental failure of peritoneal fixation of the proximal bowel.

Management  
- sigmoid volvulus: flexible sigmoidoscopy with rectal tube insertion
- caecal volvulus
    - emergency laparotomy or laparoscopy:
    - if bowel is viable: right hemicolectomy (removes the redundant mobile caecum and prevents recurrence) followed by primary ileocolic anastomosis
    - if bowel is non-viable (ischaemia/perforation): right hemicolectomy + stoma (e.g. end ileostomy with mucous fistula).
