- Usually acyanotic - eventually results in late cyanosis in the lower extremities
- Connection between the pulmonary trunk and descending aorta
- More common in [[Premature babies]], born at high altitude or maternal [[Rubella]] infection

Features: 
- Left subclavicular thrill
- Continuous machinery murmur
- Large volume, bounding, collapsing pulse
- Wide pulse pressure
- Heaving apex beat

#### Management

**Preterm infants**
- Initial supportive care as spontaneous closure often occurs
- If hemodynamically significant PDA remains or infant remains ventilator dependent after one week old, then pharmacological closure: 
	- ibuprofen, indomethacin (less common) or paracetamol 
	- inhibits prostaglandin synthesis 
	- Given to the **infant** not the mother in the antenatal period 

**Term infants and children**
- Trans-catheter PDA closure
- Indications - moderate or large PDA, prior episode of [[Pearls/Endocarditis|Endocarditis]], audible PDA

If the PDA is associated with another congential heart defect which requires surgery, prostaglandin E1 is used to keep the duct open until after the surgical repair. E.g. if the infant was dependant on the PDA for systemic oxygenation
