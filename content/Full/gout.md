A form of [[Full/Inflammatory Arthritis|Inflammatory Arthritis]]/[[Full/Crystal Arthritis|Crystal Arthritis]]. Patients have episodes that last several days between returning to symptom free. During the episodes:
- Significant pain
- Swelling
- Erythema

Most of the presentations affect the 1st MTP. Other common sites are the ankle, wrist and knee. 

Caused by high uric acid 
## Causes/Factors

**Hyperuricemia:** Too much uric acid in blood - kidneys not excreting properly, crystallises out in joints

**Decreased excretion of uric acid**
- drugs - diuretics, high dose aspirin (>150mg)
- [[Full/Chronic Kidney Disease|Chronic Kidney Disease]]
- lead toxicity

**Increased production**
- myelo/lymphoproliferative disorders ([[Pearls/Memorising the leukaemias|leukaemias]], [[Pearls/Multiple myeloma|Multiple myeloma]], [[Non-Hodgkin lymphoma]], chemo, [[Full/Tumour lysis syndrome|Tumour lysis syndrome]])
- cytotoxic rugs
- severe [[Pearls/Psoriasis|Psoriasis]]
## Symptoms

- **Sudden Onset of Joint Pain:** In more than one half of patients with acute [gout](https://notes.nosos.co.uk/Full/gout), the first metatarsophalangeal joint is the initial joint involved, a condition known as podagra
- **Swelling and Inflammation:** Redness and warmth over the affected joint.
- **Limited Range of Motion:** Difficulty moving the joint.
- **Recurrent Attacks:** Periods of symptom-free intervals followed by flares.
- Severe pain - can’t bare weight
- Feet and knee are classic locations


## Signs

**Tophi:** Nodules under the skin containing uric acid crystals.

## Investigations

#### Bloods

Uric acid levels: 
- > 450 $\mu$mol/L pretty much diagnostic 
- $\geq$ 360 $\mu$mol/L supportive of diagnosis
- < 360 during a flare and gout is still strongly suspect, repeat 2 weeks after flare has settled. 


#### Joint aspiration

Essential to exclude [[Pearls/Septic Arthritis|Septic Arthritis]]. Fluid for gout should show:
- monosodium urate crystals - **needle-shaped**, **negatively birefringent of polarised light**
- no bacterial growth 

#### XR

- Maintained joint space
- Lytic lesions
- Punched out erosions $\pm$ sclerotic boarders

## Management

#### Acute 

NSAIDs or colchicine - max dose until 1/2 days after symptoms have settled
- + **PPI**
- colchicine may be use with caution in renal impairment. Main side effect of diarrhoea
- **Continue allopurinol** if they are already taking it

Oral steroids if both above contraindicated (oral pred 15mg OD)

#### Prophylaxis

**Lifestyle modifications**
- reduce alcohol intake
- lose weight
- avoid food high in purines (liver, kidenys, seafood, oily fish)


**Medications:** offer allopurinol to all patients after their first attack (maybe 2 weeks after)
- 100mg OD titrated to aim for serum uric acid <360
- febuxostat 2nd line if allopurinol not tolerated or effective
