Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) is a complex autoimmune disease that can affect multiple organs and systems in the body. It is a Type III hypersensitivity reaction. SLE varies widely in its presentation and severity.

- thought to be caused by immune system dysregulation leading to immune complex formation
- immune complex deposition can affect any organ including the skin, joints, kidneys and brain

Anti-DNA reaction so can present in any system

## Causes/Factors

Combination of environmental and genetic factors
- Much more common in females (like most autoimmune stuff)
- Onset usually 20-40 years
- HLA B8; DR2; DR3

## Symptoms and Signs

Classic differentiating symptoms of SLE
- **Joint Pain and Swelling:** Arthritis especially in small joints of hand and foot.
- **Hair Loss:** Thinning or hair loss, often in patches.
- **Malar Rash:** Butterfly-shaped rash across the cheeks and bridge of the nose.
- **Mouth Sores:** Ulcers in the mouth or nose (esp in odd places, arch of palate)

Other non-specific symptoms
- **Fatigue:** Profound tiredness and weakness.
- **Fever:** Elevated body temperature.
- **[[Pearls/Raynaud's Phenomenon]]:** Fingers and toes turning white or blue in response to cold or stress.
- **Chest Pain:** Pleurisy or [[Pearls/Pericarditis|Pericarditis]], inflammation of the lining around the lungs or heart.
- **Kidney Problems:** Protein or blood in the urine due to kidney inflammation.
- **Neurological Symptoms:** [[Pearls/Headaches|Headaches]], seizures, or cognitive dysfunction.

## Diagnostic Tests
Diagnosis of SLE is done on the balance of the patient having a number of key features found in guidelines. A patient does not need to have all of the features to be diagnosed with SLE

- 99% are ANA positive 
- 20% are RF postiive
- Anti-dsDNA highly specific but less sensitive. Levels correlate with SLE disease activity
- anti-Smith, anti-U1 RNP, SS-A (anti-Ro) and SS-B (anti-La)
- FBC: anaemia (of chronic disease or hemolytic), lymphopenia, thrombocytopenia
- Complement levels are low
  
> [!tldr] 2019 EULAR/ACR Classification Criteria for SLE
>
> **Entry criterion (mandatory):** ANA ≥1:80 on HEp-2 cells (or equivalent) at least once. If absent → do not classify as SLE.
> **Classification:** Entry criterion met **+ ≥10 points + at least one clinical criterion** = SLE.
>
> **Rules:** Only count a criterion if there's no more likely explanation. A criterion counts if it occurred at least once (need not be concurrent). Within each domain, count **only the highest-weighted** criterion.
>

## Management

#### Hydroxychloroquine

- treatment of choice for almost all patients with SLE
- reduces disease activity and flare frequency - associated with reduced long-term organ damage and mortality
- improves fatigue, joint and skin symptoms
- baseline and annual **ophthalmology screening required** (retinal toxicity risk) -  bull's eye maculopathy 
- usually continued long-term unless contraindicated
- can be continued during pregnancy (particularly relevant as SLE often affects women of childbearing age)



1. NSAIDS for mild musculoskeletal pain and serositis
2. Sun protection SPF 50, exercise, balanced diet
3. Corticosteroids - used for flares and moderate-severe disease. IV methylpred for severe/life-threatening 
4. Regular monitoring (FBC, U&E/urinalysis, CRP/ESR, anti-dsDNA)


## Complications/red Flags

- **Organ Damage:** SLE can damage the skin, kidneys, heart, lungs, nervous system, and more.
- **Flares:** Periods of increased disease activity and symptoms.
- **Infections:** Weakened immune system can lead to infections.
- Secondary [[Pearls/Antiphospholipid syndrome|Antiphospholipid syndrome]] - **[[Full/Pregnancy]] complications:** High-risk pregnancies due to increased disease activity.
#### Lupus Nephritis

Severe manifestation of SLE that can result in end-stage renal disease. Should get regular urinalysis to rule out proteinuria

Biopsy  
- biopsy guides management in most cases as clinical features do not reliably predict histological class
- treatment intensity and drug choice depend on histological class
- life-saving treatment (e.g. steroids) should not be delayed in severe disease while awaiting biopsy
- example of strong indications:
    - persistent proteinuria (usually ≥0.5 g/day or raised PCR/ACR)
    - active urinary sediment (haematuria, casts)
    - unexplained or rising serum creatinine
- mild disease may be managed conservatively without immediate biopsy

Usually give **mycophenolate**
