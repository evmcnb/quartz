## Before

- **Wash hands** (and don PPE if needed)
- **Introduce yourself** (name and role) to friends and family and offer condolences
- Confirm patient's **name** and **DOB** with ward staff and on the wrist band
- Explain what the certification involves
- Gain consent to continue and invite them to leave or stay

## Death Confirmation

1. Check identity
2. For a minimum of 5 minutes, confirm the absence of: 
	- Central pulse (carotid)
	- Heart sounds on auscultation
	- Respiratory sounds on auscultation
	- Signs of life (asystole on continuous ECG monitoring)
3. Confirm absence of reflexes: pupillary with a pen torch (pupils should be fixed and dilated), bilateral corneal reflexes, absence of any motor response to supraorbital pressure
4. The time of death should be recorded as the time at which all these criteria have been confirmed 

## Documentation

Once you have completed the steps to confirm the death, you must document your assessment with timings.

### Completing the death certificate

- Write clearly with a black pen
- Avoid all abbreviations
- Legal document so must be completed accurately 


#### Part 1 - cause of death

The section should be discussed with either the ME/MEO or coroner before documenting. 

Part 1 is the sequence of conditions that lead to directly to the death. 

The disease that directly led to death should be documented as 1(a), then work backwards to the disease or condition that led to 1(a) as 1(b). 

Where possible document the approximate duration of diseases/conditions 

Where there are joint causes of death, this should be clearly stated, e.g.: “_I (a) Cardiorespiratory failure_; _I (b) Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and ischaemic heart disease (joint causes of death)”_.


> [!danger] Things that CANNOT be written as 1(a)
> - **Failures** as the sole cause of death. You must be able to define the underlying cause within the rest of part 1. **Heart failure is the exception**, although often an underlying cause can be identified and documented
> - **Learning disabilities, mental health diagnoses and physical disabilities**. An exception may be Alzheimer's dementia
>  - **Cardiac/respiratory arrest, syncope, shock**. These are modes of death, not causes
>  - Natural causes
>  - Vague diagnoses: eg *cardiac event, ?cerebral infarction, ?cardiac tamponade*
>  - Old age/frailty of old age. This can only be used in patients over 80 years old who have been known to the medical practitioner for at least 6 months, where the gradual decline has been noted


#### Part 2 - contributing conditions

Document other conditions that are not part of the main causal pathway but likely played a role in hastening the death. It is NOT somewhere to list the entire past medical history. Only things that are directly relevant.
