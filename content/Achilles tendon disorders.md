Achilles tendon connects the gastronemius and soleus to the calcaneus bone.

Risk factors: 
- quinolone use (e.g. **ciprofloxacin**) is associated with tendon disorders
- hypercholesterolaemia (predisposes to tendon xanthomata)

### Tendinopathy (tendinitis)

- Gradual onset of posterior heel pain
- Worse following activity
- Morning pain and stiffness common

Two types of 
- **_Insertion tendinopathy_** (within 2cm of the insertion point on the calcaneus) 
- **_Mid-portion tendinopathy_** (2-6 cm above the insertion point)

Supportive management - NSAIDs + physio

> [!success] Simmonds' calf squeeze test
> Essential to exclude tendon rupture. Patient lies prone with feet over the edge of the bed. 
> 1. Look for abnormal angle of declination. Rupture may lead to greater dorisoflexion
> 2. Feel for a gap in the tendon
> 3. Gently squeeze the calf and observe lack of movement of the foot 

### Rupture

- Pop
- Sudden onset significant pain
- Inability to walk

Perform the Simmonds' exam/test as above. Ultrasound initial imaging modality of choice

**Acute referral to otho for suspected rupture** (same day)

Debate between non-surgical vs surgical. Above my pay, either way consider VTE prophylaxis for periods of immobilisation
