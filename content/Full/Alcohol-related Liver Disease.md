Alcohol-related liver disease (ARLD) refers to liver damage caused by excess alcohol intake. There are several **stages** of severity and a range of associated symptoms:

1. Alcoholic fatty liver disease - reversible after 2 weeks of drink free
2. Alcoholic [[Full/Hepatitis]] - first point of symptoms. Usually reversible. Severe alcoholic [[Full/Hepatitis]] can be life threatening
3. [[Full/Liver Cirrhosis]]

## Causes/Factors

- Binge drinking -> fatty liver disease and sometimes alcoholic [[Full/Hepatitis]]
- Chronic alcohol abuse -> alcoholic [[Full/Hepatitis]] and [[Full/Liver Cirrhosis]]
- Overweight/obese
- Females seem to be more vulnerable to the harmful effects than men
- Genetics

## Symptoms

- Nausea
- Weight loss
- Loss of appetite
- [[Pearls/Jaundice]]
- Oedema
- [[Pearls/Ascites|Ascites]]

## Diagnostic Tests

- [[Pearls/Liver function tests|Liver function tests]] ($\downarrow$ albumin) - AST/ALT > 2 is characteristic of alcoholic [[Full/Hepatitis]]
	- $\gamma$GT especially raised
- Ultrasound
- Biopsy
- Raised MCB

## Management

- Stop drinking
- Good diet and nutrition
- Corticosteroids ([[Pearls/Prednisolone|Prednisolone]]) reduces mortality (Maddrey's discriminant score)
- Liver transplant if [[Pearls/Liver failure|Liver failure]] occurs

> [!NOTE] Maddrey's Discriminant Score
>
> Prognostic score for **alcoholic (alcohol-associated) hepatitis** - identifies severe disease and guides corticosteroid use.
>
> | Score | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **< 32** | Mild–moderate disease; good short-term prognosis |
> | **≥ 32** | **Severe** alcoholic hepatitis; high 28–90 day mortality (~30–50% untreated) |
>
> - A score **≥ 32** is the threshold to consider **corticosteroids** (e.g. prednisolone 40 mg/day), provided no contraindication (active infection, GI bleed, uncontrolled sepsis).
> - Reassess steroid response at day 7 using the **Lille score** — stop steroids if non-responder.
>
> ### Limitations
> - Relies on PT, which varies with lab reagents/control values (INR not interchangeable in the original formula).
> - Largely superseded or supplemented by **GAHS** (Glasgow Alcoholic Hepatitis Score) and **MELD**, which some find more reproducible.
## Complications/red Flags
- Hepatic encephalopathy - reduce ammonia by giving lactulose (excreted in stool)

### Alcohol Withdrawal

Dependance on alcohol and then suddenly going cold turkey has a risk of progressing to withdrawal and [[Full/Delirium]] tremens.

- 6-12h: tremors, sweating, headache, craving, anxiety
- 12-24h: hallucinations
- 24-48h: seizures
- 24-72h: **[[Full/Delirium]] tremens**

##### Management
- Chlordiazepoxide - benzo used to combat the effects. Given orally and titrated
- High dose B vitamins (Pabrinex) IV, followed by long term oral thiamine. Prevention of [[Pearls/Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome|Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome]]

#### Delirium tremens
- Medial emergency - 35% mortality if left untreated
- Removal of the depressant causes the brain to become extremely excitable.
