Recurrent episodes of mania/hypomania then depression. Two types of bipolar disorder recognised:
- Type 1: at least one manic episode (major depressive episode **not** required) 
- Type 2: at least one hypomanic episode **AND** at least one major depressive episode

Typically develops in the late teen years. 

> [!info] What is mania/hypomania?
> Both feature an abnormally and persistently elevated, expansive, or irritable mood, along with increased energy. The difference comes down to **duration, severity, and psychosis.**
> 
> | **Feature**               | **Mania**                                        | **Hypomania**                                   |
> | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
> | **Duration**              | **≥ 7 days** (or any duration if hospitalised)   | **≥ 4 days**                                    |
> | **Functional Impairment** | Severe (often disrupts work/social life heavily) | Clear change in functioning, but **NOT** severe |
> | **Psychotic Symptoms**    | Can be present (e.g., delusions of grandeur)     | **Never** present                               |
> | **Hospitalisation**       | Often required to keep the patient safe          | Not required                                    |
> 

### Management 


1. Stop antidepressant (if prescribed)
2. Start anti-psychotic (usually 2nd generation). Acute management with quetiapine (improves mania symptoms + sedation). Usually improvement in symptoms over 1-2 days
3. Once stable start [[Pearls/Lithium|Lithium]] (quetiapine/[[Pearls/Lamotrigine|Lamotrigine]] alternatives. [[Pearls/Sodium Valproate|Sodium Valproate]] strictly regulated now). Counselling around lithium starting
4. Consider talking therapies for depression or **low dose fluoxetine**

If symptoms suggest hypomania then NICE recommend routine referral to the community mental health team (CMHT)

If there are features of mania or severe depression then an urgent referral to the CMHT should be made


> [!info] Anti-depressives in Bipolar disorder
> If a patient presents with major depression (but is actually undiagnosed bipolar) and is prescribed an antidepressant, the medication can "overshoot" the baseline and propel them directly into mania or hypomania.
