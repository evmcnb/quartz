The Bartholin's glands are located either side of the posterior part of the vaginal opening. They are usually pea-sized and not palpable. They produce mucus to help with vaginal lubrication

When the ducts become blocked, the glands can swell and become tender causing a cyst to form.
- Usually unilateral 1-4cm in size
- Can become infected forming a Bartholin's abscess

Usually resolve with conservative management - good hygiene, analgesia, warm compresses. Incision generally avoided

**Abscesses** will require antibiotics. A swab can be taken to culture and check sensitivities. Send specific swabs for chlamydia and gonorrhoea. 

Bartholin's gland balloon first line generally (aka Word catheter)
- Local anaesthetic needed
- Incision made to trade the pus
- The catheter is then inserted into the abscess space and inflated
- This prevents a cyst/abscess from recurring.
- The catheter can be deflated and remove at a later date
