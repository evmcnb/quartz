Typically HSV-2 for genital herpes but HSV-1 can also cause it. 

Following a primary infection, the virus becomes latent in a local sensory ganglia, periodically reactivating to cause symptomatic lesion. 

Features: 
- painful genital **ulceration**
- primary infection is more severe than recurrent episodes - systemic infection
- tender inguinal lymphadenopathy 

Investigations:
- NAAT gold standard 
- HSV serology may be used in certain situations


**Management**
- Oral aciclovir 
- Conservative management: saline bathing, analgesia, topical anaesthetic agents

Recurrent/severe hepers: oral antiviral therapy for 6-12 months

**Pregnancy**
- Elective [[Pearls/Caesarean Section|Caesarean Section]] at term if primary attack occurs during pregnancy at >28 weeks gestation 
- Current herpes should be treated with suppressive therapy and advised that risk to baby is low
