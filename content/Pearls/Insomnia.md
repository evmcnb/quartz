Difficulty initiating or maintaining seep or early-morning awakening that leads to dissatisfaction with sleep quantity or quality. 
- Acute: typically related to a life event and resolves without treatment
- Chronic: at least 3 nights per week for 3 months or longer

The following features are associated with insomnia:  
- Female gender
- Increased age
- Lower educational attainment
- Unemployment
- Economic inactivity
- Widowed, divorced, or separated status

Along with other expected things (stimulant use, alcohol use, other psychiatric illness, [[Pearls/Corticosteroids|Corticosteroid use]]) 

Features: 
- Daytime napping
- Enlarged tonsils or tongue
- Micrognathia or retrognathia (small jaw)

Clinical diagnosis, need to exclude [[Full/Obstructive Sleep Apnoea|Obstructive Sleep Apnoea]]

### Management


#### Short-term

- Identify causes and associations
- Advise the person not to drive
- Advise good sleep hygiene: no screens before bed, limited caffeine intake, fixed bed times
- Only consider hypnotics if daytime impairment is severe (good evidence).
	- Short-acting or non-benzos (e.g. zopiclone)
	- Not diazepam
	- Lowest effective dose
	- No effect with one, don't try another, that's it
	- Review after 2 weeks and consider CBT
	- Other sedative drugs (SSRIs, antihistamines, etc) are not recommended

#### Longer-term

All of the above plus referral for CBT to treat underlying issue.
