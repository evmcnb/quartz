Most commonly affects the femur, then tibia and humerus. Most common in 10-20 year olds

Mainly presents with persiustent bone pain, worse at night. This may wake from sleep. Other symptoms: 
- Bone swelling
- Mass
- Restricted joint movements


> [!info] Referral Criteria
> XR within **48 hours** for children presenting with unexplained bone pain or swelling. If suspicious, need to see a specialist for assessment within 48 hours. 
> 
> Typical "fluffy" appearance of XR. There will be a periosteal reaction - giving  a "sun-burst" appearance

Further investigations: 
- Blood tests - bone screen, raised ALP
- CT
- MRI
- Bone scan
- PET
- Bone biopsy 

### Management

**Surgical resection** of the lesion often with **limb amputation**. Adjuvant chemotherapy 

Main complication of [[Pearls/Pathological fractures|Pathological fractures]] and metastasis
