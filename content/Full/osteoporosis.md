Reduced [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] mass

Primary - age related
Secondary - to another condition or drug

- If trabecular [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] is affected, crush fractures of vertebrae are common.
- If cortical [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] is affected, long [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] fractures are more likely [[Full/Hip Fractures]]


## Causes/Factors

SHATTERED for [[Pearls/Osteoporosis|Osteoporosis]] risk factors

- **S**teroid use of >5mg/day of [[Pearls/Prednisolone|Prednisolone]]
- **H**[[Full/Hyperthyroidism|yperthyroidism]], hyperparathyroidism, hypercalciuria 
- **A**lcohol and tobacco use
- **T**hin - BMI < 18.5
- **T**estosterone $\downarrow$
- **E**arly menopause 
- **R**enal or [[Pearls/Liver failure|Liver failure]]
- **E**rosive/inflammatory [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] disease - [[Pearls/Multiple myeloma]] or [[Full/Inflammatory Arthritis]]
- **D**ietary low calcium absorption - T1 [[Full/Diabetes Mellitus]]

## Symptoms

- Associated fractures
- Loss of height 

> [!tldr] Fracture Risk Assessment Tool 
>
> Developed by the University of Sheffield/WHO. Estimates a patient's **10-year probability** of:
> - **Major osteoporotic fracture** (clinical spine, hip, forearm, or proximal humerus)
> - **Hip fracture**
>
> Country-specific; calculated with or without femoral neck BMD. Applicable to untreated patients aged **40–90**.
>
> ### Input variables
> | Factor | Notes |
> |---|---|
> | Age | 40–90 years |
> | Sex | — |
> | Weight & height | Used to derive BMI |
> | Previous fragility fracture | Includes morphometric vertebral fracture |
> | Parental hip fracture | Family history |
> | Current smoking | Yes/No |
> | Glucocorticoids | Current or ≥3 months of ≥5 mg prednisolone/day |
> | Rheumatoid arthritis | Confirmed diagnosis |
> | Secondary osteoporosis | e.g. T1DM, untreated hypogonadism, hyperthyroidism, chronic liver disease |
> | Alcohol ≥3 units/day | — |
> | Femoral neck BMD | Optional; improves accuracy (T-score or g/cm²) |
>
> ### Using the result
> - Plug the 10-year risk into national guidance (e.g. **NOGG** in the UK) to guide treatment thresholds.
> - Output falls into a **treat / lifestyle-advice / reassess** category, often plotted against an intervention threshold.
>
> ### Key limitations
> - **Does not** account for dose/duration of steroids, number or recency of prior fractures, falls risk, or lumbar spine BMD (femoral neck only).
> - May **underestimate** risk in recent fractures, high-dose steroids, and diabetes.
> - Not validated in patients already on osteoporosis treatment.

## Diagnostic Tests

- Diagnosing [[Pearls/Osteoporosis|Osteoporosis]] involves a [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] density test, typically measured by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA or DEXA). This test measures [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] mineral density (BMD) and assesses the risk of fractures.
- Bloods - Ca, PO4, ALP (for [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] mets) 

Biopsy unreliable and unnecessary 


> [!tldr] DEXA Scans
> Basics  
> - T score: based on bone mass of young reference population
> - T score of -1.0 means bone mass of one standard deviation below that of young reference population
> - Z score is adjusted for age, gender and ethnic factors
> 
>   
> T score  
> - $>$ -1.0 = normal
> - -1.0 to -2.5 = osteopaenia
> - < -2.5 = osteoporosis


## Management

May not be entirely irreversible 

1. **Calcium and Vitamin D Supplements:** Adequate calcium and vitamin D intake are essential for [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]] health.
2. **Lifestyle Changes:** Regular weight-bearing exercises, quitting smoking, and moderating alcohol intake.
3. **Medications:** Various medications, including **bisphosphonates**, hormone therapy, and denosumab, may be prescribed to strengthen bones.
4. **Fall Prevention:** Taking steps to prevent falls, such as using assistive devices and making home modifications.
5. **Fracture Management:** Treatment of fractures if they occur, including pain management and rehabilitation.

NICE recommends considering pharmacological treatment in people aged >= 50 years and postmenopausal women with:  
- a previous hip or vertebral fragility fracture
- very high-dose systemic glucocorticoids, for example prednisolone >= 15 mg/day for >= 3 months
- a BMD T-score <= -2.5
- a BMD T-score <= -1.5 plus:
    - a previous fragility fracture
    - current or frequent systemic glucocorticoid use
    - another factor associated with accelerated bone loss, for example aromatase inhibitors or androgen deprivation therapy

## Complications/red Flags

- **Fractures:** The main complication of [[Pearls/Osteoporosis|Osteoporosis]], fractures can lead to pain, disability, and a decreased quality of life.
- **Loss of Independence:** Severe fractures, especially [[Full/Hip Fractures]], can lead to a loss of independence.
