**_Sciatica_** refers to the symptoms associated with irritation of the **_sciatic nerve_**.

Acute low back pain should improve within 1-2 weeks. Recovery can take longer (4-6 weeks) for sciatica.

**_Chronic lower back pain_** can have a massive impact on the patient’s quality of life and be difficult to manage. 

There are several challenges with managing patients with lower back pain:

## Causes/Factors

- Muscle/ligament sprain
- Facet joint dysfunction
- Sacroiliac dysfunction
- Herniated disc
- Spondylolisthesis (anterior displacement of a vertebra out of line with the one below)
- Scoliosis
- Degenerative changes

### Red flag causes
- Spinal fracture - trauma
- [[Pearls/Cauda equina syndrome|Cauda equina syndrome]] (altered sensation in perianal area, incontinence)
- Spinal stenosis (intermittent neurogenic claudication)
- [[Full/Ankylosing Spondylitis]]
- Spinal infection (fever or history of drug use)
- [[Pearls/Spinal metastases|Spinal metastases]]

Lots of other causes of pain around the area of the back - so take a good history & exam

### Prolapsed disc

Usually produces clear dermatomal leg pain with neurological deficits

|**Site of compression**|**Features**|
|---|---|
|**L3 nerve root compression**|Sensory loss over anterior thigh  <br>Weak hip flexion, knee extension and hip adduction  <br>Reduced knee reflex  <br>Positive femoral stretch test|
|**L4 nerve root compression**|Sensory loss anterior aspect of knee and medial malleolus  <br>Weak knee extension and hip adduction  <br>Reduced knee reflex  <br>Positive femoral stretch test|
|**L5 nerve root compression**|Sensory loss dorsum of foot  <br>Weakness in foot and big toe dorsiflexion  <br>Reflexes intact  <br>Positive sciatic nerve stretch test|
|**S1 nerve root compression**|Sensory loss posterolateral aspect of leg and lateral aspect of foot  <br>Weakness in plantar flexion of foot  <br>Reduced ankle reflex  <br>Positive sciatic nerve stretch test|

## Management

Patients at **_low risk_** of chronic back pain can generally be managed with: 
- Self-management, education, reassurance
- Physiotherapy
- Analgesia
- Staying active and continuing to mobilise as tolerated

> [!warning] NICE analgesic recommendations
> Patients at **_low risk_** of chronic back pain can generally be managed with: 
> - NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen or naproxen) first-line + PPI cover
> - Codeine as an alternative
> - Benzodiazepines (e.g., diazepam) for muscle spasm (short-term only – up to 5 days)

If symptoms persist after 4-6 weeks, then refer for MRI
