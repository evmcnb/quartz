When the umbilical cord descends ahead of the presenting fetus. Left untreated this can lead to compression of the cord and fatal fetal hypoxia

Risk factors:
- Prematurity
- Multiparity
- Polyhydramnios
- Twin pregnancy
- Abnormal presentations

Around 50% of cord prolapses occur at artificial rupture of the membranes

**Management** - *obstetric emergency*
- The presenting part of the fetus may be pushed back into the uterus to avoid compression 
- If the cord is past the level of the introitus, there should be minimal handling and it should be kept warm and moist
- The patient should be asked to go on all fours until preparations are made for an **immediate C-section**
- Tocolytics may be used e.g. (terbutaline)
- Retrofilling the baldder with 500-700ml of saline may be helpful
