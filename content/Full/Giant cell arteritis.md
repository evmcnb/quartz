Inflammation of the temporal artery
## Causes/Factors

- Older age
- Caucasian
- Smoking
- [[Full/Polymyalgia rheumatica]] 
## Presentation

- Severe headache
- Jaw pain - at the end of speech or a meal 
- Scalp tenderness
- Loss of/double vision - can be transient but large risk of permanent loss
- Prominent temporal vessels but no pulse
- Pale optic disc

## Investigations

- Painful temporal arteries of palpation/eating
- CRP, ESR (> 50)
- Biopsy for definitive diagnosis (multinucleated giant cells)
- USS halo sign on temporal artery 

## Management

- High dose corticosteroids - oral [[Pearls/Prednisolone|Prednisolone]] 40mg or 60mg
	- If visual involvement or jaw claudication 500-1000mg  methylprednisolone
	- (+ PPI, bisphosphonates, calcium and vitamin D for protection of stomach and bones)
- Rheumatology referral

## Complications/red Flags

**Ocular** - anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy (causing the pale optic disc). Can lead to irreversible blindness
