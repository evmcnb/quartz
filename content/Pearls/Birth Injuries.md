### Caput Succedaneum

Oedema of the subcutaneous tissue on the scalp. Caused by pressure to a specific area of the scalp during a traumatic prolonged or instrumental delivery

Soft and diffuse swelling across the suture lines. 

Does not require any treatment and will resolve within a few days


> [!tip] How to tell the difference
> Caput SuccaDAYneum (Crosses Sutures) - resolves within a few days  
> 
> CephalohaematoMONTH (doesn't cross sutures) - resolves within a few months

### Cephalohaematoma

Blood collecting between the skull and the periosteum. Caused by blood vessel damage during a traumatic, prolonged or instrumental delivery

The lump created does not cross suture lines (help to distinguish between above)

Generally resolve without treatment within a few months. Risk of anaemia and jaundice (occurs when the blood within the haematoma breaks down). Newborn should be monitored for signs of anaemia and jaundice

### Facial paralysis

Delivery can damage the facial nerve (CN VII). Injury due to compression by forceps or of the mother's pelvis. Function normally returns spontaneously within a few months

Specialist input when there is persistent paralysis

### Frey's syndrome 
aka Auriculotemporal syndrome

Due to damage of the auriculotemporal nerve (a branch of the trigeminal nerve) during forceps delivery

Provides parasympathetic innervation to the salivary gland. After trauma the parasympathetic fibres may be rewired to send signals to the sympathetic fibres of the sweat glands and blood vessels. Consequently when the child eats, it **triggers sweating and skin flushing.**

May be misdiagnosed as a food allergy


### Erb's Palsy

Injury to the C5-C6 nerves in the brachial plexus during birth. As such affects shoulder abduction and external rotation, arm flexion, and finger extension. thus presents with: 
- Internally rotated shoulder
- Extended elbow
- Flexed wrist facing backwards 
- Lack of movement in the affected arm 

Function usually returns spontaneously within a few months. Specialist input when symptoms persist 


### Fractured Clavicle

A fractured clavicle can occur with shoulder dystocia, traumatic or instrumental delivery, and macrosomia (large baby).

- Reduced arm movement
- Shoulder asymmetry
- Pain and distress with movement 

Confirmed by ultrasound or XR. Conservative management - heals within 6 weeks. A brachial plexus injury is a potential complication which will also resolve with time.
