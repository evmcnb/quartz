Fractures may also involve the growth plate and these are classified according to Salter-Harris.
Use the **_SALTR mnemonic_** to remember the types:

| **Type** | **Injury pattern**                                                                                                     | Slang               |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| I        | Fracture through the physis only (x-ray often normal)                                                                  | **S**traight across |
| II       | Fracture through the physis and metaphysis                                                                             | **A**bove           |
| III      | Fracture through the physis and epiphysis to include the joint                                                         | **B**elow           |
| IV       | Fracture involving the physis, metaphysis and epiphysis                                                                | **T**hrough         |
| V        | Crush injury involving the physis (x-ray may resemble type I, and appear normal). Associated with disruption of growth | **C**rush           |
![[z_attachments/Pasted image 20260202150016.png]]

If there is tenderness around the growth plate, assume an underlying fracture even if the XR appears normal. 


  Genetic conditions, such as [[Pearls/Osteogenesis imperfecta]], may cause pathological fractures.


#### Management

1. Achieve mechanical alignment - closed reduction via manipulation of the joint, open reduction via surgery.
2. Provide relative stability for a period of time. Various ways to do this: 
	- External casts
	- K wires
	- Plates, screws, nails
3. Pain management - only two steps in the WHO pain ladder for children:
	- Paracetamol or ibuprofen
	- Morphine

Codeine and tramadol are not used in children as these is unpredictability in their metabolism
