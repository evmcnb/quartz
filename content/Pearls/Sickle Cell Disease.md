Autosomal recessive, more common in those of African descent (heterozygous condition offers protection against [[Malaria]])
* Anaemic, Hb-S >95% Hb-A 0%
* Clinical - vaso-occlusive crisis

Sickle cells are fragile and haemolyse, occluding small blood vessels causing infarction. 
- Diagnosed via haemoglobin electrophoresis 

**Acute** - septicaemia, aplastic crisis, sequestration crisis

**Chronic** - hyposplenism, renal disease, avascular necrosis


#### Vaso-occlusive crisis

- Extreme pain
- Distal ischaemia 
- Can cause priapism

#### Splanchnic crisis 

Blood getting trapped in the spleen.
- Severe pain
- Hypovolaemic shock 
- Recurrent crsis can lead to the hyposplenism and infections with encapsulated bacteria (Strep pneumo, Haem influ)

#### Aplastic crisis 

-  Temporary absence of RBC production
- May be triggered by parvovirus B19
- Significant anaemia - transfusions often required
- Usually resolves spontaneously after a week
### Management

![[Pearls/Sickle Cell Crisis|Sickle Cell Crisis]]

- Blood transfusion
- Investigate any event that may be precipitating the crisis e.g. infection 

**Long term management**
- Daily oral hydroxycarbamide - increases HbF levels, prophylactic
- Pneumococcal vaccine every 5 years (hyposplenism)
