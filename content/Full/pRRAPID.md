- Treat problems as you discover them and **re-assess after every intervention**
- If no signs of life put out a paediatric crash call and commence CPR
- Use local guidelines and algorithms to manage specific scenarios 
- Any medications or fluids must be prescribed at the time

- Have you checked the relevant observation?
- Have you examined the relevant parts of the system?
- Have you requested relevant investigations? 
- Have you intervened to correct the issues identified?
- Have you reassessed to see if they have worked?

## Initial steps

- Brief handover about the patient and why the review has been requested
- Introduce yourself to the practitioner
- Ensure the patient's notes, observation chart and prescription chart are easily accessible


## Airway

- Introduce yourself to the parents & patient if appropriate - name and role
- Children have smaller airways which are more easily obstructed
- If the child becomes frightened e.g. from bloods/needles this can trigger an obstruction. 

### Anatomical Differences

- Smaller
- Large head - neck is already flexed when a child is lying supine
- Large tongue
- Long and stiff epiglottis
- Highly compliant trachea risks kinking
- Short trachea and in line with right bronchus 

#### Observations

How is the patient feeling - listen to their response to assess the airway. 
- If they are talking their airway is patent.
- If not move onto exam:


>[!tldr] SAMPLE History Whilst Assessing Airway
>- **S**igns and symptoms
>- **A**llergies
>- **M**edications
>- **P**ast medical history
>- **L**ast oral intake
>- **E**vents surround injury/illness


#### Exam
- Look for signs of airway compromise: 
- Listen for abnormal airway noises: stridor, snoring, gurgling
- Feel for expired air

- Vocalisations such as crying indicate at least some degree of airway patency
- **Cyanosis/hypoxia** is a late sign

- Foreign body, anaphylaxis, burns, croup, epiglottis 

#### Interventions

Always seek immediate expert support - crash call if not breathing. Basic manoeuvres can be performed until they arrive:

- Infants - neutral position is the best for the airway
- Children - sniffing the air

Suction can be used to clear secretions - be very careful of not stimulating the oropharnyx - this could cause vomiting or laryngospasm.

### Airway adjuncts

Must be sized correctly
- Oropharyngeal - for unconscious  children. Incisors to angle of mandible. Put in the way that you want it to be (not upside down and twist)
- Nasopharyngeal - partial consciousness, e.g. seizure. Lateral nostril to tragus

## Breathing

#### Observations

- **Respiratory rate**
- **Oxygen saturation** (94-98%)

#### Exam

##### Effort of breathing

- Recessions (inter/sub-costal)
- Accessory muscle use
- Flaring of nostrils
- Head bobbing

##### Efficacy of breathing

- Air entry
- Percussion note
- Trachea central?
- Chest movement

#### Investigations

- ABG - when low O2 present 
- CXR - portable, if suspect of lung pathology 

#### Interventions

- **Oxygen** - 15L via non-rebreathe initially - aiming for sats 94-98%. Need to identify minute volume ($MV = TV \times RR$). For an infant 1L may result in a high oxygen concentration
	- Nasal cannula 1-4L
	- Simple face-mask 5-10L
- VBG usually maximum intervention while conscious. ABG too painful
- Can site a NG tube to decompress the stomach and improve ventilation
- CXR if have a focal cause of breathing difficulties (not for just a wheeze)


## Circulation

#### Observations

- HR
- Blood pressure - children are very good at maintaining their blood pressure. Any drop is a worrying sign. Ensure the correctly sized cuff
- Pulse volume
- CRT

#### Exam

- **General inspection** - pallor, oedema, mottled appearance. 
- **Fluid balance** - see chart if available. Reduced urine output (oliguria) is defined as less than 0.5ml/kg/hour
- **CRT** - peripherally on the finger, centrally on the sternum. 
- **Radial/brachial pulse** 
- **JVP** assessment
- **Auscultation** of heart sounds

#### Investigations

2 peripheral cannulae if possible. Else early IO insertion in the proximal tibia 

- Fluid challenge - 10ml/kg bolus of crystalloid/0.9% saline
- If more than 40ml/kg is needed, inotropes by the anaesthetist need to be considered 
- **Blood tests**/cultures
	- Sepsis: CRP, lactate and blood cultures
	- Haemorrhage or surgical emergency: coagulation and cross-match
	- Acute coronary syndrome: troponin
	- Arrhythmia: calcium, magnesium, phosphate, thyroid function test, coagulation
	- Pulmonary embolism: D-dimer (if appropriate based on Well’s score)
	- Overdose: toxicology screen (e.g. paracetamol levels)
	- Anaphylaxis: consider serial mast cell tryptase levels
- **ECG** - consider continuous cardiac monitoring in critically unwell patients. 
- **Pregnancy test** - any female of childbearing age presenting with clinical evidence of shock, abdo pain or gynae symptoms

#### Interventions

- **Control bleeding** - any areas of active loss
- **Fluid resuscitation** - 10ml/kg STAT bolus of Hartmann's or 0.9% saline. After each fluid bolus reassess for clinical evidence of overload. Repeat the bolus up to 4 times (2L total). ***Seek senior input if negative response or lack of response after 60ml/kg.***
- **Blood transfusion** - in patients with significant haemorrhage in shock. Need balanced transfusion of red cells and FFP. Arranged via **major haemorrhage protocol**
- **Catheterisation** - where appropriate

## Disability

#### Observation

- Review the patient's drug chart for medications 

#### Exam

Assess the patient’s **level of consciousness** using the ACVPU

- **A**lert: the patient is fully alert
- **C**onfusion: the patient has new onset confusion or worse confusion than usual
- **V**erbal: the patient makes some kind of response when you talk to them (e.g. words, grunt)
- **P**ain: the patient responds to a painful stimulus (e.g. supraorbital pressure)
- **U**nresponsive: the patient does not show evidence of any eye, voice or motor responses to pain

[[Full/Glasgow Coma Scale|Glasgow Coma Scale]] can be used for a more detailed assessment


> [!success] GCS with modification for children
> **Best eye response**
> 
> |**Score**|**Description**|
> |---|---|
> |**1**|No eye opening|
> |**2**|Eye opening to pain|
> |**3**|Eye opening to verbal command|
> |**4**|Eye open spontaneously|
> 
> **Best verbal response** _(use one of the following)_
> 
> |**Score**|**Adult version (aged 5+)**|**Children's modification**|**Grimace response for preverbal or intubated patients**|
> |---|---|---|---|
> |**1**|No verbal response|No vocal response|No response to pain|
> |**2**|Incomprehensible sounds|Occasionally whimpers and/or moans|Mild grimace to pain|

> |**4**|Confused|Less than usual ability and/or spontaneous irritable cry|Less than usual spontaneous ability or only response to touch stimuli|
> |**5**|Orientated|Alert, babbles, coos, words or sentences to usual ability|Spontaneous normal facial/oromotor activity|
> 
> **Best motor response**
> 
> |**Score**|**Description**|
> |---|---|
> |**1**|No motor response to pain|
> |**2**|Abnormal extension to pain|
> |**3**|Abnormal flexion to pain|
> |**4**|Withdrawal to painful stimuli|
> |**5**|Localities to painful stimuli or withdraws to touch*|
> |**6**|Obeys commands or performs normal spontaneous movement|

- **Pupils** - size, symmetry and reactivity to light
- **Brief neurological assessment** - moving all limbs, looking for signs of focal neurology
- Posture - seriously ill child may be hypotonic.


#### Investigations

- **Blood glucose** (4.0-5.8 mmol/L) and **ketones** (>3mmol/L)
- CT head if intracranial pathology is suspected

#### Interventions

- Mannitol/dexamethasone for reducing ICP
- Correct hypoglycaemia  - IV or oral glucose
- Treat seizure activity with benzodiazepines


## Exposure

#### Observation

- Ask if the patient is in pain anywhere
- **Temperature** (36°c-37.9°c)
- Review any further charts/patient notes

#### Exam

- **Top to toe assessment** - rashes, bruising, signs of infection, distension, erythema, surgical wounds, active bleeding, catheter output
- **Palpation** - abdomen, calves 


#### Investigations

- Cultures/swabs or potential infection source

#### Interventions

- Warm the patient - blankets & warmed fluids
- Treat infection
- Documentation/plan/handover to senior


### SBAR Communication
Method of communication to get all info necessary

**Situation** - who is the patient and where are they

**Background** - why where they admitted

**Assessment** - what's the current problem

**Recommendations** - what should be done? - get things ready and what might be needed
