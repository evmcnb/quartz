Inflammation of the bursa overlying the olecranon. 
![[z_attachments/Pasted image 20261016131120.png|488]]

## Causes/Factors

- Repetitive trauma - writers, students, plumbers, miners. Anyone leaning on elbows
- Direct trauma
- Infection - usually *Staph. aureus*. 
- [[Full/gout|gout]], [[Pearls/Rheumatoid Arthritis|Rheumatoid Arthritis]]
## Symptoms

- Swelling over the olecranon
- Septic cases may be more painful with systemic features

## Signs

- Fluctuant, well-circumscribed swelling at the posterior elbow
    - sometimes described as a 'golf ball on the end of the elbow'
- Tenderness and warmth over the bursa
- Fever and overlying skin changes suggest possible infection
- Tophi or joint effusions if due to gout or rheumatoid arthritis
## Investigations

- Not required if diagnosis is clear and patient is systemically well
- If septic bursitis, aspirate for gram stain, crystals and culture
- Consider FBC and CRP if uncertain

## Management

- **Non-septic**: conservative management with rest, ice, compression, and NSAIDs
- **Septic**: aspirate and treat empirically with oral flucloxacillin; consider admission for IV antibiotics if systemically unwell
- Avoid further trauma to the area; use elbow pads if needed
- Surgical drainage may be necessary if aspiration fails or infection persists
