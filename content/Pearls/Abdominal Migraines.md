Recurrent episodes of central abdominal pain. It tends to affect children but can also affect adults.

They most often occur in children aged 3-10 years and are more common in girls. Children with abdominal migraine are more likely to develop traditional migraine headaches as adults.

No underlying physical pathology will be present

### Presentation 

Abdominal migraine presents with episodes of periumbilical abdominal pain lasting between one hour and three days.

There may be associated:
- Nausea and vomiting
- Anorexia (loss of appetite)
- Pallor
- Headache
- Photophobia
- Aura (e.g., visual changes or a tingling sensation in the hands)


### Management 

Similar to a traditional [[Pearls/Migraine]]
