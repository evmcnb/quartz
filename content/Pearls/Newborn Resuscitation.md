Hypoxia is the key concern in newborn resuscitation. Normal labour and birth involve a certain amount of fetal hypoxia. During contractions gas exchange is reduced in the placenta - **aim for a gradual rise in oxygen saturation not exceeding 95%**

The [[Pearls/Apgar Score|Apgar Score]] is calculated 1, 5 and 10 minutes after birth. It assesses the overall well-being and progress of the baby in the immediate post-natal period. 


### Principles of Neonatal Resuscitation

**Dry and warm the newborn as quickly as possible** 

Support breathing if necessary: 
- Stimulating the newborn - drying with a towel
- Placing their head in a neutral position 
- Check for airway obstruction (meconium) and consider aspiration under direct visualisation

Inflation breaths are given when the neonate is gasping or not breathing despite adequate stimulation. 
- 5 breaths (2-3 seconds each) are given
- If no heart rate response or chest movement, recheck mask and technique and give 5 more
- If heart rate remains low continuous ventilation breaths can be used - 1 every 2 seconds (30 per min, 1 second inflation time)
- Look for more airway management options, ET , LMA
- Additional oxygen up to 30% FiO2 can be given

**Chest compressions** are started if the heart rate remains below 60 despite resuscitation and inflation breaths. 3:1 ratio with ventilation breaths
