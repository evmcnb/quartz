Patients present with confusion, headaches and seizures.

Almost always due to having too much water relative to the amount of sodium in the blood. Need to ascertain:
1. What is the serum osmolality - is it genuine hyponatremia
2. Whats the volume status - if its genuine how much fluid in the body


> [!danger] Pseudohyponatremia
> The blood concentration is completely normal. The hyponatremia is fake. Happens when there are extremely high levels of proteins or lipids in the blood. 
> 
> 

Similarly for **hypertonic hyponatremia**, the blood is highly concentrated due to something else pulling water into the blood - usually glucose. This extra water dilutes the sodium that was already there

### Hypotonic hyponatremia

#### Hypovolaemic 

The body has lost both water and sodium, but **more** sodium than water. Patient would look dry 

- Renal causes - diuretics
- Severe vomiting, diarrhoea, excessive sweating, extensive burns

#### Hypervolemic 

The body has retained both water and sodium but has held onto more water than sodium. Patient would look wet - swelling, oedema

- Heart failure, liver cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome. Loss of fluid out of the blood vessels causes ADH stimulation causing a dilutionary effect


#### Euvolemic

The total body water is high, but the total body sodium is normal. The patient looks normal

- [[Pearls/Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone|Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone]] 
- Severe hypothyroidism, adrenal insufficiency (low cortisol) or psychogenic polydipsia


### Drugs

PPI TACO:

* PPIs
* Thiazides
* ACEi/ARBs
* [[Pearls/Carbamazepine|Carbamazepine]]
* Opioids


Other common drug causes of low sodium include: chlorpropramide, selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) [[Full/Antidepressants|Antidepressants]], tricyclic antidepressants, [[Pearls/Lithium|Lithium]], MDMA/ecstasy, tramadol, haloperidol, vincristine, desmopressin, fluphenazine
