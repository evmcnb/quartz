When blood flows from the right to the left side of the heart across a structural heart lesion, bypassing the lungs: 
- Atrial septal defect
- Ventricular septal defect
- Patent ductus arteriosus 

### Pathophysiology

1. Patient starts off with the left ventricle being stronger than the right so blood flows from **left to right**. Blood still travels to the lungs and is oxygenated $\rightarrow$ no cyanosis
2. Over time the extra blood flowing into the RV causes pulmonary hypertension. When the pulmonary pressure exceeds the systemic pressure the blood flows from **right to left**. 
3. Deoxygenated blood bypasses the lungs and enters the body causing cyanosis

### Exam findings

- Right ventricular heave
- Loud P2 (forceful shutting of pulmonary valve)
- Signs of right HF - raised JVP + peripheral oedema
- Cyanosis
- Finger clubbing (chronic hypoxia)
- Dyspnoea
- Plethoric complexion (from polycythaemia)

### Management

Depends on the underlying condition

Management of complications may involve:
- **_Oxygen_** for **_shortness of breath_**
- **_Sildenafil_** and **_bosentan_** for **_pulmonary hypertension_**
- **_Diuretics_** for **_oedema_**
- **_Anti-arrhythmic drugs_** for **_arrhythmia_**
- **_Venesection_** for **_polycythaemia_**
- **_Anticoagulation_** for **_thrombosis_**
- **_Antibiotics_** for **_infective endocarditis_**

#### Prognosis

Eisenmenger syndrome reduces life expectancy by around 20 years compared with healthy individuals. The main causes of death are heart failure, infection, thromboembolism and haemorrhage. The mortality can be up to 50% in pregnancy.
