When there is an acute stress to the body which it cannot produce an adequate response to:
- Sepsis on background of [[Pearls/Addison's Disease|adrenal insufficiency]]
- Surgery on background of [[Pearls/Addison's Disease|adrenal insufficiency]]
- Steroid withdrawal (most common precipitant)
- Inadequate sick day rule management in patents with underlying adrenal insufficiency

**Presents with:** 
- Confusion, syncope, lethargy, convulsions
- Hypotension, tachycardia, hypothermia
- **Hyponatraemia**, **hyperkalaemia**, **hypoglycaemia** 
- Abdominal pain

**Treat with:**
- Hydrocortisone 100mg IM or IV followed by 200mg per 24 hours as a continuous infusion (or 50mg IV/IM every 6 hours). Taper according to response
- 1L normal saline over 30-60 minutes, followed by a further 4-6L over the first 24 hours. Include dextrose if hypoglycemic. 
- Monitor electrolytes and fluid balance

No fludrocortisone is required acutely as high-dose cortisol exerts a weak mineralocorticoid effect 

Once stable convert to oral replacement after 24 hours. Reducing to maintenance dose over 3-4 days. 
- Identify and treat the precipitating cause
- Educate patients and encourage use of steroid alert cards
