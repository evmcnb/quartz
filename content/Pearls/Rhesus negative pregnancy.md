The D antigen is the most important rhesus antigen
- Around 15% are rhesus negative (Rh -ve)
- If a Rh -ve mother delivers a Rh +ve child, and a leak of fetal blood occurs, anti-D IgG antibodies form in the mother
- If a subsequent pregnancy is also Rh +ve, these antibodies can cross the placenta and cause haemolysis in the fetus
- This can also occur in the first pregnancy but rarely.

Nothing is needed for Rh +ve women. 

Prevention of sensitisation is the mainstay of treatment. This involves giving IM anti-D injections to Rh -ve women. The immunoglobulin attaches itself to the Rh-D antigens on the fetal RBCs in the mother's circulation, causing them to be destroyed. This is prophylaxis - once sensitised this cannot be reversed.
- Test for D antibodies in all Rh -ve mothers at booking
- Giving anti-D to non-sensitised Rh -ve mothers at **28** and **34** weeks (but little difference in efficacy between single-dose at 28 weeks and double dose.)

Anti-D Ig should be given STAT (always within 72 hours) in the following situations:
- Delivery of Rh +ve infant whether live or stillborn
- Any termination of pregnancy
- Miscarriage if gestation is >12 weeks
- Ectopic pregnancy managed surgically. 
- External cephalic version
- Antepartum haemorrhage
- Amniocentesis, chorionic villus sampling, fetal blood sampling
- Abdominal trauma

**All babies born to Rh -ve mother should have cord blood taken at delivery for FBC, blood group and direct [[Pearls/Coombs test]]**

**Kleihauer test** - checks how much fetal blood has passed into the mother's blood during a sensitisation event. Used after any sensitising event past 20 weeks gestation. Add acid to maternal blood, fetal cells are resistant
