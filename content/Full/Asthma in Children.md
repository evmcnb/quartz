Smooth muscle hypersensitivity (bronchoconstriction) revsible with bronchodilators such as salbutamol.

Seen commonly in [[Pearls/Atopic individuals|Atopic individuals]]

For more see [[Full/asthma|asthma in adults]]

## Causes/Factors

- Viral infections
- Exercise
- Animals
- Cold, damp or dusty air
- Strong emotions

- In some people, NSAIDs
- Beta blockers
## Presentation

Episodic - diurnal variability, worse at night. Typical sypmtoms: 
- SoB
- Chest tightness
- Dry cough
- Wheeze

Symptoms should improve with bronchodilators. No response reduces the likelihood of asthma

Generally normal respiratory exam when the patient is well. But a key finding in a ***widespread polyphonic expiratory wheeze***

## Investigations

Adults and children over 16:
- Initially **_FeNO_** (>50 ppb) or **_eosinophil count_**. **_Smoking_** can lower the FeNO, making the results unreliable
- Then **_reversibility testing with spirometry_** - >12% increase in FEV1 supports diagnosis
- Then **_peak flow diary twice daily for 2 weeks_** - >20% variability supports diagnosis
- Then **_bronchial challenge test_**. Opposite of reversibility testing. Inhaled histamine to stimulate bronchoconstriction. 

Children aged 5-16:
- Initially **_FeNO_** (>35 ppb)
- Then **_reversibility testing with spirometry_**
- Then **_peak flow diary twice daily for 2 weeks_**
- Then **_skin prick testing to house dust mite_** or **_bloods for total IgE and eosinophil count_**

Children under 5 with suspected asthma are treated until they are 5 years old, after which objective tests are performed. This involves an 8-12 week trial of a regular inhaled corticosteroid to see if symptoms resolve during this trial. Children in this age group often have [[Pearls/Pre-school wheeze in children]] rather than asthma.
## Management

#### Children under 5

Initial management:
- Regular low-dose ICS 
- Short acting B2 agonist - salbutamol PRN 

Treatment escalation: 
1. Regular moderate dose ICS
2. Oral leukotriene receptor antagonist (montelukast)

#### Children 5-11

Same initial management as above

If asthma remains uncontrolled, choice between MART therapy (see [[Pearls/Asthma Pharmacology|here]]) or conventional pathway. 

#### Children 12+

Same as the treatment steps in [[Full/asthma|adults]]: 
1. AIR therapy
2. Low-dose MART
3. Moderate-dose MART
4. Specialist referral if FeNO or eosinophils are raised at this stage
5. Add **_leukotriene receptor antagonist_** or **_LAMA_** (8-12 week trial and switch if not helping)
6. Specialist referral


> [!warning] Inhaled Corticosteroids and Growth 
> Some evidence that steroids can very slightly reduce growth velocity which can cause a slight reduction in height. The effect is dose dependent 


### Inhaler Technique

**_Dry powder inhalers_** require the patient to inhale quickly and deeply to draw the powder into the lungs.

For a typical salbutamol inhaler; 

MDI technique without a spacer:
- Remove the cap
- Shake the inhaler (depending on the type)
- Sit or stand up straight
- Lift the chin slightly
- Fully exhale
- Make a tight seal around the inhaler between the lips
- Take a steady breath in whilst pressing the canister
- Continue breathing in for 3 – 4 seconds after pressing the canister
- Hold the breath for 10 seconds (or as long as comfortably possible)
- Wait 30 seconds before any further doses
- *Rinse the mouth after using a steroid inhaler*

MDI technique with a spacer:
- Assemble the spacer
- Shake the inhaler (depending on the type)
- Attach the inhaler to the correct end
- Sit or stand up straight
- Lift the chin slightly
- Make a seal around the spacer mouthpiece or place the mask over the face
- Spray the dose into the spacer
- Take steady breaths in and out 5 times until the mist is fully inhaled

### Acute Management

#### Mild to Moderate

Bronchodilator therapy  
- give a beta-2 agonist via a spacer (for a child < 3 years use a close-fitting mask)
- give 1 puff every 30-60 seconds up to a maximum of 10 puffs
- if symptoms are not controlled repeat beta-2 agonist and refer to hospital

  
Steroid therapy  
- should be given to all children with an asthma exacerbation
- treatment should be given for 3-5 days

#### Severe to Life-threatening

Same principles as adults. 

1. Oxygen to maintain 94-98%
2. Nebulised salbutamol  
3. Nebulised ipratropium bromide

If not responding to treatment: 
4. IV magnesium sulphate
5. IV salbutamol
6. IV aminophylline

Low threshold to intubate a patient with life-threatening asthma. 

Discharge is considered when the patient is stable on four hourly inhalers.
