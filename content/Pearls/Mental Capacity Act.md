  1. A person is assumed to have capacity
  2. All practical steps must be taken to enable decision making
  3. People can make unwise decisions
  4. Decisions we make on behalf of people who lack capacity must be in their best interests
  5. Decisions we make on behalf of people who lack capacity  must be the least restrictive option


### Assessment of Capacity

1. He or she has an 'impairment of, or disturbance in, the functioning of the mind or  
brain' whether permanent or temporary **AND**  
  
2. He or she is unable to undertake any of the following:
- a. understand the information relevant to the decision
- b. retain that information
- c. use or weigh that information as part of the process of making the decision
- d. communicate the decision made by talking, sign language or other means


### Best Interests

The following should be considered when assessing what is in someone's best interests:  
- 1. Whether the person is likely to regain capacity and can the decision wait.
- 2. How to encourage and optimise the participation of the person in the decision.
- 3. The past and present wishes, feelings, beliefs, values of the person and any other relevant factors
- 4. Views of other relevant people

### Lasting Power of Attorney

- People can appoint someone to act on their behalf in the event they should lose capacity 
- The attorney only has the authority to make decisions about life-sustaining treatment if the LPA specifies that. Before it can be used an LPA must be registered with the Office of the Public Guardian

Two types:
- Health and welfare
- Property and finance

### Advance Directives

- Can be done by anyone will capacity
- Only to refuse treatments if they lost capacity
- Must lay out the specific circumstances
- May be made verbally **unless** its refusing life sustaining treatment where it needs to be written signed and witnessed
- Cannot demand treatment
- Lasting Power of Attorney has precedence over the written advance decision.

### Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS)

When someone lacks capacity and its in their best interest for someone to stay in hospital/care home
- The MHA must not be more appropriate
- Basically locking the door/not telling them the code
- Must be over 18
- Usually for disorders that cannot be treated.
