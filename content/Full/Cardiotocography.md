Used to measure the fetal heart rate (FHR) and the contractions of the uterus. Two transducers are used
- One above the fetal heart to monitor FHR
- One near the fundus of the uterus to monitor the uterine contractions 

![[z_attachments/Pasted image 20251230140515.png]]



To interpret a CTG you need a structured method of assessing its various characteristics. The most popular structure can be remembered using the acronym **DR C BRAVADO**:

- **DR:** Define risk
- **C:** Contractions
- **BRa:** Baseline rate
- **V:** Variability
- **A:** Accelerations
- **D:** Decelerations
- **O:** Overall impression

## Define Risk#

You need to determine if the pregnancy is high or low risk [[Full/Pregnancy#Risk|Pregnancy]]. This puts everything else into context. Main ones (that may have developed since the start of pregnancy)
- [[Full/Pre-eclampsia]]
- Meconium-stained liqour (sign of fetal distress)
- Needing oxytocin to induce/progress


## Contractions

Determine the number of contractions in a 10 minute period: 
- Each big square is equal to one minute - distance between numbers is 10 minutes
- Uterine contractions are shown on the lower trace

Assess how strong these contractions are, use the CTG to look at contraction length

Aiming for 4-5 contractions in 10 minutes
- Greater than that indicates uterine hyperstimulation 
- Can lead to an abnormal FHR


## Baseline Rate

The baseline rate is the average heart rate within a 10-minute window

A normal heart rate is between 110-160. To say the FHR is stable, need to look at previous 1 hour

#### Fetal Tachycardia

Can be caused by: 
- Fetal hypoxia
- Chorioamnionitis - check maternal temperature
- Chronic causes: hyperthyroidism, anaemia

#### Fetal Bradycardia 

Common to have a baseline HR of between 100-120 whem:
- Postdate gestation
- Occiput posterior or transverse presentations 

##### Severe bradycardia

Less than 80 bpm for more than 3 minute. Rule for management
- **3** - Call for help
- **6** - Move to theatre
- **9** - Prep for delivery
- **12** - aim to deliver the baby

Causes prolonged severe bradycardia
- Prolonged cord compression
- Cord prolapse
- Epidural/spinal anaesthesia
- Maternal seizures
- Rapid fetal descent

## Variability

Beat to beat variability. Normal range of 5-25 BPM.

Normal variability indicates intact neurological function. 

Decreased variability may occur during: 
- Fetal sleep (max 40 mins)
- Fetal hypoxia
- Drugs; mag sulphate, opiate 

### Sinusoidal pattern

Rare but very concerning. Associated with rates of high fetal mortality. Usually indicates:
 - Severe fetal hypoxia
 - Severe fetal anaemia 
 - Fetal/maternal haemorrhage 

## Accelerations 

Increase of FHR by 15 bpm for >15 seconds. **A sign of a healthy fetus**
- Accelerations occurs alongside uterine contractions is a sign of a healthy fetus. 
- Absence of accelerations with an otherwise normal CTG is uncertain significance 

## Decelerations

An abrupt decrease in the FHR of greater than 15 bpm for greater than 15 seconds

###### Pathophysiology
- FHR controlled by the autonomic and somatic nervous system. 
- In response to hypoxic stress the fetus reduces its HR to preserve myocardial oxygenation and perfusion (as it cannot increase its RR)
### Early deceleration

Trough of deceleration coincides with peak of contraction
- Secondary to head compression of the fetus
- Increased ICP leads to increased vagal tone and decrease heart rate during the contraction 
- Physiological not pathological

### Variable deceleration 

A rapid fall in baseline fetal heart rate with a variable recovery phase
- Most common type of deceleration 
- Variable in duration
- May not have any relationship with uterine contractions

Usually caused by umbilical cord compression. Umbilical cord comprised of 1 umbilical vein and 2 umbilical arteries: 
1. When pressure is applied on the cord, the vein is occluded first causing a spike in FHR
2. When the artery is then occluded, the FHR decelerates 
3. When pressure is then released another acceleration occurs before returning to baseline 
4. **Leads to "shouldering" of the deceleration** 

#### Concerning characteristics 
- Lasting >60 seconds
- Biphasic shape (trace looks like a W)
- No shouldering (lack of peaks either side of the deceleration)
- Reduced baseline variability within the deceleration 
- Failure/slow to return to baseline

### Late deceleration

**Begin** at the **peak** of the uterine contraction and recover **after the contraction ends**. This indicates that there is insufficient blood flow to the uterus and placenta. 

Causes include: 
- Maternal hypotension 
- [[Full/Pre-eclampsia]]
- Uterine hyperstimulation 

### Pro-longed deceleration

A deceleration that lasts longer than 2 minutes
- Between 2-3 minutes its non-reassuring 
- Longer than 3 mins its abnormal

	- Maternal hypotension $\rightarrow$ IV fluids
	- Uterine hyperstimulation $\rightarrow$ stop oxytocin

Considerations after a pathological CTG: 
- Fetal scalp stimulation - firm pressure on the head looking for acceleration to be reassured
- Consider expediting delivery
