The 3 major causes:
- [[Pearls/Bleeding in pregnancy|Obstetric haemorrhage]]
- [[Full/pulmonary embolism|pulmonary embolism]]
- [[Full/Sepsis|Sepsis]] leading to metabolic acidosis and septic shock 

Other important cause is:

##### Aortocaval Compression

After 20 weeks the uterus is large. When a pregnant woman lies on her back, the mass of the uterus can compress the IVC, reducing venous return. This reduces the cardiac output. In osme instances this can be enough to cause cardiac arrest. 

As the vena cava is on the slight right side of the body, the solution is to place the woman in the left lateral position. 


### Resuscitation in Pregnancy

Resuscitation in pregnancy follows the same principles as standard adult life support, except for:
- A **_15 degree tilt_** to the **_left side_** for CPR, to relieve compression of the inferior vena cava and aorta
- Early intubation to protect the airway
- Early supplementary oxygen
- Aggressive fluid resuscitation (caution in pre-eclampsia)
- **Delivery of the baby after 4 minutes, and within 5 minutes of starting CPR**

The primary reason for immediate delivery is to **improve the survival of the mother**. It helps with: 
- Venous return
- Decreases oxygen consumption
- Improves ventilation
- Improves chest compressions
