> [!danger] Criteria for detention
> Must be suffering form a MENTAL HEALTH DISORDER of a nature or degree warranting admission
> ***and***
> Must be a RISK to their own health and/or safety and/or a risk to the safety of others
> ***and*** 
> Must be UNWILLING TO BE ADMITTED voluntarily, or LACK THE  CAPACITY to consent to an admission
> 
> 
> The person must have an allocated bed, **not in A&E** before being detained. 

Used for someone who will not be admitted voluntarily. Patients who are under the influence of alcohol or drugs are **specifically excluded**

> [!warning] Appeals/Discharge Process for Section 2&3
> The patient can appeal to hospital managers or mental health tribunal.
> 
> The nearest relative can apply for the person to be discharged from their section. The only reason this can be denied is if the person is actively dangerous. 

#### Section 2
*****Compulsory admission for assessment*** **

- Admission for assessment for up to 28 days - not renewable
- an Approved Mental Health Professional or rarely the nearest relative makes the application on the recommendation of 2 doctors
- Need 2 doctors and AMHP
- One of the doctors should be approved under Section 12(2) of the MHA (usually a consultant psychiatrist)
- Treatment can be given against a patient's wishes
- Can be appealed by the patient 

#### Section 3 
***Compulsory admission for treatment*** 

- Admission for treatment for up to 6 months - can be renewed
- AMHP along with 2 doctors, both of which must have seen the patient within the last 24 hours
- Treatment can be given against a patient's wish
- Can be appealed 
- Must be discussed with nearest relative

#### Section 4

- 72 hour assessment order
- used as an emergency when a Section 2 would involve an unacceptable delay
- a GP and an AMHP or NR required
- Often changed to a Section 2 upon arrival at hospital

#### Section 5

Just a holding power. No rights to treatment. If not accepting treatment, assess [[Mental Capacity Act|capacity]]. No right to appeal. **Cannot be used in A&E**. Need to get a MHA assessment from psychiatry within the 72 hours. 

- **Section 5(2)**: a patient who is a voluntary patient in hospital can be legally detained by a single doctor for 72 hours 
- **Section 5(4)**: a patient who is a voluntary patient in hospital can be legally detained by a single mental health nurse for 6 hours 

#### Section 135

- A court order can be obtained to allow the police to break into a property to remove a person to a Place of Safety

#### Section 136 

- Someone found in a public place who appears to have a mental disorder can be taken by the police to a Place of Safety (section 136 suite within a hospital)
- Can only be used for up to 24 hours 
- Cannot enforce treatment 

### Nearest Relative

When sectioned the "nearest relative" gains special legal rights when detained. 

When there are multiple at the same level, the older person takes priority. To change the NR need to go to court.

1. Husband, wife 
2. Civil partner or cohabitee of more than 6 months
3. Son or daughter
4. Father or mother, but an unmarried father must have parental responsibility
5. Brother or sister
6. Grandparent
7. Grandchild
8. Uncle or aunt
9. Nephew or niece

A nearest relative must also **meet both of the following criteria:**
- They must be over 18, unless they're your mother, father, husband, wife or civil partner.
- They must live in the UK, Channel Islands or the Isle of Man, unless you normally live abroad with them too.

They have the right to:
- ask for a Mental Health Act assessment
- apply to the hospital managers for admission to hospital
- get certain information
- be consulted by the approved mental health professional (AMHP)
- ask for you to be discharged
- get notice of your discharge from the hospital
- delegate their NR rights to someone else
- be told about renewal of your detention, extension of your CTO, and if you’re being transferred from one hospital to another
- contact the Independent Mental Health Advocacy service (IMHA)
