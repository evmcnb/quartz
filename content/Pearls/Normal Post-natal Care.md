#### Immediate

- Skin-to-skin contact with the mother
- Delayed cord clamping (60 seconds)
- Dried and kept warm
- Feeding as soon as the baby is alert enough
- Vitamin K infection (subject to parental consent) 
- Measured for their birthweight 
- Label with name and details


#### Further care

- [[Full/Newborn infant physical examination]] within 72 hours and at 6-8 weeks
- [[Pearls/Newborn Blood Spot Screening Test|Newborn Blood Spot Screening Test]] on day 5 
- Newborn hearing test within the first few weeks
- Safe sleeping advice - cot in the same bedroom, sleeping on their back, avoiding smoking
- Umbilical cord care - keep clean and dry and wait for it to fall off naturally 
- Midwife home visits
- Health visitor input after being discharged by the midwives


### Vitamin K

Essential for normal clotting, Newborns have low vitamin K (slow to cross the placenta and insufficient amounts in breath milk). Injection (or oral) vitamin K reduces the risk of bleeding - bruising, umbilical stump bleeding and intracranial haemorrhage 

If oral 3 doses are given at birth, 4-7 days and 4-6 weeks.


### Maternal Care

- A routine 6 week appointment is offered to the mothers to check how they are doing. Usually done at the same time as the 6 week newborn baby check

#### Menstruation After Delivery

- Shortly after birth there will be vaginal bleeding - blood, endometrial tissue and mucus called lochia. Tampons should be avoided in this period. Should settle within 6 weeks
- Breast feeding released oxytocin which can cause the uterus to contract. This is normal 
- Breastfeeding women may not have regular menstrual period for 6 months or longer - lactational amenorrhea
- Bottle-feeding women will begin having period from 3 weeks onwards
- Cabergoline can be used to suppress lactation (dopamine agonist)

#### Contraception After Childbirth

- Fertility is not considered to return until 21 days after giving birth. Contraception is not required up to this point
- Lactational amenorrhea is 98% effective as contraception for up to 6 months after birth - must be fully breastfeeding and amenorrhoeic
- Progestogen only pill and implant are considered safe in breastfeeding and can be started at any time after birth
- COCP should not be started before 6 weeks after childbirth in women that are breastfeeding. 
- COCP should not be used in the first 21 days due to increased [[Pearls/VTE in Pregnancy|VTE]] risk
- IUD or IUS can be inserted within 48 hours of childbirth or after 4 weeks
