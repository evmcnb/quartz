Start off with positional changes: 
- Head-tilt chin-lift 
- Jaw thrust if concern around C-spine injury 

| Device                    | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Oropharyngeal airway**  | - Easy to insert and use<br>- Gag reflexes absent<br>- Most often used as a bridge to more definitive airway<br>- Incisors to angle of mandible. Put in upside down and twist round in adults (not in children)                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Nasopharyngeal airway** | - Easy to insert<br>- Patient can be partially conscious with reflexes to tolerate<br>- Contra-indicated in basal skull fractures<br>- Size via distance from lateral nostril to tragus                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Laryngeal mask/iGel**   | - Widely used<br>- Very easy to insert<br>- Device sits in pharynx and aligns to cover the airway<br>- Poor control against reflux of gastric contents<br>- Paralysis is not usually required<br>- Commonly used for wide range of anaesthetic uses, especially in day surgery<br>- Not suitable for high-pressure ventilation (small amount of PEEP often possible)                          |
| **Endotracheal tube**     | - Provides optimal definitive control of the airway once the cuff inflated<br>- May be used for long or short-term ventilation<br>- Errors in insertion may result in oesophageal intubation<br>    - accounts for around 5% of major airway complications<br>    - therefore monitor end-tidal CO2 (capnography)<br>- Paralysis often required<br>- Higher ventilation pressures can be used |
| **Tracheostomy**          | - Usually for a last restore for a can't intubate can't ventilate patient<br>- Reduces the work of breathing (and dead space)<br>- May be useful in slow weaning<br>- Percutaneous tracheostomy widely used in ITU<br>- Dries secretions, humidified air usually required                                                                                                                     |
