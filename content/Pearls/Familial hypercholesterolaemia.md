Familial hypercholesterolaemia is an **_autosomal dominant_** genetic condition causing very high cholesterol levels. Several genes have the potential to cause the disorder.

**_Heterozygous_** occurs in about 1 in 250 people. Homozygous very rare and causes a very high cholesterol and guaranteed disease.

The **_Simon Broome criteria_** or the **_Dutch Lipid Clinic Network Criteria_** are used for making a **_clinical diagnosis_**. Three important features to remember are:
- **_Family history_** of premature cardiovascular disease (e.g., myocardial infarction under 60 in a first-degree relative)
- **_Very high cholesterol_** (e.g., above 7.5 mmol/L in an adult)
- **_Tendon xanthomata_** (hard nodules in the tendons containing cholesterol, often on the back of the hand and Achilles)
