A syndrome caused by a congenital accessory pathway between the atria and ventricles leading to AVRT (atrioventricular re-entry tachycardia)

![[z_attachments/WPW-Delta-wave-preexcitation.png|500]]

As the accessory pathway doesn't slow conduction, AF can degenerate into VF 

#### Presentation

- Typically seen in teenagers/young adults but can be at any age
- Palpitations - sudden onset and offset, often rapid and regular
- Light-headedness and pre-syncope 

##### ECG features

- Short PR
- Wide QRS complex with a slurred upstroke - delta wave
- Left axis deviation (if right sided accessory pathway, most common)

Type A - left sided pathway: dominant R wave in V1
Type B - right sided pathway: no dominant wave in V1

#### Associated with

- [[Pearls/Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy|Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy]]
- Mitral valve prolapse
- [[Pearls/Ebstein’s Anomaly|Ebstein’s Anomaly]]
- [[Full/Hyperthyroidism|Thyrotoxicosis]]
- Secundum ASD

#### Management 

Definitive treatment: radiofrequency ablation of accessory pathway

Medical therapy: **sotalol** (avoid if coexistent AF, prolonging the refractory period at AV node may increase rate of transmission through accessory pathway), **flecainide**
