> [!tldr] Vertigo
> Vertigo is the sensation of illusory movement. It arises when the labyrinth, vestibular nerve, or central vestibular tracts of the brain stem are dysfunctional. The aim of the exam is to distinguish between the peripheral causes and the central. 
> 
> Peripheral causes - much more common
> - [[Full/Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo|Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo]]
> - [[Pearls/Vestibular neuritis vs viral labyrinthitis|Vestibular neuritis/labyrinthitis]]
> - [[Full/Meniere's disease|Meniere's disease]]
>   
>   Central causes:
> - [[Full/Stroke|Stroke]] affecting the posterior circulation supplied vestibular structures (cerebellum, brainstem, or vestibular nuclei)
> - [[Pearls/Multiple Sclerosis|Multiple Sclerosis]]
> - Medication toxicity 
> 


## Introduction

- **Wash hands** (and don PPE if needed)
- **Introduce yourself** (name and role)
- Confirm patient's **name** and **DOB**
- Explain what the examination involves
- Gain consent to continue
- Ask if patient in any **pain** before continuing

## Head-impulse test

1. Gently move the patient's head side to side, making sure the neck muscles are relaxed
2. THen ask the patient to keep looking at your nose whilst you turn their head
3. Turn the patient's head 10-20 degrees to each side rapidly and then back to the mid-point

![[z_attachments/Pasted image 20261017172721.png]]
A positive test move with the head then saccade rapidly back to the point of fixation. Indicates a disruption to the vestibulo-ocular reflex. Patients will also have difficulty fixating on the clinician’s nose

If there is a **corrective saccade** (a positive head-impulse test) this is **reassuring** that the pathology is most likely a problem with the **vestibulocochlear nerve** on the ipsilateral side – that is, it is **peripheral** and not central.

If negative with symptoms, supportive of a central cause

## Nystagmus

1. Observe the patient's primary gaze while they look straight ahead
2. Then ask the patient to look left and right without fixating on any object

Unidirectional and horizontal indicates a peripheral cause (including rotary? for [[Full/Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo|Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo]]). Horizontal with direction change or torsional indicates central  

**Bidirectional nystagmus**, in particular, is highly specific for **stroke**

## Test of skew

1. Ask the patient to look at your nose and subsequently cover one of their eyes
2. Then quickly move your hand to cover the patient's other eye. Obsere the unconvered eye for any vertical/diagonal corrective movement
3. Repeat with other eye

Any abnormal movement is highly specific for a central cause of vertigo

## To Complete the Exam

- **Explain** to the patient that the examination is now **finished**.
- **Thank the patient** for their time.
- **Dispose of PPE** appropriately and **wash your hands**.
- **Summarise** your findings.
