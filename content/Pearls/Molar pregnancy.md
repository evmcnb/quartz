A hydatidiform mole is a type of tumour that grows like a pregnancy inside the uterus
- A complete mole occurs when 2 sperm cells fertilise an ovum that contains no genetic material. The cell has 2 sets of paternal chromosomes. No fetal material will form
- A partial mole occurs when 2 sperm cells fertilise a normal ovum. The cell will have 3 sets of chromosomes. Some fetal material may form

### Diagnosis

Molar pregnancy behaves like a normal pregnancy but:
- More severe morning sickness
- Vaginal bleeding
- Increased enlargement of the uterus
- Abnormally high b-hCG. The structure of the hormone is similar to TSH, so causes:
	- [[Full/Hyperthyroidism|Thyrotoxicosis]] (high T3/4, low TSH)

Ultrasound can show a characteristic **snowstorm appearance**

Need to evacuate the uterus and the products of conception need to be sent for histological examination 

Occasionally the mole can metastasise and the patient may require systematic chemotherapy
