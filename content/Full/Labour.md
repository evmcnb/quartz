Normally occurs between 37 and 42 weeks gestation

3 stages:
1. From the onset of labour (true contractions) until 10cm cervical dilation (fully dilated)
2. From 10cm cervical dilation until delivery of the baby
3. From the delivery of the baby until delivery of the placenta 

##### Monitoring
- FHR every 15 mins or continuously via CTG
- Contractions assessed every 30 mins
- Maternal pulse assessed every 60 min
- Maternal BP and temp every 4 hours 
- Vaginal exam should be offered every 4 hours to check progression of labour
- Maternal urine should be checked for ketones and protein every 4 hours

### Stage 1

The signs of labour are:
- **_Show_** (mucus plug from the cervix)
- **_Rupture of membranes_**
- **_Regular_**, **_painful_** contractions
- **_Dilating cervix_** on examination


In a primigravida last typically 10-16 hours
- Latent phase - 0-3 cm dilation normally takes 6 hours
- Active phase - 3-10cm dilation, normally 1cm/hr 


#### Partogram
Women are monitored for their progress in the first stage of labour using a partogram. 

![[z_attachments/Pasted image 20251230111301.png]]

It records: 
- Cervical dilation (measured by 4 hourly vaginal exam)
- Descent of the fetal head  in relation to the ischial spines
- Maternal pulse, BP, temp and UO
- FHR
- Frequency of contractions
- Status of the membranes - presence of liquor, stained by blood or meconium
- Drugs and fluids that have been given 

Uterine contractions are measured in contractions per 10 mins. "2 in 10" means 2 contractions in 10 mins

When the cervix takes too long to dilate, the readings will cross the right of the alert and action lines
- **Crossing the alert line** is an indication for amniotomy (artificially rupturing the membranes) and repeat examination in 2 hours
- **Crossing the action line** means care needs to be escalated to obstetric led for appropriate action

### Stage 2

**Passive second stage** - in the absence of pushing
**Active second stage** - active process of maternal pushing 

- Lasts approximately 1 hour
- If longer than 1 hour (can be left longer if epidural) consider ventouse extraction, forceps delivery or C-section
- Episiotomy may be necessary following crowning 
- Associated with transient fetal bradycardia 

#### Failure to Progress

More likely to occur in women in labour for the first time. Progress is influenced by the 3 Ps:
- Power - uterine contractions
- Passenger - size, presentation and position of the baby
- Passage - the shape and size of the pelvis and soft tissues

Delay is when the active second stage lasts over: 
- 2 hours in a nullparous woman
- 1 hour in a multiparous woman 

**Size** refers to the size of the baby. Large babies will be more difficult to deliver and may be issues such as shoulder dystocia. The size of the head is the largest part of the fetus. 

**Attitude** refers to the posture of the fetus. How the back is rounded how the head and limbs are flexed

**Lie** refers to the position of the fetus in relation to the mother's body
- Longitudinal lie – the fetus is straight up and down
- Transverse lie – the fetus is straight side to side
- Oblique lie – the fetus is at an angle

**Presentation** refers to the part of the fetus closest to the cervix
- Cephalic presentation – the head is first
- Shoulder presentation – the shoulder is first
- Breech presentation – the legs are first. This can be:
    - Complete breech – with hips and knees flexed (like doing a cannonball jump into a pool)
    - Frank breech – with hips flexed and knees extended, bottom first
    - Footling breech – with a foot hanging through the cervix

### Stage 3

**Physiological management** - when the placenta is delivered by maternal effort without medications or cord traction
**Active management** - a dose of IM oxytocin (10IU) to help the uterus contract and careful traction to the umbilical cord to guide the placenta out of the uterus and vagina

Active management shortens the 3rd stage and reduces risk of bleeding, but can be associated with nausea and vomiting 

Delay in the third stage is defined:
- More than 30 minutes with active management
- More than 60 minutes with physiological management


## Pain relief

Several options before labour to improve symptoms
- Understanding what to expect
- Having good support
- Being in a relaxed environment
- Changing position to stay comfortable
- Controlled breathing
- Water births may help some women
- TENS machines may be useful in the early stages of labour

#### Simple analgesia
- Paracetamol - frequently used 
- Codeine may be added
- Avoid NSAIDs

#### Gas and air (entonox)
- 50:50 mix of nitrous oxide and oxygen
- Used during contractions for short term pain relief
- Can cause lightheadedness nausea of sleepiness

#### Epidural 
- Catheter into the epidural space with combined local anaeshetic and fentanyl
- Very good pain relief during labour
- Some more adverse effects: headache after insertion, hypotension, **increased probability of instrumental delivery**



#### IM Pethidine or Diamorphine
- Opoids
- May help with anxiety and distress
- Can cause drowsiness or nausea
- Can cause respiratory depression in the neonate if given too close to birth - can make the first feed more difficult

#### PCA
- May be offered IV remifentanil - short acting opiate medication
- Requires careful monitoring and input from anaesthetist 
- Rescue medications prescribed
