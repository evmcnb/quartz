- **_Squamous cell carcinoma_** (5% – higher in areas of schistosomiasis)
- Rarer causes are adenocarcinoma (2%), sarcoma and small-cell carcinoma


Cancer arising from urothelium accounts for >90% of bladder

## Causes/Factors

- Age 60+
- Males
- Exposure to certain industrial chemicals - rubber, textile and dye industries
- Smoking
- FH
- Treatment for other cancers in pelvis - radiation from treatment can cause cancer

## Clinical Signs

###### Local Primary Tumour Effects

- **Painless haematuria**
- Frequency, urgency, dysuria
- Urinary obstruction
- Back pain

###### Effects of Distant Metastases

- Lungs
- [[Pearls/Bone|Bone]]
- Liver

###### Paraneoplastic Syndromes

Rare


> [!info] Referral Criteria
> 2WW for:
> - Aged >45 with unexplained visible haematuria without a UTI or persisting after UTI treatment
> - Aged >60 with microscopic haematuria **AND** dysuria **OR** raised WBC on FBC
>
 NICE also recommend considering a non-urgent referral in people >60 with recurrent unexplained UTIs

## Diagnostic Tests
- Cystoscopy - camera on thin tube to examine inside of the bladder and biopsy
- CT, MRI, ultrasound
- Urine cytology

### Staging 

TNM (tumour, nodes, mets)

Clear distinction between:
- Non-muscle invasive bladder cancer 
- Muscle-invasive bladder cancer - invading the muscle and beyond

**_Non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer_** includes:
- **_Tis_**/**_carcinoma in situ_**: cancer cells only affect the urothelium and are flat
- **_Ta_**: cancer only affecting the urothelium and projecting into the bladder
- **_T1_**: cancer invading the connective tissue layer beyond the urothelium, but not the muscle layer
**_Invasive bladder cancer_** includes **_T2 – 4_** and any **_lymph node_** or **_metastatic spread_**.

## Management

1. Surgery 
	- **_Transurethral resection of bladder tumour_** (**_TURBT_**) may be used for non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer. The involves removing the bladder tumour during a cystoscopy procedure.
	- Radical cystectomy - removal of the entire bladder. Urostomy with an ileal conduit is the most common options for draining urine afterwards
2. Intravesical chemotherapy - often used after a TURBT procedure to reduce the risk of recurrence
3. Radiation & other chemotherapy
4. Immunotherapy - giving the BCG vaccine into the bladder is thought to stimulate the immune system


### Surgical Outcomes

#### Urostomy

#### Continent Urinary Diversion

A thin tube is connected between a stoma on the skin and the internal pouch. Urine does not drain from the stoma (unlike a urostomy), and the patient needs to intermittently insert a catheter into the stoma to drain urine from the pouch.

![[z_attachments/MACD097_Continent-Urinary-Diversion_labelled_20220818.jpg|400]]

#### Neobladder reconstruction

It may require intermittent catheterisation and bladder washouts to clear secretions from the small bowel tissue

![[z_attachments/neobladder_reconstruction-8col.webp|500]]

#### Ureterosigmoidostomy

This used to be used more often but is very rarely done now. It is associated with infection in the kidneys, electrolyte imbalances and secondary cancer at the anastomosis (join) between the ureters and sigmoid colon.

## Complications/red Flags
- Metastasis
- Recurrence
- Bladder dysfunction
