Psychiatric conditions involving an unhealthy and distorted obsession with body image and food. Main types:
- Anorexia nervosa
- Bulimia nervosa
- Binge eating disorder

More common in young females


> [!info] SCOFF Questionnaire
> 2 or more of the following have a 92% sensitivity of an ED: 
> - Do you make yourself **S**ick because you’re uncomfortably full?
>  - Do you worry you’ve lost **C**ontrol over how much you eat?
>  - Have you recently lost more than **O**ne stone in a 3-month period?
> - Do you believe yourself to be **F**at when others say you are too thin?
>  - Would you say that **F**ood dominates your life?


### Anorexia nervosa

The person feels overweight despite evidence of normal or low body weight. It involves **obsessively restricting calorie intake** to lose weight. Self-perception of being too fat leading to an overvalued idea (a less severe delusion)

Features include:
- Weight loss (15% below expected) - using diet pills/laxatives to limit absorption
- Amenorrhoea - disruption of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis (no LH or FSH production). May be primary or secondary depending on the age
- Lanugo hair - fine soft hair across most of the body
- Hypotension
- Hypothermia
- Mood changes
- Metabolic disturbances - Na+, K+, Ca++ $\rightarrow$ arrhythmias, kidney stones, fluid balance issues

Has the **highest mortality** of any psychiatric condition. Up to 5-20% of patients will eventually die because of the disorder despite treatment. 
- 50% recover
- 30% improve
- 20% becomes chronic illness

### Bulimia nervosa

Recurrent episodes of overeating (2+ per week for 3 months) followed by purging by inducing vomiting or taking laxatives to prevent the calories from being absorbed. Features:
- Erosion of teeth
- Swollen salivary glands
- Mouth ulcers
- [[Full/Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease|GORD]]
- Calluses on the knuckles where they have been scraped across the teeth (Russell's sign)
- Normal body weight that tends to fluctuate. 

Can be normal or overweight - fear of fatness 

### Binge eating disorder

Often as an expression of underlying psychological distress

The person typically feels a loss of control. Not a restrictive condition and the patients are likely to be overweight. 
- A planned binge involving "binge" foods
- Eating very quickly
- Unrelated to feelings of hunger
- Becoming uncomfortably full 
- Eating in a dazed state

## Management

Need to involve specialist services and MDT particularly with anorexia nervosa. Management in centred around changing behaviour and addressing environmental factors
- Weight gaining - 0.5kg/week in community
- CBT-ED (eating disorder focused CBT)
- Family therapy in children 
- Possible trial of fluoxetine 

Severe cases may require admission and observed refeeding
