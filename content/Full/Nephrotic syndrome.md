> [!success] Classic triad:
> Nephrotic syndrome is classically defined as a triad of:
> - Proteinuria (more than 3+ on dipstick)
> - Hypoalbuminaemia (< 25 g/L)
> - Oedema (face, legs or generalised)
> In children peak incidence of 2-5 years. Around 80% of cases are due to [[Full/Minimal change disease|Minimal change disease]]

## Causes/Factors

**Primary**
- **[[Full/Minimal change disease]]** - most common cause in children. Usually idiopathic and treated with steroids
- Membranous nephropathy
- Focal Segmental Glomerulosclerosis - most common cause in adults

**Secondary**
- [[Full/Diabetes Mellitus]]
- IgA vasculitis - ([[Pearls/Henoch-Schonlein purpura|Henoch-Schonlein purpura]])
- [[Full/Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]]
- Amyloid
- [[Full/HIV]], HEP B&C, malaria
- NSAIDs

> [!tip]
> [[Full/Minimal change disease]] often comes up in exams. If there's a 2-5 year old child with oedema proteinuria and low albumin the diagnosis is likely nephrotic syndrome. 

## Symptoms

- Proteinuria
- Hypoalbuminaemia
- Oedema - ankles, feet, eyes (in severe)

- Worse in the morning better later in the day
- Can have a proceeding immune trigger

+ Hypercholesterolaemia (as a response to the low albumin)
- Hyper-coagulability

## Signs

- High plood pressure
- Peripheral oedema
- Frothy urine
- Weight gain
- Fatigue
- Loss of appetite

## Diagnostic Tests

- Urinalysis - +++ protein
- Blood tests - [[Pearls/Urea to Creatinine Ratio|Urea to Creatinine Ratio]],  U&E, LFTs (albumin)
- Renal USS 
- Blood pressure 
- Renal biopsy

## Management

Depends on underlying cause

Specific to minimal change:

- Steroids
- Cyclophosphamide, tacrolimus

- Loop diuretics [[Pearls/Furosemide|Furosemide]]
- BP control - ACEi
- Hypercholesterolaemia - statins
- Thrombo-prophylaxis - heparin

## Complications/red Flags

- Thrombosis - due to loss of blood blotting proteins
- High blood cholesterol and elevated blood triglycerides - live makes more albumin along with cholesterol and triglycerides
- Poor nutrition - loss of blood protein
- High BP
- [[Full/Acute Kidney Injury]]
- [[Full/Chronic Kidney Disease]]
- Infections
