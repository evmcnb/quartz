Excessive volume of CSF within the ventricular system - imbalance between production and absorption

Symptoms due to $\uparrow$ ICP: 
- Headache
- Nausea and vomiting
- Papilloedema
- Coma (in severe cases)


> [!info] Hydrocephalus in infants
> Since their skull sutures are not fused, the increase in ICP will cause an increase in head circumference. The anterior fontanelle will bulge and become tense. 
> 
> With severe hydrocephalus children also present with failure of upward gaze due to compression of the superior colliculus of the midbrain $\rightarrow$ Sunset sign
> ![[z_attachments/Pasted image 20260309145500.png]]
> 
> Neonatal babies at higher risk. Delicate vessels which if they bleed into ventricles can block the arachnoid granulations 

Two categories of hydrocephalus:
- Obstructive (non-communicating) - due to a structural pathology blocking the flow of CSF. Dilation of the ventricles are seen superior to the site of obstruction. 
- Non-obstructive (communicating) - due to imbalance of CSF production/absorption. Usually failure of re-absorption from meningitis or post-haemorrhage 

[[Pearls/Normal pressure hydrocephalus]] is slightly different

**Investigations**
- CT head
- MRI for more detail/suspected underlying lesion
- LP diagnostic and therapeutic - measure opening pressure and drain fluid. Not to be used in obstructive hydrocephalus as the difference between cranial and spinal pressures will cause brain herniation


**Management**
- An external ventricular drain (EVD) is used in acute, severe hydrocephalus and is typically inserted into the right lateral ventricle and drains into a bag at the bedside
- A **ventriculoperitoneal** shunt (VPS) is a long-term CSF diversion technique that drains CSF from the ventricles to the peritoneum
- In obstructive hydrocephalus, the treatment may involve surgically treating the obstructing pathology
