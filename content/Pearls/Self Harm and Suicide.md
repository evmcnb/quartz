The cycle of self-harm involves the following six repeating steps:

1. **_Emotional suffering_**
2. **_Emotional overload_**
3. **_Panic_**
4. **_Self-harming_**
5. **_Temporary relief_**
6. **_Shame and guilt_**

Very hard to stratify risk, not very sensitive or specific

There are a number of factors shown to be associated with an increased risk of suicide  
- male sex
- history of deliberate self-harm
- alcohol or drug misuse
- history of [[Full/Depression|Depression]] of [[Full/Schizophrenia|Schizophrenia]]
    - depression
    - schizophrenia: NICE estimates that 10% of people with schizophrenia will complete suicide
- history of chronic disease
- advancing age
- unemployment or social isolation/living alone
- being unmarried, divorced or widowed

If a patient has actually attempted suicide, there are a number of factors associated with an increased risk of completed suicide at a future date:  
- efforts to avoid discovery
- planning
- leaving a written note
- final acts such as sorting out finances
- violent method

## Management

Subjective and based on many individual factors and judgement. There are no rules. Seek input. 

Safety-netting, safety plan and follow-up are important. Potential safeguarding issues to be considered. Involving others (with the patient's consent)

Patients may require immediate referral to A&E after a suicide attempt (physical injuries, overdoses, safety concerns)
