Most common STI (Chlamydia tharchomatis). Incubation period of 7-21 days, although a large percentage of cases are asymptomatic (70% of women and 50% of men)
- Women: cervicitis (discharge, bleeding), dysuria
- Men: urethral discharge, dysuria
- Rectal symptoms should always be investigated for lymphogranuloma venereum (varient of chlamydia)

**Investigation**
- Nuclear acid amplification tests (NAATs) 
- Women: vulvovaginal swab (can't diagnose from microscopy)
- Men: urine test (culutre needs to be swab)
- 3 site swab for MSM and those at risk

Chlamydia testing should be carried out two weeks after a possible exposure


**Management**:

Thinking 'CD', chlamydia: doxycycline:
- Doxycycline 7 days 
- Azithromycin (1g STAT) is an alternative
- If pregnant, azithromycin is first-line, erythromycin or amoxicillin alternatives

Patients with confirmed diagnosis should be contact traced. 
- For men with urethral symptoms, all contacts since and in the 4 weeks prior to the onset of symptoms
- For women and asymptomatic men, all partners from the last 6 months or the most recent sexual partner should be contacted
- Avoid sex until them and their partners have been treated

Contacts of confirmed cases should be offered treatment prior to the diagnostic results 

**Potential complications**
- [[Pearls/Epididymitis]]
- [[Pearls/Pelvic inflammatory disease]]
- [[Endometritis]]
- Increased chance of [[Full/Ectopic Pregnancy|Ectopic Pregnancy]]
- Infertility
- Reactive arthritis 

Screening program is open to all men and women aged 15-24
