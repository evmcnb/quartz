A severe epileptic encephalopathy of infancy
- 3-7 months of age
- More common in males
- Associated with underlying structural/genetic/metabolic disorder
- Poor prognosis 

Features  
- characteristic 'salaam' attacks: sudden flexion of the head, trunk and arms, sometimes followed by extension (looks like colic)
- spasms are brief (1–2 seconds) but occur in clusters, often dozens of episodes over several minutes
- developmental arrest or regression is typical

Investigation
- EEG
- MRI brain to show underlying cause ([[Pearls/Tuberous sclerosis]], cortical malformations, hypoxic-ischaemic injury)

Managed with vigabatrin combined with high-dose oral prednisolone for infantile spasms that are not due to [[Pearls/Tuberous sclerosis]]
