| Artery/Syndrome                                 | Location                                                                  | Key Deficits                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Mnemonic                                                                                                                                              |
| :---------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Anterior Cerebral Artery (ACA)**              | Supplies the medial aspect of the frontal and parietal lobes.             | - Leg weakness (contralateral)<br>- Sensory loss in the leg<br>- Cognitive changes (e.g., personality changes or executive function deficits).                                                                       | 'A for Anterior, A for 'Affects the Ankle' – Leg weakness and sensory loss primarily, with some cognitive deficits.'                                  |
| **Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA)**                | Supplies the lateral aspect of the frontal, temporal, and parietal lobes. | - Contralateral hemiparesis (mainly upper limb and face)<br>- Contralateral sensory loss (upper limb, face)<br>- Aphasia (if dominant hemisphere involved)<br>- Hemineglect (if non-dominant hemisphere involved)    | 'MCA = Motor and Communication affected.'<br>(Motor weakness in upper limb/face and Aphasia if dominant hemisphere is involved.)                      |
| **Posterior Cerebral Artery (PCA)**             | Supplies the occipital lobe and parts of the temporal lobe.               | - Visual disturbances (contralateral homonymous hemianopia)<br>- Memory problems (if hippocampus affected)<br>- Sometimes visual agnosia or alexia.                                                                  | 'PCA = Picture and Cognition'<br>(PCA affects Picture (vision) and can affect Cognition (memory).)                                                    |
| **Weber’s Syndrome**                            | Midbrain, typically due to a PCA or basilar artery occlusion.             | - Oculomotor nerve palsy (due to involvement of the third cranial nerve, so the eye may be 'down and out')<br>- Contralateral hemiparesis (due to involvement of the corticospinal tract)                            | 'Weber’s Wound to the Midbrain''<br>(The main symptoms are eye (oculomotor) problems and hemiparesis.)                                                |
| **Posterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery (PICA)** | Supplies the cerebellum and medulla.                                      | - Wallenberg syndrome (lateral medullary syndrome): loss of pain and temperature sensation on the **contralateral** body and **ipsilateral** face, dysphagia, hoarseness, vertigo, ataxia, and Horner's syndrome.    | 'PICA = Pain, Ipsilateral face, and Cerebellar Ataxia'<br>(PICA causes both sensory deficits and cerebellar signs.)<br><br>PICACHU - PICA CAN'T CHEW  |
| **Anterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery (AICA)**  | Supplies the cerebellum, pons, and part of the inner ear.                 | - Lateral pontine syndrome: Vertigo, ataxia, facial weakness, hearing loss, and loss of pain/temperature sensation in the contralateral body and ipsilateral face.                                                   | 'AICA = Ataxis, Incoordination, Cerebellar and Auditory deficits'<br>(AICA affects the cerebellum and inner ear.)                                     |
| **Retinal/Ophthalmic Artery**                   | Supplies the retina.                                                      | - Amaurosis fugax (transient monocular blindness), can progress to permanent vision loss if occluded.                                                                                                                | 'Ophthalmic = Only one eye''<br>(Occlusion can lead to loss of vision in just one eye – 'monocular blindness.')                                       |
| **Basilar Artery**                              | Supplies the brainstem and cerebellum.                                    | - Locked-in syndrome (quadriparesis with preserved consciousness, but unable to move or speak except for eye movements)<br>- Cranial nerve deficits (due to brainstem involvement), ataxia, and respiratory failure. | 'Basilar = Brainstem deficits and Locked-in syndrome''<br>(Basilar artery affects the brainstem and may lead to locked-in syndrome.)                  |
