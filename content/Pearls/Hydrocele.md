A hydrocele describes the accumulation of fluid within the tunica vaginalis.


![[z_attachments/Pasted image 20251216180615.png|475]]

- **Communicating**: peritoneal fluid drains down into the scrotum by a patent processus vaginalis. Common in newborns and usually resolve within the first few months of life
- Non communicating: excessive fluid production within the tunica vaginalis

Hydroceles may develop secondary to:  
- epididymo-orchitis
- [[Pearls/Testicular Torsion|Testicular Torsion]]
- [[Full/Testicular Cancer|Testicular Cancer]]

Features: 
- soft, non-tender selling of the hemi-scrotum. Unusually anterior to and below the testicle
- Trans-illuminates with a pen torch
- Testis may be difficult to palpate if the hydrocele is large 

Management:
- Infantile hydroceles are surgically repaired if they don't resolve by the age of 1-2 years due to risk of development of indirect inguinal hernias
- Conservative approach taken in adults depending on severity. **Further investigation to exclude sinister cause**
