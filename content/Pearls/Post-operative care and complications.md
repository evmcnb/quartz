#Acute #Anaesthetics #OSCE 


Anaesthetic drugs stay in the system for up to 24 hours

Patients should respond to voice when discharged to the ward. 

**Airway** 
- Drowsiness may cause reduced ability to protect airway

**Breathing**
- Residual neuromuscular blockade
- Early respiratory complications due to intraoperative ventilation - most common in people with pre-existing respiratory disease or smokers
- High spinal or epidural due to paralysis of accessory muscles
- Opioid toxicity

**Circulation**
- Residual anaesthetic drugs causing ongoing vasodilation -> hypotension
- Dehydration due to a combination of fasting, blood loss and inadequate replacement

**Disability**
- Low GCS due to ongoing anaesthetic drugs
- Hypoglycaemia due to fasting. Note hyperglycaemia is more common due to surgical stress response. 


### Post-operative complications

#### Hypotension 

Relatively common but targets need to be met prior to ward discharge. 
Causes:
- residual anaesthetic drugs
- spinal/spinal anaesthesia or analgesia 
- hypovolaemia 

#### SOB

Usual causes to access in usual way.  Most common:
- Pain
- Residual neurmuscular blockade - rare. *Fish out of water look*
- High spinal/epidural - could complain of arm weakness/numbness or drop in GCS

#### Low GCS

Common to remain sleep for up to 24 hours after general anesthetic. Beyond that isn't normal
Causes:
- Drug overdose - opioids
- High spinal/epidural
- Post operative delirium
- Intraoperative/postoperative intracerebral event 

#### Pain 

Recognise, assess, treat.

See WHO pain ladder

##### Patient controlled analgesia
- Commonly used post-op
- Either morphine or oxycodone (rarely fentanyl)
- Small bolus doses (usually 1mg) with 5 minute lock-out period
- Self administer  
- Needs enhanced observations, background fluid to maintain cannula patency and supplementary O2 prescription
- 24 hours usage indicated to appropriate oral step down

**ALWAYS PRESCRIBE PRN NALOXONE TO ANYONE ON OPIOIDS**


#### Complications of spinal and epidural

- Hypotension
- Urinary retention
- Pruritis
- Respiratory depression 


Local anaesthesic toxicity
- Perioral tingling and metallic taste
- Ringing ears
- Confusion and drowsiness
- Dizziness
- Arrhythmias
