Very common - 25% of children having an episode before 18 months. Viral induced wheeze is one of the most common diagnoses made on paediatric wards.
- Episodic viral wheeze: only wheezes when has a viral URTI & symptom free in between episodes 
- Multiple trigger wheeze: viral UTRIs & other factors such as exercise, allergens and cigarette smoke

Episodic viral wheeze not associated with an increased risk of [[Full/asthma|asthma]], multiple trigger wheeze is. 

#### Management

Parents who are smokers should be encouraged to stop

Episodic wheeze
- First-line: short acting beta 2 agonists - salbutamol or anticholinergic
- Next step: intermittent leukotriene receptor antagonist (montelukast), intermittent inhaled corticosteroids or both
- Little role of oral pred for children who do not require hospital treatment

Multiple trigger wheeze: 
- Trial of either ICS or montelukast for 4-8 weeks
