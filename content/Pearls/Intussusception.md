Intussusception describes the invagination of one portion of the bowel into the lumen of the adjacent bowel, most commonly around the ileo-caecal region.
- usually between 6-18 months
- boy are affected twice as often as girls


> [!tip] Typical exam question
> If “redcurrant jelly stool” and a “sausage-shaped” mass in the abdomen appear in your exams, think of intussusception. The typical child in the exam will have had a viral upper respiratory tract infection preceding the illness and will have features of intestinal obstruction (vomiting, absolute constipation and abdominal distention).


Features:
- intermittent, severe, progressive abdominal pain 
- inconsolable crying
- paroxysm the infant will characteristically **draw their knees up and turn pale**
- vomiting
- **typically after a URTI***
- 'red-current jelly' stool is a late sign
- sausage shaped mass in the RUQ

Ultrasound to investigate - 'target-like mass'/doughnut sign.

**Management:**
- the majority of children can be treated with reduction by air insufflation under radiological control (instead of a barium enema)
- if this fails, or the child has signs of peritonitis, surgery is performed
