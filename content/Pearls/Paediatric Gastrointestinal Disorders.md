| **[[Pearls/Pyloric Stenosis\|Pyloric Stenosis]]**                   | - M>F<br>- 5-10% Family history in parents<br>- Projectile non bile stained vomiting at 4-6 weeks of life<br>- Diagnosis is made by test feed or USS<br>- Treatment: Ramstedt pyloromyotomy (open or laparoscopic)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Acute [[Full/Appendicitis\|Appendicitis]]**                       | - Uncommon under 3 years<br>- When occurs may present atypically                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **[[Pearls/Mesenteric adenitis\|Mesenteric adenitis]]**             | - Central abdominal pain and URTI<br>- Conservative management                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **[[Pearls/Intussusception\|Intussusception]]**                     | - Telescoping bowel<br>- Proximal to or at the level of, ileocaecal valve<br>- 6-9 months of age<br>- Colicky pain, diarrhoea and vomiting, sausage-shaped mass, red jelly stool.<br>- Treatment: reduction with air insufflation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **[[Pearls/Intestinal Malrotation]]**                                      | - High caecum at the midline<br>- Feature in exomphalos, congenital diaphragmatic hernia, intrinsic duodenal atresia<br>- May be complicated by the development of volvulus, an infant with volvulus may have bile stained vomiting<br>- Diagnosis is made by upper GI contrast study and USS<br>- Treatment is by laparotomy, if volvulus is present (or at high risk of occurring then a Ladd's procedure is performed (includes division of Ladd bands and widening of the base of the mesentery) |
| **[[Full/Hirschsprung’s Disease\|Hirschsprung’s Disease]]**         | - Absence of ganglion cells from myenteric and submucosal plexuses<br>- Occurs in 1/5000 births<br>- Full-thickness rectal biopsy for diagnosis<br>- Delayed passage of meconium and abdominal distension<br>- Treatment is with rectal washouts initially, after that an anorectal pull through procedure                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Oesophageal atresia**                                             | - Associated with tracheo-oesophageal fistula and polyhydramnios<br>- May present with choking and cyanotic spells following aspiration<br>- VACTERL associations                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Meconium ileus**                                                  | - Usually delayed passage of meconium and abdominal distension<br>- The majority have cystic fibrosis<br>- X-Rays will not show a fluid level as the meconium is viscid, PR contrast studies may dislodge meconium plugs and be therapeutic<br>- Infants who do not respond to PR contrast and NG N-acetyl cysteine will require surgery to remove the plugs                                                                                                                                         |
| **[[Pearls/Biliary Atresia\|Biliary Atresia]]**                     | - Jaundice > 14 days<br>- Increased conjugated bilirubin<br>- Urgent Kasai procedure                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **[[Pearls/Necrotising enterocolitis\|Necrotising enterocolitis]]** | - Prematurity is the main risk factor<br>- Early features include abdominal distension and passage of bloody stools<br>- X-Rays may show pneumatosis intestinalis and evidence of free air<br>- Increased risk when empirical antibiotics are given to infants beyond 5 days<br>- Treatment is with total gut rest and TPN, babies with perforations will require laparotomy                                                                                                                         |
