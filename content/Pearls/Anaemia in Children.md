##### Anaemia of Infancy

There is a normal dip in haemoglobin at around 6-9 weeks. High Hb at birth $\rightarrow$ high oxygen delivery to cells $\rightarrow$ negative feedback $\rightarrow$ less EPO produced 

##### Anaemia of Prematurity

The more premature the infant the higher chance they will need a transfusion. This is confounded by any further complications e.g. [[Pearls/Neonate sepsis|Neonate sepsis]]

##### Haemolytic disease of the newborn

Incompatibility of rhesus antigens on the surface of RBCs between the mother and baby. If a mother becomes sensitised, in her second pregnancy the anti-D antibodies can cross the placenta and cause haemolysis. See [[Pearls/Rhesus negative pregnancy|Rhesus negative pregnancy]] for more
