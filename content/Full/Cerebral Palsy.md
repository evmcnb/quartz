Name given to permanent neurological problems resulting from damage to the brain around the time of birth. Non-progressive lesion of the motor pathways. More of a syndrome - huge variety of symptoms

## Causes/Factors

**Antenatal**
- [[Pearls/Infections in Pregnancy|Infections in Pregnancy]]
- Trauma during pregnancy

**Perinatal**
- Birth asphyxia
- [[Pearls/Premature Babies|Premature Babies]]

**Post-natal**
- [[Pearls/Meningitis in children|Meningitis]]
- Severe [[Pearls/Neonatal Jaundice|Neonatal Jaundice]]
- Head injury

## Classification

Spastic (70%)
- Increased tone resulting from damage to UPNs
- Subtypes include hemiplegia, diplegia or quadriplegia

**Dyskinetic**
- Caused by damage to the basal ganglia ad substantia nigra
- Athetoid movements and oro-motor problems 

**Ataxic**
- Caused by damage to the cerebellum
- Typical cerebellar signs

**Mixed**

## Presentation

- Failure to meet milestones
- Increased/decreased tone in the limbs
- **Hand preference below 18 months**
- Problems with coordination, speech or walking

## Investigations

Neurological examination: 

| **Finding**    | **Upper Motor Neurone** | **Lower Motor Neurone**                 |
| -------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Inspection** | Muscle bulk preserved   | Reduced muscle bulk with fasciculations |
| **Tone**       | Hypertonia              | Hypotonia                               |
| **Power**      | Slightly reduced        | Dramatically reduced                    |
| **Reflexes**   | Brisk                   | Reduced                                 |

## Management

Treatments for spasticity include oral diazepam, oral/intrathecal baclofen, botulinum toxin type A and other

Anticonvulsants and analgesia as required 

When swallowing difficultly prevents them meeting their nutritional requirements they may require an **_NG tube_** or **_PEG tube_** to be fitted.
