## Insertion

#### Introduction

- Introduce yourself
- Check patient identity, explain procedure and gain valid consent
- Consider cautions for insertion
- Wash hands with soap and water.
#### Equipment

- Clean trolley
- Gather and assemble equipment, checking expiry dates and packaging integrity
- Nasogastric tube and guidewire (fine bore, radio opaque 6-8Fr)
- Tape measure
- pH paper (Medicina)
- 60 ml enteral syringe
- Tape to secure NG tube
- Non-sterile gloves
- Plastic Apron
- Sterile water in gallipot for dipping NGT into.
- Water for swallowing (if patient has a safe swallow) , vomit bowl and tissues
- Documentation

### Procedure 

- Clean hands and put apron on
- Open packs and layout equipment on trolley
- Measure the length of Nasogastric tube (NGT) to be inserted by using a paper tape measure. Measuring from the tip of nose, to the earlobe and down to the Xiphisternum (NEX measurement) The NGT should be passed to **at least** 55cm in adult patients – LTHT guidelines.
- Apply gloves 
- Position patient in High Fowler’s / sat upright, head slightly forward
- Check nasal patency
- Discuss how patients can communicate with you during procedure (i.e raise hand instead of talking)
- Prepare water for drinking (only if patient has safe swallow)
- Ensure vomit bowl and tissues are within easy reach for the patient
- Lubricate the tip of NGT with sterile water in the gallipot (do not use gel as this can affect the pH of an aspirate)
- Insert NGT into the patent nostril and angle it upwards initially, then back towards the nasopharynx. Then ask patient to swallow (only if safe to do so) some water using a straw
- Advance NGT as patient swallows till reached NEX measurement or 55cm at least
- Remove guidewire (NEVER re-insert the guidewire)
**- DO NOT FLUSH WITH WATER BEFORE COMFIRMATION OF POSITION**- Check Position of NGT:

Aspirate using a 60ml enteral syringe and test pH on Medicina pH paper

pH readings should be between **1 and 5** for feeding to commence safely. However, there may be difficulty in differentiating pH readings of pH 5 and 6. It is therefore recommended that a second competent person checks any readings of pH 5, to confirm the result.

- Once position confirmed – secure NGT with tape
- Flush with water (only once position confirmed)
- Measure external length and record in notes
- Ensure patient is comfortable
- Dispose of all equipment in relevant clinical waste/sharps bins
- Remove apron and gloves
- Wash hands
- Complete appropriate documentation
## Confirmation 

#### What if I can’t get aspirate?

You may find it difficult to get an aspirate. If so here are some techniques you should try before requesting an x-ray!

- Lay patient on left hand side.
- Give patient mouth care as this may stimulate the production of gastric secretions.
- Inject 10-20mls of AIR.
- Offer a drink (if patient has safe swallow)
- Advance or withdraw tube by 5cm.

 If you have tried the techniques listed above and have been unable to obtain an aspirate, or the aspirate does not have an acceptable pH, you should order a x-ray to radiologically confirm the position of NGT.


### CXR Criteria

- The chest X-ray **viewing field** should include the **upper oesophagus** and extend to **below the diaphragm**.
- The NG tube should **remain in the midline** down to the level of the **diaphragm**.
- The NG tube should **bisect the carina**.
- The **tip** of the NG tube should be **clearly visible** and **below the left hemidiaphragm**.
- The **tip** of the NG tube should be approximately **10 cm beyond the GOJ** (i.e. within the stomach).
