The emergence of high blood pressure during pregnancy that may be a precursor to a woman developing [[Pearls/Eclampsia]] and other complications


> [!success] Classic triad of symptoms
> - New-onset hypertension
> - Proteinuria
> - Oedema (facial)


Formal definition:
- New-onset blood pressure >140/90 after 20 weeks of pregnancy **AND** 1 or more of: 
	- proteinuria
	- Other end organ dysfunction - renal insufficency (creatinine >90 $\mu$mol/L, liver, neurological, haematological, uteroplacental)


### Blood pressure in pregnancy

> [!info] Blood pressure throughout pregnancy
> - Usually falls in the first trimester (esp the diastolic) and continues until 20-24 weeks (when the placenta is fully developed)
> - after this time the blood pressure usually increases to pre-pregnancy levels by term

Hypertension in pregnancy in usually defined as:  
- systolic > 140 mmHg or diastolic > 90 mmHg
- or an increase above booking readings of > 30 mmHg systolic or > 15 mmHg diastolic
## Causes/Factors

|High risk factors|Moderate risk factors|
|---|---|
|- hypertensive disease in a previous pregnancy<br>- chronic kidney disease<br>- autoimmune disease, such as systemic lupus erythematosus or antiphospholipid syndrome<br>- type 1 or type 2 diabetes<br>- chronic hypertension|- first pregnancy<br>- age 40 years or older<br>- pregnancy interval of more than 10 years<br>- body mass index (BMI) of 35 kg/m² or more at first visit<br>- family history of pre-eclampsia<br>- multiple pregnancy|

75-150mg aspirin is used for prophylaxis against the development of pre-eclampsia. It is given from 12 weeks gestation until birth to women with:
- A single **high-risk** factor
- Two or more **moderate-risk** factors


> [!tldr] Shocking way to remember the moderate risk factors:
> **Fat, Old, Unlucky Virgin**:  
Fat: BMI > 35  
Old: Age > 40 yrs  
Unlucky: FH/over 10 yrs between pregnancies  
Virgin: Nulliparity

## Presentation

Features of severe pre-eclampsia  
- hypertension: typically > 160/110 mmHg and proteinuria as above
- proteinuria: dipstick ++/+++
- headache
- visual disturbance
- papilloedema
- RUQ/epigastric pain
- hyperreflexia & clonus
- platelet count < 100 * 106/l, abnormal liver enzymes or HELLP syndrome

##### HELLP Syndrome

A combination of features that occur as a complication of pre-eclampsia and eclampsia:
- **H**aemolysis
- **E**levated **L**iver enzymes 
- **L**ow **P**latelets

## Investigations

- FBC
- U&E
- LFTs
- Clotting 
- Urine PCR - a urine protein:creatinine ratio of 30 mg/mmol

## Management

All pregnant women are routinely monitored at every antenatal appointment for evidence of pre-eclampsia with:
- BP
- Symptoms
- Urine dip 

**Emergency secondary care assessment** for any women who pre-eclampsia is suspected. Women with a BP of >160/110 are likely to be admitted and observed regardless of proteinuria presence

Further management: 
- Oral labetalol is first line
- Nifedipine if asthmatic 
- Delivery of the baby is the most important definitive management step via any route




#### Gestational hypertension

When hypertension is identified without proteinuria, general management involves: 
- BP target of <135/85 mmHg
- Urine dipstick testing weekly
- Monitoring of blood tests weekly (FBC, liver enzymes and renal profile)
- Monitor of feal growth by serial growth scans
- PlGF testing on one occasion
