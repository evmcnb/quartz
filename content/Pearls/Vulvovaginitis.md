Inflammation and irritation of the vulva and vagina.

Common between 3 and 10 years old. More prone to colonisation and infection with bacteria spread from faeces

Exacerbated by:
- Poor toilet hygiene (wiping back to front)
- Use of chemicals or soaps in cleaning the area
- Tight clothing that treats moisture
- Consipation
- Threadworms
- Pressure on the area 
- Heavily chlorinated pools 

Uncommon after puberty as oestrogen helps keep the skin and vaginal mucosa healthy

Patients may have already been given treatment for a suspected **_urinary tract infection_** and **_thrush_**, usually with little improvement in symptoms. It is unusual for girls to develop thrush **_before_** puberty.

Management focuses on measures to improve symptoms:
- Avoid washing with soap and chemicals
- Avoid perfumed or antiseptic products
- Good toilet hygiene (wiping from front to back)
- Keeping the area dry (pat dry after washing)
- Emollients and barrier creams may help soothe the area
- Loose cotton clothing
- Avoid underwear for sleeping at night
- Treating constipation and worms
- Avoiding activities that exacerbate the problem
