Vulval cancer is rare compared with other gynaecological cancers. Around 90% are **_squamous cell carcinomas_**. Less commonly, they can be **_malignant melanomas_**.

FIGO used for staging
## Causes/Factors

- Advanced age (particularly over 75 years)
- Immunosuppression
- [[Pearls/Human Papilloma virus Infection|Human Papilloma virus Infection]]
- [[Pearls/Lichen sclerosus]]

Around 5% of women with **_lichen sclerosis_** get vulval cancer.

### Vulval Intraepithelial Neoplasia

VIN is a premalignant condition affecting the squamous epithelium of the skin that can precede vulval cancer. Similar to CIN for cervical cancer. 
- **Undifferentiated** vulval intraepithelial neoplasia
    - associated with HPV infection
    - 5% progress to [[Full/Squamous cell carcinoma]]
- **Differentiated VIN**
    - *not associated with HPV*
    - greater risk to [[Full/Squamous cell carcinoma]]

A biopsy is required to diagnose VIN. A specialist will coordinate management. Treatment options include:
- **_Watch and wait_** with close followup
- **_Wide local excision_** (surgery) to remove the lesion
- **_Imiquimod_** cream
- **_Laser ablation_**

## Symptoms

- Lump/ulcer on the labia majora
- Inguinal lymphadenopathy
- Bleeding
- Pain
- Itching

## Investigations

Establishing the diagnosis and staging involves:
- **_Biopsy_** of the lesion
- **_Sentinel node biopsy_** to demonstrate lymph node spread
- Further imaging for staging (e.g. CT abdomen and pelvis)

## Management

Management depends on the stage, and may involve:
- Wide local excision to remove the cancer
- Groin lymph node dissection
- Chemotherapy
- Radiotherapy
