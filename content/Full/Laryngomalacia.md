Increased flaccidity of the supraglottic structures - soft and floppy so collapses during breathing

Most common congenital laryngeal abnormality 

- **Type 1**: tightening of the aryepiglottic folds
- **Type 2:** redundant tissue in the supraglottic region
- **Type 3:** associated with other disorders - neuromuscular weakness, GORD 
## Causes/Factors

- Affects both sexes equally
- 60-70% of cases of congenital stridor
- Presents within the first few weeks of life (4-6 weeks)

## Presentation

- Inspiratory stridor. Usually intermittent occuring in the supine position
- Symptoms increase in severity in the first 8 months but usually resolve by 18-24 months
- Rare: respiratory distress, failure to thrive, and cyanosis

## Investigations

SpO2 - monitored and gases taken if desaturation

Laryngoscopy and bronchoscopy are only indicated if there are severe features, or if there is diagnostic difficulty

## Management

- 99% of cases usually resolve spontaneously by 18-24 months
- Symptomatic relief may be provided by hyperextending the neck during episodes of stridor
- Surgical intervention is only required with severe respiratory distress e.g. tracheostomy, laryngoplasty
