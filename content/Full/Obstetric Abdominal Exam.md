## Introduction

- **Wash hands** (and don PPE if needed)
- **Introduce yourself** (name and role)
- Confirm patient's **name** and **DOB**
- Explain what the examination involves - *"Today I need to examine your tummy as part of the assessment of your pregnancy.*
- **Offer a chaperone**
- Gain consent to continue
- Ask if patient in any **pain** before continuing
- Offer for them to empty their bladder first
- **Position the patient on the clinical examination couch with the bed at 30-45 degree angle**

#### Gather equipment

- Measuring tape
- Stethoscope

## General Inspection

#### Clinical Signs

- **Pain** - if they look uncomfortable ask where
- Obvious scars - previous [[Pearls/Caesarean Section|Caesarean Section]]
- Pallor - anaemia
- Jaundice - [[Pearls/Obstetric Cholestasis|Obstetric Cholestasis]]
- Oedema - a small amount is normal later in the pregnancy, but wide spread can indicate [[Full/Pre-eclampsia|Pre-eclampsia]]

#### Objects and Equipment

- Mobility aids
- Vital signs - charts 
- Fluid balance charts
- Prescriptions

## Hands

#### Inspection

- Colour - suggesting poor perfusion
- Peripheral oedema
- Palmar erythema

##### Temperature

- Hands should be symmetrically warm

##### Capillary refill time

More assessment of peripheral perfusion. CRT >2 seconds indicates poor peripheral perfusion


##### Radial pulse

Palpate the patient's radial pulse and assess rate and rhythm

## Face

- Jaundice - check sclera
- Melasma - benign dark and irregular hyperpigmented macules which are normal in pregnancy
- Oedema - check for [[Full/Pre-eclampsia|Pre-eclampsia]]
- Conjunctival pallor - anaemia 


## Abdomen


### Position the patient

- **Early pregnancy:** position the patient supine on the couch, with the head end of the bed elevated to 15-30°.
- **Late pregnancy:** position the patient in the left lateral position (tilted 15° to the horizontal level) to avoid compression of the abdominal aorta and inferior vena cava by the gravid uterus (known as aortocaval compression).

#### Inspection

Expose the abdomen appropriately from the xiphisternum to the pubic symphysis and inspect for relevant clinical signs: 
- Abdominal shape - give indication of fetal lie
- Fetal movements - from 24 weeks
- Surgical scars
- Linea nigra - a dark line running vertically down the middle of the abdomen (normal in pregnancy)
- Striae gravidarum - stretch marks
- Striae albicans - mature stretch marks which appear silver and less pronounced


#### Palpation

##### Abdomen

Briefly palpate over each of the 9 regions of the abdomen to identify tenderness/masses that may not relate to pregnancy

##### Uterus

Identify the borders. The uterine fundus can be found at different locations during pregnancy depending on the patient's current gestation
- 12 weeks - pubic symphysis
- 20 weeks - umbilicus
- 36 weeks - xiphoid process of sternum 

###### Fetal lie 

Refers to the relationship between the long axis of the fetus with respect to that of the mother

1. Place your hands on either side of the patient's uterus 
2. Gently palpate each side of the uterus
3. One side should feel full (presence of fetal back), the other side may be able to feel fetus's limbs

- **Longitudinal lie:** the head and buttocks are palpable at each end of the uterus.
- **Oblique lie:** the head and buttocks are palpable in one of the iliac fossae.
- **Transverse lie:** the fetus is lying directly across the uterus.

###### Fetal presentation

Which anatomical part of the fetus is closest to the pelvic inlet
1. Ensure you are facing the patient to observe for signs of discomfort and warn the patient it may feel uncomfortable 
2. Place hands either side of the lower pole of the uterus, just above the pubic symphysis 
3. Apply firm pressure to the uterus angled medially, palpating for the presenting part 

- A hard round presenting part is suggestive of a cephalic presentation (normal).
- A broader, softer, less defined presenting part (i.e. the fetal bottom or legs) is suggestive of a breech presentation (abnormal).

**Fetal engagement**

In late pregnancy the fetus is considered engaged when more than 50% of the presenting part has descended into the pelvis

The fetal head is divided into 5ths when assessing engagement

##### Symphyseal-fundal height

The distance between the fundus and the upper boarder of the pubic symphysis. After 20 weeks, the height should correlate with the gestational age of the fetus in weeks.
1. Begin palpation of the abdomen just inferior to the xiphisternum using the ulnar border of your left hand
2. Locate the fundus of the uterus (form feeling edge at the upper boarder of the bump)
3. Once the fundus has been identified locate the upper boarder of the pubic symphysis 
4. Measure the distance between the upper uterine border and pubic symphysis in cms

To avoid bias, best to turn the tape measure face down and only view the numbers once everything is in position

### Fetal Heartbeat

Usually done with a Doppler probe. Important to have a basic understanding of how to locate and identify. 
1. Based on the assessment of the fetal position should place the probe aiming between the fetal shoulders on the fetal back.
2. Palpate the maternal pulse
3. Listen for the fetal heart beat - it should **not** coincide with the maternal pulse 

## To Complete the Exam

- **Explain** to the patient that the examination is now **finished**.
- **Thank the patient** for their time.
- **Dispose of PPE** appropriately and **wash your hands**.
- **Summarise** your findings.

## Further Assessments and Investigations

- Blood pressure measurement
- Urinalysis
- Speculum exam
- Weight and height measurement
- Ultrasound scan
