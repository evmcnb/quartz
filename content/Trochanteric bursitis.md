Greater trochanteric pain syndrome is also referred to as trochanteric bursitis.  
  
It is due to repeated movement of the fibroelastic iliotibial band and is most common in women aged 50-70 years.


![[z_attachments/Pasted image 20261012184343.png|465]]

- Tenderness over the greater trochanter
- Trendelenburg - positive when the other side of the pelvis drops down, suggesting weakness in the affected hip.

It can take 6-9 months to recover fully, sometimes longer.

- Rest
- Ice
- Analgesia (e.g., ibuprofen or naproxen)
- Physiotherapy
- Steroid injections
